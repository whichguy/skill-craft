# Prompting and Evaluating LLM-Generated Software Architecture Decisions

## 1. Prompting for architecture and stack evaluation

- **Elicit quality attributes as concrete scenarios before comparing options.** ATAM extracts business drivers → quality-attribute requirements → scenarios, then evaluates architectural approaches against them — this scenario-first order is what should be baked into the prompt sequence. [SEI ATAM (Kazman, Klein, Barbacci)](https://www.sei.cmu.edu/documents/1186/1998_005_001_16646.pdf), [ATAM overview](https://www.sei.cmu.edu/library/file_redirect/2011_015_001_28266.pdf/)
- **Force explicit trade-offs, not just pros/cons.** ATAM's outputs are named trade-off points, sensitivity points, and risks tied to specific quality attributes — ask the model to label which attributes conflict and why, not just list generic pros/cons. [ATAM (GeeksforGeeks summary)](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/software-engineering/architecture-tradeoff-analysis-method-atam/)
- **Require an ADR-shaped output**: Title, Status, Context, Decision, Consequences — this Nygard structure forces the model to state constraints and accepted downsides, not just the choice. [ADR GitHub org](https://adr.github.io/), [Nygard template](https://github.com/joelparkerhenderson/architecture-decision-record/blob/main/locales/en/templates/decision-record-template-by-michael-nygard/index.md)
- **Use a checklist-of-pillars as the requirement lens, not the grading rubric itself** — AWS (6 pillars), Google Cloud (System Design + 5 pillars), and Azure (5 pillars) all converge on operational excellence/reliability/security/cost/performance(+sustainability), which is a reasonable canonical checklist to require the model to address per-decision. [AWS pillars](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/apn/the-6-pillars-of-the-aws-well-architected-framework/), [Google Cloud framework](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework), [Azure pillars](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/well-architected/pillars)
- **Ask for evidence on platform-capability claims** — no vendor guide states this as a named technique; it is an extrapolation from general grounding/citation practice, not a documented pattern. **Unverified**, but low-risk to require ("cite the doc/benchmark you're relying on for this claim").
- **Guard against over-engineering explicitly** — prompt for YAGNI/proportionality checks ("what is the simplest architecture that meets the stated scenarios? what would you cut if load were 10x lower?"); complexity that isn't backed by measured constraints is a known failure mode. [YAGNI and architecture](https://blog.bencope.land/yagni-and-building-good-practices-into-your-architecture/), [Cost of over-engineering](https://www.appstudio.ca/blog/cost-of-over-engineering-software/)
- **Prefer structured output for grading/comparison, XML/tags for internal prompt organization.** Claude is trained to expect `<context>`, `<criteria>`, `<output_format>`-style tags; OpenAI recommends JSON-schema-constrained structured outputs for anything downstream code will parse. [Claude XML tags](https://docs.anthropic.com/en/docs/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/use-xml-tags), [OpenAI structured outputs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/structured-outputs)
- **Position and length affect what gets used.** Models are weakest at extracting information from the middle of long prompts ("lost in the middle"); Anthropic's own long-context guidance recommends placing key documents/requirements near the top and repeating the critical instruction near the end. [Liu et al., "Lost in the Middle"](https://cs.stanford.edu/~nfliu/papers/lost-in-the-middle.arxiv2023.pdf), [Anthropic long-context prompting](https://www.anthropic.com/news/prompting-long-context)
- **Use chain-of-thought / a visible thinking pass before the final answer**, especially a "guided" or "structured" CoT with explicit steps (list requirements → list candidate stacks → score trade-offs → decide), rather than bare "think step by step." [Anthropic chain-of-thought guide](https://docs.anthropic.com/en/docs/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/chain-of-thought)
- **Add a self-critique/review pass.** Self-Refine and Reflexion both show a model can meaningfully improve its own output via a second pass that critiques against stated criteria, without external training. [Self-Refine](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.17651), [Reflexion](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.11366)

## 2. LLM-as-judge reliability

- **Pointwise (rubric/Likert) vs. pairwise comparison is a real trade-off, not a solved question.** Pairwise verdicts often track human preference better for subjective qualities, but flip ~35% of the time under superficial rewrites vs. ~9% for absolute scores; pointwise is more stable but suffers scale drift and central tendency. [Pairwise or Pointwise? (arXiv 2504.14716)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.14716), [pointwise vs pairwise practitioner summary](https://oneuptime.com/blog/post/2026-08-31-pointwise-vs-pairwise-llm-evaluation/view)
- **Position bias is real and judge/task-dependent** — a 15-judge, 150k-instance study found significant variation by judge and task, meaning a fixed mitigation (e.g., single reordering) can't be assumed to generalize; swap and average is the standard mitigation. [Judging the Judges: Position Bias (arXiv 2406.07791)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07791)
- **Verbosity/length bias is documented but not uniform across judge model families** — test your specific judge rather than assuming direction or magnitude. [Zheng et al., MT-Bench (arXiv 2306.05685)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.05685), [Bias mitigation strategies (arXiv 2604.23178)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.23178)
- **Requiring rationale/evidence before a grade (G-Eval's CoT + form-filling paradigm) raises correlation with human judgment**, but the benefit shrinks when the rubric already has well-defined per-level anchors — so anchors and CoT are partial substitutes, not strictly additive. [G-Eval (arXiv 2303.16634)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16634)
- **Evidence should be locked before scoring to reduce post-hoc rationalization** — recent "Proof-Before-Preference" style designs (cite spans → lock notes → score only from locked evidence) reduce a judge's ability to invent justification for a pre-formed verdict. [Bias in the Loop (arXiv 2604.16790)](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.16790v1), [Rationalization bias in LLM judges (arXiv 2605.23970)](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.23970)
- **Analytic rubrics with per-criterion anchors + examples outperform holistic scoring** for consistency; this is the same logic underlying G-Eval's form-filling design. [G-Eval (arXiv 2303.16634)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16634)
- **Multiple judges (a panel, "PoLL") reduces cost and bias vs. one large judge** — a panel of smaller, diverse models can outperform a single strong judge and is ~7x cheaper, but correlated errors across similar judges can still collapse a panel's effective vote count, so diversity of model family matters more than headcount. [Replacing Judges with Juries / PoLL (arXiv 2404.18796)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18796), [Nine Judges, Two Effective Votes (arXiv 2605.29800)](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29800), [RoPoLL (arXiv 2606.30931)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30931)
- **Calibrate against human labels with Cohen's κ (two raters) or Krippendorff's α (multi-rater)**, and read κ alongside the confusion matrix and class prevalence — raw agreement is misleading when one label dominates. [Calibrating LLM judge with κ](https://oneuptime.com/blog/post/2026-08-31-calibrate-llm-judge-cohens-kappa/view), [Galileo calibration guide](https://galileo.ai/blog/calibrate-llm-judge-human-annotations)
- **Known failure modes**: self-preference (judges favor outputs resembling their own family's style), leniency/central-tendency in pointwise scales, verbosity bias, and low inter-rater reliability baselines (~0.45–0.60 on absolute 1–5 scales even among humans) that a judge inherits and compounds. [Self-Preference Bias (arXiv 2410.21819)](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.21819v1), [LLMs-as-Judges survey (arXiv 2412.05579)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.05579)

## 3. Experimental design for prompt A/B tests with an LLM judge

- **Compute minimum detectable effect and required N before running the test** — most eval suites run on 50–200 examples and claim significance they don't have; semantically similar items don't count as independent samples. **Practitioner source, not peer-reviewed — treat magnitude claims as unverified.** [Statistical playbook for LLM prompt A/B tests](https://futureagi.com/blog/ab-testing-llm-prompts-best-practices-2026/), [Statistical power problem](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026/04/15/statistical-power-llm-evals)
- **Use paired designs** (same input, both prompt variants, same judge call structure) and paired bootstrap resampling for significance, the long-standing NLP practice for comparing systems on the same test set. [Berg-Kirkpatrick et al., paired bootstrap](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/An-Empirical-Investigation-of-Statistical-in-NLP-Berg-Kirkpatrick-Burkett/645c9d018d6379a4c2eed5ab2f62aeb5629544ce), [Resolution diagnostics for paired LLM evaluation (arXiv 2605.30315)](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30315)
- **Hold judge, temperature, position/order, and scoring format constant** across the two arms of the comparison; vary only the prompt under test — otherwise position/verbosity bias confounds the treatment effect (§2).
- **Run a confirmation round**: re-run the winning prompt against the same holdout with a fresh (or swapped-order) judge pass before accepting a result, since a judge with κ ≈ 0.5 against humans can flip a test on noise alone. [Judge reliability and MDE](https://futureagi.com/blog/ab-testing-llm-prompts-best-practices-2026/)
- **Report confidence intervals, not point estimates**, and prefer bootstrap CIs over bare significance/SOTA claims — established recommendation in NLP evaluation methodology generally. [Please Don't Forget the CI (arXiv 2205.11134)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11134)

## Recommendations for our harness

1. Switch the 30-criterion grading from a single Sonnet pass to **analytic, per-criterion anchors**: for each of the 5 grades (met/partial/missed/overbuilt/n-a) write one concrete example, so the judge isn't inventing a threshold per call — directly follows G-Eval's form-filling result.
2. **Require a quoted evidence span from the plan before each per-criterion verdict**, then lock that span before assigning met/partial/missed — reduces post-hoc rationalization documented in the "Proof-Before-Preference"/rationalization-bias line of work.
3. Add **"overbuilt" evidence discipline symmetrically**: require the judge to cite the specific unrequested complexity, not just assert disproportion — this is your defense against a lenient judge waving through gold-plating, matching the over-engineering literature's "add complexity only with evidence" framing.
4. Since you grade one plan at a time (pointwise), you inherit pointwise's known weaknesses (central tendency, scale drift). Periodically **spot-check with a pairwise confirmation** (best plan vs. runner-up on the same criteria) since pairwise tracks human preference better for subjective/quality criteria — don't switch entirely, since pairwise doesn't give you an absolute floor.
5. **Calibrate the Sonnet judge against a small human-labeled gold set** (even 30–50 plan/criterion pairs) and report Cohen's κ per criterion category (not just overall); criteria with low κ need better anchors before you trust A/B results built on them.
5b. Consider a **second, differently-sized/family judge** for at least the criteria with lowest κ — cheap insurance against Sonnet's self-preference/family-specific bias, per the PoLL literature; a full panel isn't necessary if your criteria are objective/checklist-like.
6. For **prompt A/B tests on the architecture-generation side**, keep the judge, temperature, and grading prompt byte-identical between arms; run both variants against the same fixed plan set (paired), compute required N via an MDE calculation before trusting any "N wins" claim, and require a same-day confirmation re-run before shipping a prompt change.
7. Given prompts run at 20K+ token plan lengths, put the **30-criterion rubric and the plan's most decision-relevant sections near the start and end of the judge prompt**, not buried mid-context — direct mitigation for "lost in the middle."

## Recommendations for architecture prompts

1. Restructure the generation prompt to a fixed 4-stage order, each in its own tag: `<requirements>` (elicited quality-attribute scenarios, ATAM-style) → `<alternatives>` (≥2 stacks compared per relevant pillar) → `<tradeoffs>` (named conflicts between attributes) → `<decision_record>` (Nygard ADR: Context/Decision/Consequences/Status).
2. Explicitly require a **proportionality check** as its own section: "state the simplest architecture meeting the stated scenarios, and justify any complexity beyond it with a specific measured constraint" — your harness's "overbuilt" grade needs this section to point at.
3. Require a **visible thinking/CoT pass** ("guided" style with named steps) before the decision record, and a **second self-critique pass** ("re-read your decision record against the requirements section — what's missing or unjustified?") before final output — cheap, and both Self-Refine and Anthropic's own CoT guidance support a distinct second pass over "think harder" in one shot.
4. For platform-capability claims (e.g., "X scales to Y"), require an inline citation of the source document/benchmark relied on — **flagged unverified as a named technique**, but consistent with general grounding practice and directly checkable by your judge.
5. Use the AWS/GCP/Azure pillar set (reliability, security, performance, cost, ops[, sustainability]) as the *requirement checklist* the generation prompt must address, reserving your 30-criterion rubric for judging — don't conflate the two; the pillars are inputs, the rubric is the grading instrument.
