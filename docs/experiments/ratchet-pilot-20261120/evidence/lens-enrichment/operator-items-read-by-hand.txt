The items that fail on the correct reference, read for the operator's work
(2026-11-21; one reader, the session that ran the cells; not blind to which text wrote which
specification; exploratory. The script-run counts are in failing-by-source-log.txt; every item's
statement and Source line are in failing-by-source.json, so each label below can be read again.)

Why it was read: the session "Spec elaboration" found that the gain of its lens arms on operator items
was an operator-controls requirement nobody asked for, in specifications written without a repository,
and said that in this pilot such requirements would land among the clauses that fail on a correct
product. Both texts run here tell the writer, unconditionally, to cover the operator's work.

Rule used.
  operator's work: the item requires something of or for a person who runs the product, not one who
    calls its API: configuring it, starting or stopping it, preparing or migrating its data file,
    switching a feature on or off, rolling back or coming back, reading it through the command-line
    tool or the report, or a document or runbook that tells that person how.
  beyond the request: operator's work that requires a control, procedure or document the request does
    not name (the requests are in cases.json). A chosen form of work the request does name (the name
    and range of a setting, whether a start refuses or prepares, which failure stops a start) is not
    counted as beyond.
  new control: a command, endpoint or switch that neither the request nor the repository has.

What the reading says.
  - No failing item in either text requires a new control. Where a setting or a switch appears, the
    request names it (hours that change without a code change; DUE_TIMES), and the specifications say
    so in their own reasons ("require no live configuration endpoint", "without a new command").
  - About half of what fails on a correct product is the operator's work (27 of 58, 35 of 75), nearly
    all of it on due times, whose request is itself about operating the product (configure, backfill,
    switch on, roll back). What the specification adds there is the form: the name and range of a
    setting, refuse or prepare at start, which failure stops a start.
  - What goes beyond the request is documents: 10 of the 11 items (3 with the lens text, 8 with the
    pilot's) require a runbook, instructions or a reference page to say something. A correct product
    without that page fails them.
