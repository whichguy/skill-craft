2. Choices that are a bound (a size, a range or a ceiling). The three requests state their own limits
   (64 characters; 4, 24, 72 and 168 hours; the meter's 10,000 ticks), so there are few.

   the writer chose no further limit, or the unit the product already uses: 5, all pass on the reference
     lens orders C-28 (an id has no sign or whole-number restriction), C-36 (no ceiling under the largest
     safe integer); lens ticket assignment C-25 (the 64 counts UTF-16 code units, as requester names do);
     the pilot's orders C-38 (no safe-integer ceiling); the pilot's ticket assignment C-26 (same units).
   the writer chose a range for the hours setting: 3, all fail on the reference
     lens due times C-8 (whole hours from 1 through 4800000000); the pilot's due times C-9 (a positive
     whole number), C-10 (unusable when it overflows the timestamp range).
   Those three fail through the setting's name, not its range. Both specifications also chose the name:
   one variable a priority, DUE_HOURS_URGENT, DUE_HOURS_HIGH, DUE_HOURS_NORMAL and DUE_HOURS_LOW (lens C-6;
   the pilot's C-7), and both sets of acceptance tests set those variables. The correct reference reads
   one setting, RESPONSE_HOURS, as priority=hours pairs (its src/config.js). So every test that changes the
   hours fails on it, the test of the request's own clause included (lens C-5, the pilot's C-6: "the help
   desk must be able to change those hours without a code change").

3. What the reading supports, and what it cannot.
   - A requirement the writer adds that makes the product refuse something is where two correct products
     part ways most often: two times in three, against about one in four for its other choices.
   - A setting the request requires must have a name, and two correct products gave it different ones.
     These records cannot say that a chosen bound is wrong: the only chosen bounds sit behind a chosen name.
   - Whether an added refusal is wanted is not something "fails on the reference" can tell. The held-out
     checks, written from the request alone, failed in this pilot for one added refusal (refuse to start
     on a file without due times) and for no chosen name.
