## 3. Give the spec one rule, with a smaller invitation to invent

Keep the five fields for the pilot. `requirement` anchors the clause. `question` names the decision and its trigger. `default` tells the reader what happens. `alternative` gives one feasible answer and what it changes for users or operators. `why` makes the basis inspectable, including an honest “the request leaves this open” where there is no preference evidence. The requester also needs the **spec revision and a working reply route**. Those belong to the display. Allow multiple distinct choices under one R-ID. Display numbers are for reading order.

Three runs can show whether the model understands the row, the gate is usable, and the intended surface appears. They can all pass `migrate` without correcting anything. Using the archive’s 7/11 pass fraction as a rough independent-draw illustration, three passes already have about a **26%** chance. The stand-in reply is fair as a fixed owner preference. The keyword trigger is too loose for the primary score. “Can 1.4.0 launch on a backfilled file?” can trigger on a rollback decision. “Initialize legacy records before opening the listener” can miss the target. Preregister a semantic rubric about **who prepares old records and what enabled startup does when they lack deadlines**. Have a reader score the pre-reply spec and visible row without seeing the build result. Keep the lexical rule as a diagnostic. Trigger from the requester-facing notice or report you intend to test.
