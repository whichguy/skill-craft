The concrete changes I would prioritize are:

1. **Verified bug — restore termination checks after immediately acquiring a free lock.**  
   In `Exclusive`, successful acquisition returns before `kill_step`. Consequently `cmd_cleanup` and `step_cancel` substitute `group:"gone"` for a dead worker whose group may remain alive. My witness used a real sleeping orphan in the recorded group: cleanup returned `verified:true`, called `kill_step` zero times, and left the member alive.

   Also preserve and evaluate `held.killed` regardless of the subsequently loaded state. A second witness returned `group:"survived", survivors:[900002]` while transitioning to `prepared`; cleanup discarded that result and returned verified.

2. **Verified bug — synchronize every prepared-state publication and record rewrite.**  
   `advance` checks `worker_alive`, releases that probe lock, then calls `publish` without ownership. My barrier witness let cleanup acquire `Exclusive` between those actions: polling returned `done` and saved `published`; cleanup subsequently saved its stale `prepared` record.

   Route publication and publication-failure settlement through the same ownership protocol and reload after acquisition. `stop_live` also has a state-check-to-signal window: a snapshot of `running` can trigger `kill_step` after the durable state becomes `prepared`.

3. **Verified bug — make start’s tip snapshot consistent with reconciliation.**  
   `step_start_admitted` reads `tip` before checking `unsettled_keys`. A poll can publish between those operations despite start holding admission. My witness accepted a new step with `tip_from=T` while the published tip was already `T′`; the build tokens were equal because the intervening commit changed no candidate files.

   Check unsettled ownership before taking the authoritative tip snapshot, with synchronization covering publication.

   Separately, `cmd_cleanup` leaves dead `prepared` records unsettled while reporting verified. Repeated cleanup never releases admission; an explicit poll does. Resolve that mismatch or report the outstanding reconciliation clearly.

4. **Verified bug — recheck boot grace inside `Exclusive`.**  
   Three polls can all pass the old grace check, then acquire the lock sequentially. My witness produced two respawns and terminal failure **0.16 seconds** after the latest spawn, despite a ten-second grace. Keep the three-attempt cap, but evaluate the current `spawned_epoch` under ownership.

5. **Verified bug — honor authoritative epoch receipts and decisions.**  
   `epoch_evidence` accepts a rewritten receipt whose `receipt_sha256` no longer matches. [epoch.py — `state`](/private/tmp/g1b-sol.J8zz0X/checkout/test/shiploop_e2e/epoch.py) correctly demotes that attempt to `recorded`; the adapter still reports it complete. It also reads the first decision while the harness uses the last. Verify seals and review bindings, and select the latest decision. These are residual contract gaps, not all newly introduced.

6. **Verified bug — apply ownership refusal before collecting/signalling descendant sessions.**  
   `kill_step` uses `worker_group` to discover extra sessions before `stop_group` declares ownership unverified. It then passes those sessions to `stop_processes` anyway. My stale-group witness identified an unrelated descendant session for termination.

   `age_seconds` also depends on local wall time: changing UTC to Los Angeles converted a genuinely sixteen-hour-old record into eight hours; the DST fold is ambiguous, and parse failure returns zero. `PS_ENV` correctly fixes textual locale; locale was not the demonstrated CLI failure. Prefer durable ownership evidence and an epoch timestamp over this age heuristic.

7. **Verified bug — validate calibration recommendations before using their sample requirement.**  
   Require a positive, non-boolean integer attempt count and validate recommendation/rate consistency. The zero-attempt witness above bypasses the new substance gate. A report produced through the real `spread.calibrate` calculations, with sealed-card inputs stubbed, passed correctly.

I found **no lock-order cycle**: workers do not acquire admission, and `advance` uses nonwaiting `Exclusive` acquisition. Dead records cause explicit refusals rather than a two-lock deadlock. Admission itself has no timeout.

Keep calibration unmeasured pending its fix. Automated kill/recovery trials additionally need the acknowledged durable ownership of reviewer, judge, and improver sessions.
