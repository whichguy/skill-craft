#### Deviations

1. Adjacency became order. The old pins said the owner rule is the line under the header and the pointer the line under that; the helper says the parts come in `TOP_ORDER` within 14 lines of the header (plan 6.1's own bound). A part cannot sink or swap, but two lines can now sit between header and owner. The review proposed exactly this ("asserts their order, not their index"); the 14 is plan 6.1's number, not a new one.
2. `UNIT` in `test/shiploop-efficiency-journal.test.py` accepts `UP` so this row (unit `UP`) is well formed; the economy unit for rows 1, 3 and 4 needs its own id (the same one-line edit; on INT it is `UE`).

#### Key learning

- The cost the review measured (24.6 and 42.1 minutes of red-to-commit, 64% pure re-pointing) sat in a handful of address expressions. Finding a part by what it is (a constant, a label) and asserting the order removes the cause for the top block and, through `LoopBlock`, for the loop block. The experiment is the evidence (`UP-evidence/layout-insertion.txt`): a line added in two places breaks assertions in six suites on the base and none here.
- "No such route test today" was a finding made without running the code: four tests already submitted the remedy in the could-not-run state. The gap was real but narrower: the outcome came from the test, not from the refusal. A route test takes the exit from the text the model reads, and an empty extraction fails.
- A derived check over the stage table found nothing wrong today (0 of 62), which is the result worth having: the A1 class has no member in the current packets.
