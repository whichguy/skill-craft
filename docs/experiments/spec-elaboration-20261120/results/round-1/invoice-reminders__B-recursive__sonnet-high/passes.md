# Passes

No code, runtime or existing product was in the working directory (an empty repository), so the request is specified as a feature of an invoicing product that already has accounts, customers, invoices with a number, a due date, currency, outstanding balance and status (draft, issued, void), recorded payments, and users with roles. Each such assumption is stated as an implied clause below.

## Pass 1 — the ground
Added U-1 to U-14 and clauses C-1 to C-43: turning on/off, the default and edited schedule and its validation, when a reminder is due and the hours it can go, which invoices are eligible, what the email says, who receives it, history and upcoming views, pause/resume, customer opt-out, sample email, permissions, the problems view. Unhappy paths for each flow were included where they were obvious (invalid schedule, no address, paused). Deliberately left for later passes: failures of sending, outages and catch-up, edits to invoices in flight, concurrency, runtime and production demands.

## Pass 2 — what each sentence takes for granted
Took the one sentence of the request and each pass-1 clause and asked what must already be true. Added C-44 to C-56: invoices without a due date (and revised C-39 to "on or before the issue date" because a reminder on the day the invoice is sent is noise), what happens to invoices that already exist when reminders are first turned on or turned on again (C-45), that "unpaid" means unpaid in the system's records (C-46), more than one invoice per customer, tenant isolation, a time-zone default, a reply address that always exists, the stop link needing no login and not expiring, malformed addresses, finiteness of the schedule, the empty case, and closed or suspended accounts. Found nothing further on the request sentence itself beyond these.

## Pass 3 — where the parts meet
Walked the pairs: send vs payment/void/pause/opt-out/off arriving during the run; a late or missed run vs the schedule; two runs at once; a crash mid-send; due date, balance, address, schedule and time zone edited while reminders are pending; a payment reversed; the stop link opened twice or in another letter case; delete vs pending; the daily summary vs retries. Added C-57 to C-79 (lateness window, supersede rule, recheck before send, at-most-once, outcome unknown, retry and permanent failure, async bounce and its end, edit interactions, summary email). Revised C-32 and C-33 so pause is decided at the moment the system comes to send, which is what makes "skipped for good" observable and consistent with C-57.

## Pass 4 — the runtime's own rules
The request names no runtime, so the platform's rules are stated as behaviours that must hold whatever it is. Added C-80 to C-92: an execution cut short by a platform time limit (limit unconfirmed), the email provider's send-rate cap and throttling (figure unconfirmed), missing provider configuration, sender authentication and one-click unsubscribe as mailbox providers demand of bulk senders (thresholds unconfirmed), a postal-address footer (legal need unconfirmed), non-production environments emailing only a test inbox, the operator's stop switch and per-day counts, logs without customer data, retention, and turning on for a very large account without waiting synchronously.

## Pass 5 — what must not change, what must never happen
Added C-93 to C-102. Must not change: invoice data, existing invoice emails, invoice operations during a provider outage, existing data after upgrade, and rollback/roll-forward. Must never happen: a due reminder vanishing without an outcome, another recipient's address shown, rules enforced on first attempts but not retries, "sent" shown for an unaccepted message, guessable links.

## Pass 6 — grind (first read as the builder)
Re-read the whole specification from the top. Found and fixed: C-29 omitted the "bounced" outcome that C-64 and C-98 use; C-13 promised sending by 18:00 although C-81 caps the send rate; the sample (C-40) conflicted with the daytime window (C-12) and was really three behaviours (split out C-105); the problems view (C-43) and the summary (C-77) used different lists of problems and the view had no end (defined one list and when an entry leaves); the timing wording of a late reminder (C-22) was ambiguous; "billing address" (C-27) was undefined; the examples of C-1, C-45 and C-56 were wrong or ambiguous at the day of the change; C-4, C-19, C-57, C-77, C-78, C-81, C-83, C-85 and C-97 each held two behaviours and were narrowed, with the dropped halves added as C-103, C-104, C-105; three numbers that were silently presented as implied (C-88, C-92) are now marked as choices; upcoming-list behaviour while Off or paused (C-106), the account time zone being shown and changeable (C-107) and concurrent schedule saves (C-108) were missing and added. C-78's 08:00–18:00 window on the summary was dropped as not serving anyone.

## Pass 7 — grind (second full read, clause by clause as the person blamed for it)
Checked each Observable can be seen by someone outside the code, each example's dates by arithmetic (C-1, C-6, C-11, C-44, C-45, C-58, C-67, C-70, C-72, C-75), the counts (108 clauses matching 108 Observables, 14 use cases, every Source one of request/implied/choice) and the use cases for an unhappy path (all have one: refusal, missing address, provider failure, bounce, outage, wrong link). Found one defect: C-13 promised the customer "receives" the mail by a time, which no one can observe or control; changed to the hand-over to the provider.

## Pass 8 — grind (looking for what a clause cannot see: categories nobody asked about)
Asked what user-supplied text reaches the outgoing message, and what the links themselves must be. Added C-109 (user-entered names cannot alter recipients or layout) and C-110 (https links). Considered and added nothing for: per-customer language, plain-text alternative part, accessibility of the stop page, a cap on emails per customer per day, manual "send now", editable email wording, reminders to the business itself, and re-sending after a missed reminder. These are left out on purpose: the request asks for automatic reminders, none of them is needed for them to be correct, and each would be a fixed choice nothing in the request supports.
