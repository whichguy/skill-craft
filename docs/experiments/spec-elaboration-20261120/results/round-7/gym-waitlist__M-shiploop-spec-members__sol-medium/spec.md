# Class Booking App

## Source and scope

The user’s **Incoming request**, 2026-11-21, is “Add a waitlist to our class booking app so full classes don't turn people away.” The request establishes the outcome. The queue order, automatic booking with consent, and in-app confirmation below are product defaults for this standalone specification.

The request refers to an existing app and full class sessions. No prior specification, selected baseline, discovery, research, repository, or reference product was supplied. Existing screens, roles, booking rules, capacity writers, and runtime behavior are unknown. The preservation clauses below constrain the change. They carry no claim about how the app currently works. Preserve ordinary booking and cancellation and existing eligibility, price, capacity, and class lifecycle rules wherever the waitlist does not expressly change the outcome. Scope is one scheduled occurrence at a time. New membership roles, staff queue administration, priority tiers, external notifications, and new payment or penalty policies are outside this request.

**Terms.** A *session* is a scheduled class occurrence, identified in the existing booking scope. A *member* is an authenticated user acting for their own booking under existing rules. The *booking authority* is the product authority that determines current eligibility, booking status, capacity and booking availability. Booking-open means that the session is active and the existing policy allows new bookings now. A seat is free when confirmed bookings consume fewer seats than the session’s effective capacity. Waiting entries consume zero seats. The actual capacity, cutoff and eligibility inputs come from the existing product. Class cancellation and the end of booking each stop promotion.

## Requirements

### R-1 — Join the waitlist for a full session

**Basis and delta.** Incoming request, 2026-11-21, “Add a waitlist to our class booking app so full classes don't turn people away.” Preserve normal booking when seats are freely bookable and preserve existing eligibility and booking scope. Add a way for eligible members to queue for a specific full occurrence. No existing workflow was available to inspect. Implementation and checks are pending. See F-1, T-1/T-2 and the replay evidence.

| Acceptance line | Positive | Failure or alternative | Boundary | Consumer surface and confirmation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Join a full session | An eligible member with no confirmed booking or active entry can choose **Join waitlist**. Before they join, explain that a seat may be booked automatically if one becomes available while booking is open, and show the applicable booking consequences. A durable join shows **Waitlisted** and offers **Leave waitlist**. | If the session is missing, inaccessible, canceled, or closed to booking, or the member is definitively ineligible, reject the join without creating an entry. Use the app’s access-safe messaging for missing or inaccessible sessions. | At capacity, join is available. **State and data.** The target is the authenticated member and the exact session within the app’s existing booking scope. Before joining, neither an active entry nor a confirmed booking exists for that pair. After a successful join there is one active entry and no confirmed booking. The app keeps the member/session association and a stable ordering fact in authoritative waitlist state. A reload or lost response cannot create an extra place. R-4 governs access and recovery.

**Open gaps.** The product owner must establish which existing rules allow a member to queue even when a class is full, which terms must be disclosed at join, and whether existing inventory or policy distinguishes seats that cannot go to this waitlist. The booking owner must identify the actual session and member scope and check stale availability on the existing class-detail and booking entry points. If fees, credits, waivers or penalties require consent beyond the ordinary booking flow, affected sessions are unready for enrollment until settled. Any maximum queue length and the admission result when it is reached are unresolved. The booking owner must establish them before bound-dependent verification. Independent status and leave interaction work can proceed.

### R-2 — Offer freed capacity to the queue through automatic booking

**Basis and delta.** Incoming request, 2026-11-21, “so full classes don't turn people away.” Automatic booking in join order is the default used to make the waitlist lead to a seat. Preserve existing booking rules, confirmed seats and normal booking when no queued claim exists. Change which eligible person gets the next free seat while an active queue exists. Source is the incoming request plus the disclosed automatic booking default in R-1. Current cancellation, capacity updates and booking routes are unobserved. Implementation and checks are pending. See F-2, T-3/T-4/T-5 and the replay evidence.

| Acceptance line | Positive | Failure or alternative | Boundary | Consumer surface and confirmation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Allocate a vacancy in order | A confirmed cancellation or effective capacity increase that makes seats free while booking is open triggers allocation, even with all members’ screens closed. Give each seat to the oldest eligible active entry in authority-accepted join order. The member’s entry ends as the ordinary confirmed booking comes into effect. | If the head is definitively ineligible at allocation, end that entry, show the affected member an access-safe reason where permitted, and consider the next entry. If eligibility cannot be established or the promotion fails before commit, keep that candidate waiting, retain the seat for queue reconciliation, and recover under R-4. | Accepted joins have one total order per session, including simultaneous joins. Settle up to the smaller of the number of free seats and eligible waiters, then make any remaining capacity available for normal booking once the queue has cleared. At zero free seats there is no promotion. A vacancy at or after booking closure cannot promote anyone. | Cancel a booking or raise capacity using the authorized route, with queued clients closed. Compare the resulting booking identities, waitlist order and seat count, then open each member’s own view. Use both one-seat and multi-seat cases and inject an eligibility outage followed by recovery. |
| Keep competing writes consistent | Recheck the current session, eligibility and seat count at the point a promotion or normal booking takes effect. If no active queue claims the seat, leave ordinary booking available. | If a normal booking races for capacity owed to the queue, refuse that booking while the claim is active. If the session closes or is canceled first, R-3 ends waiting entries instead. An authorized reduction in capacity that takes effect first leaves only the new available capacity for promotions. | The same seat cannot be taken twice. A promotion is one logical change from waiting to booked, with no externally effective half-booking. A committed promotion before cutoff is an ordinary booking. **State and data.** The affected records are the session’s effective capacity and booking status, confirmed bookings and the ordered active entries for that session. Authority chooses against current confirmed seats, current booking permission and current queue order. A commit removes one active claim and gives that same member one confirmed booking. A rejected or interrupted commit gives no seat away and does not lose the waiting member. On unknown outcomes, resolve from the authoritative state before another promotion. Where processing is pending and a free seat remains claimed, a user may see waiting or allocation feedback, with no booking confirmation until commit.

**Open gaps.** The booking owner must identify every capacity-producing or consuming route, booking cutoff/time-zone semantics and any exceptions required by existing holds, eligibility rules, payment, attendance conflicts or staff actions. The product owner must resolve any conflict between automatic enrollment and existing charges or obligations. The runtime owner must show how current rights are evaluated for off-screen processing and how interruption recovery prevents both bypass and double-booking. These gaps gate real allocation and its connected checks for affected sessions. Queue-order and invariant modeling can proceed independently.

### R-3 — Leave, close and expire the waitlist

**Basis and delta.** Incoming request, 2026-11-21, “Add a waitlist to our class booking app.” Leaving and ending a queue are default lifecycle rules for the requested feature. Preserve ordinary booking cancellation for already booked members and existing session cancellation behavior. Add member withdrawal and closure of waiting entries. No baseline of cancellation, closure, or retention was supplied. Implementation and checks are pending. See F-3, T-6/T-7 and the replay evidence.

| Acceptance line | Positive | Failure or alternative | Boundary | Consumer surface and confirmation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Leave a queue | An authorized member can leave their own active entry. On success the app shows they are no longer waiting and the entry is immediately excluded from allocation. | A known failed leave retains the entry. An uncertain leave reconciles under R-4. A repeated leave of an ended entry has no new effect. | If leave becomes effective first, that entry cannot promote. If promotion becomes effective first, leave returns the booked state and offers the existing booking cancellation route where available. An old leave intent cannot remove a later rejoin. | Race leave against promotion in both orders. Read the departing member’s status and verify which member received the next seat. Replay the old leave after rejoining and check that the new entry survives. |
| End a session’s waitlist | At session cancellation or when booking closes, all remaining entries stop qualifying for any future seat. Show the relevant closed or canceled outcome without a booking confirmation. | A stale join or promotion after closure changes neither queue nor bookings. If an ending action has an unknown outcome, reconcile against authoritative session status. | At the booking cutoff itself closure wins. Terminal waitlist entries never reactivate, including if ordinary booking reopens. A fresh eligible join may occur if an authorized reopening leaves the session full. Existing booked members follow ordinary session rules. | Use the class status and the member’s own waitlist/booking state. Check immediately before and at the configured cutoff, with a separate authorized session cancellation. Verify delayed processing cannot assign a seat afterward. |

**State and data.** Active waiting ends on leave, promotion, definitive ineligibility, closure or cancellation. The authority removes an ended entry from active ordering immediately. A promoted member’s record of the seat is the normal booking. A private terminal outcome may remain only as needed to explain the result or reconcile old intents, then is removed under the applicable retention policy. Account deletion and records removal follow the existing privacy authority. No queue membership carries to a different occurrence.

**Open gaps.** The booking owner must establish actual closure, cancellation, reopening and clock semantics on supported clients. The privacy owner must identify retention, deletion and unattended cleanup rules for terminal outcomes and retry information, including account deletion. These gate expiry integration and data-lifecycle verification. The withdrawal and race rules are specified independently of those choices.

### R-4 — Keep membership private and report the confirmed outcome

**Basis and delta.** Incoming request, 2026-11-21, “Add a waitlist to our class booking app so full classes don't turn people away.” Self-service status and recovery make joining and getting a seat observable. Preserve existing sign-in and access rules, class visibility, and ordinary booking presentation. Add private queue status and outcome feedback. Runtime, UI and logging contracts were unavailable. Implementation and checks are pending. See F-4, T-8 and the replay evidence.

| Acceptance line | Positive | Failure or alternative | Boundary | Consumer surface and confirmation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Show own status | On class details, show whether the signed-in member can book, can join, is waitlisted, is booked, or can no longer act. A promotion appears in the ordinary booking view. A member can see their own relevant outcome on return while that outcome is retained. | An anonymous, revoked or unauthorized request uses the app’s existing sign-in or access-safe denial. Other members’ entries, names, contact details, order and outcomes stay off the client, including response payloads and errors. | Switching account or signing out clears private presentation state. A delayed response from an older identity or a superseded view cannot restore it. | Open as two members and inspect rendered status and the data actually exposed to each. Revoke access during an in-flight read, switch accounts, and verify late results cannot disclose protected data. |
| Report pending, failure and recovery truthfully | Show an in-progress state during join or leave, then update from the authoritative result. Reload or reconnect can recover committed status even if the reply was lost. A pending vacancy is checked and settled by the booking authority without requiring the member to keep a screen open. | A known failure shows a persistent recovery action where appropriate. With an unknown result or unavailable source show an unconfirmed status, then read current state when available. Retrying the same intent cannot duplicate a committed join, undo a later choice, or create an extra booking. | Repeated or out-of-order replies cannot regress the latest confirmed state. Resolve a committed outcome before repeating a write. Unavailable authority cannot confirm a change or expose stale private status as current. | Interrupt join and leave before and after commit, then repeat the same intent, reload and compare authoritative state. Interrupt vacancy processing and verify recovery of the right booking. Observe pending, failure and recovery in the user surface as well as durable state. |

**State and information lifecycle.** Reuse the app’s established identity for authority and privacy. The shared booking/waitlist authority holds the member and session references, active order, and minimum outcome and intent information necessary to reconcile retries. A member reads only their own private state and otherwise sees existing availability information. The client holds transient view and in-flight action state, scoped to the current account and session and discarded on context change or exit. Persistent client copies of the queue are unnecessary. The feature adds no contact data, roster or sharing surface. Credentials, contact information and raw personal payloads stay out of feature logs. Pending operational evidence is access-controlled and purged under the applicable policy. Quotas and retention bounds are open below.

**Service boundary contracts.** The only required logical boundary is the client and the existing booking authority, including any existing separation of identity, inventory or cancellation that discovery may establish. The app rechecks authorization on protected reads and again when consequential writes take effect. Display snapshots may age. Open, refresh and reconnect recover from an authorized current read, and stale views confer no admission or allocation rights. Apply updates to the affected member/session only and preserve unrelated sessions, bookings, configuration and records. Existing sessions need no inferred memberships. A received intent alone is not success. The authority owns final commit, read-back and recovery of an unsettled vacancy or interrupted response. Duplicate or delayed work observes current booking state and cannot replay a terminal transition. No separate notification, subscription, or external delivery boundary is required by this default.

**Owned event coverage.** The booking authority owns retrievable evidence of committed joins, leaves, promotions and terminal queue outcomes, authorization denials on this feature, and allocation failures and subsequent recovery. Use the existing authorized operational destination if it covers these outcomes. Record outcome, coarse reason and an opaque correlation reference without event duplication, credentials, contact details or raw request bodies. Existing identity authority owns login success and failure. Diagnostic sink failure must preserve the original booking result or rejection and be separately observable through existing operational coverage where supported. Verify by retrieving correlated records for a normal flow, denial and interrupted promotion, with a sink failure exercised separately. Missing coverage and runtime access remain open.

**Open gaps.** The runtime and privacy owners must identify existing read/write authority, revocation behavior, reconnect capabilities, records and event lifetimes, role-scoped event access and applicable platform quotas. The runtime owner must demonstrate that committed state and pending vacancies can be recovered on the actual environment across disconnection and interruption. Where an existing remote boundary is found, verify its authorized read and effective write identity, state read-back and recovery before relying on it. This gates concrete integration and deployed verification. Status semantics can be specified independently.

**Fixture.** Each replay starts from a fresh synthetic state. Sessions `spin-17` and `spin-18` are distinct occurrences of the same class, each with capacity 1 and one existing booking held by fictional member Sam. Ari, Bea and Cam are authenticated, eligible members without bookings or entries unless a case says otherwise. Sam can cancel their own booking. Pat is a fixture actor authorized to change capacity or cancel a session under the assumed existing rules. The fixture booking cutoff is `2027-01-15T18:00:00Z` for `spin-17`. Unless specified, actions occur in their listed order before that time, beginning at `17:00:00Z` with the session open. Roles, dates and capacity are model inputs, not claims about the app or global product limits. A real fixture must obtain these starting conditions through authorized routes or approved setup.

**Method and result.** The following are manual specification replays. For each action, start at the fixture or the state left by the preceding action, check the actor and transition guard, then recompute the booking count and identities, ordered active queue, and member-visible output from the resulting state. The named checks below discriminate by comparing multiple identities, independent views, or both event orders. They record model consistency only.

- **Join and vacancy** (R-1/R-2/R-4). Ari joins `spin-17` as `join-a1`, then Bea as `join-b1` (T-1). Repeat `join-a1` after assuming its reply was lost. T-2/T-8 return Ari’s existing entry, leaving order Ari, Bea, with only Sam booked. Both close their screens. Sam cancels `spin-17` using their own booking, yielding one seat (T-3). Cam attempts normal booking before allocation settles. R-2 denies the attempt because the seat is claimed. T-4 then books Ari, leaving Bea waiting and one confirmed booking. On return Ari sees the booking and Bea sees **Waitlisted**. `spin-18` still has Sam’s booking and no queue. Replaying the vacancy signal sees zero free seats and changes neither occurrence.
- **Withdrawal** (R-2/R-3). Ari and Bea join `spin-17` in that order (T-1). Ari leaves using `leave-a1` while Sam still holds the seat (T-6), rejoins using `join-a2` (T-1), then repeats `leave-a1`. T-6/T-8 leave the new entry intact, so order is Bea, Ari. Sam cancels (T-3); T-4 books Bea and leaves Ari waiting. From the original Ari, Bea queue instead, if Sam cancels and promotion commits before Ari’s leave, Ari’s leave produces the booked status and preserves Ari’s confirmed booking. Bea remains waiting. Only the ordinary cancellation action can now release Ari’s seat.
- **Capacity and recovery** (R-2/R-4). Ari, Bea and Cam join `spin-17` in that order (T-1). Ari becomes definitively ineligible through the fixture’s normal eligibility authority. Authorized Pat sets capacity from 1 to 3 (T-3), opening two seats. T-5 ends Ari’s entry, making Bea the head. Simulate interruption before Bea’s promotion commits. R-2/T-8 leave Sam booked, order Bea, Cam, and neither candidate confirmed. When the authority resumes, T-4 books Bea, then Cam. Re-deliver the change after the second commit with its original identity. Current occupancy is three (Sam, Bea, Cam), with no active queue and zero remaining seats. Ari sees an ended outcome while retained. In a separate ordering from the vacancy with Ari’s entry already ended, Pat lowers capacity to 2 before either promotion commits. T-4 gives the one remaining seat to Bea, leaving Sam and Bea booked and Cam waiting. If Ari’s eligibility is instead temporarily unknown, stop allocation while Ari remains the head. On restoration with Ari eligible, the two seats go to Ari and Bea, with Cam waiting. These expected owners distinguish a skip from a transient failure.
- **Closure** (R-1/R-2/R-3). Ari joins `spin-17` at `17:59:58Z` (T-1). Sam cancels at `17:59:59Z` (T-3). If T-4 takes effect at `17:59:59.500Z`, Ari has the only confirmed booking. In the separate ordering where allocation first attempts to commit at `18:00:00Z`, T-7 has ended Ari’s active entry at the cutoff and T-4’s open guard fails. Ari sees closed, the queue is empty, and the confirmed count is zero. Cam’s stale join at the cutoff also has no effect (T-2). In a fresh copy, Ari joins at `17:59:57Z`, Sam cancels their booking at `17:59:58Z`, and Pat cancels the session at `17:59:59Z` before allocation commits. T-7 ends the queue. At `17:59:59.500Z` T-4 cannot act on that canceled session. Ari sees canceled, with no promotion and zero confirmed bookings.
- **Privacy and stale availability** (R-1/R-4). From the fixture Ari begins reading their own `spin-17` status after joining (T-1). Switch the client from Ari to Cam before the reply is rendered. T-8 prevents the old reply from contributing to Cam’s view. Cam’s attempt to read or leave Ari’s entry yields no disclosure or mutation. From a fresh copy instead, Cam sees **Join waitlist** while full, Sam cancels when the queue is empty (T-3), and Cam submits that stale join. T-2 returns ordinary **Book** without joining or booking. Cam can now book under the normal route because the class is open, the seat is free and the queue is empty (R-2). Cam sees their confirmed booking. Ari has no entry or booking in this copy and sees the full session’s **Join waitlist** action. These observations catch a false confirmation or leaked record.

## Submission

**summary** — A full session gains an eligible member waitlist and an allocation path that turns freed capacity into a confirmed booking. Defaults are one active entry per member per occurrence, first accepted join first among currently eligible members, automatic promotion with disclosed consent, private in-app status, member withdrawal and expiry at booking closure. Definitive ineligibility ends the queue entry. A transient eligibility or booking failure holds its place until reconciliation. No material conflict is established from the available sources. Compatibility of automatic booking with existing charges, credits or penalties remains open and gates enrollment and promotion where those rules apply. Existing authority, booking rules and runtime limits also remain open as recorded. Independent behavior modeling is ready. This stage supplies acceptance criteria and manual model replay evidence. Implementation and product verification are pending.

**primary** — R-1 (join when full), R-2 (turn a waitlist place into a confirmed seat when capacity opens). At product-acceptance a primary outcome left unverified may be accepted only with a passed lower-layer test for that outcome.
