# Invoice Billing Specification

## Scope and basis

The 2026-11-21 incoming request, under **The incoming request**, is “Set up automatic reminders for unpaid invoices.” The requested outcome is that the billing system identifies unpaid invoices as they become overdue and automatically reminds the responsible billing recipient. The recipient should be able to recognize the invoice and know what to do next.

The request is the source of approved intent. The policies below are proposed unattended defaults where the request is silent. No prior spec, current-system baseline, discovery, research, target system, or external parity reference was supplied. Existing roles, invoice states, communications, and capabilities are unknown. Invoice issuance, payment collection and allocation, invoice balances and due dates, and access to invoices remain under their existing billing rules. Reminder automation does not change those rules. Manual sends, custom cadence or content editors, new accounts or sharing, and new customer or operator screens are outside scope.

Throughout, **operator** means an existing actor authorized to manage reminder policy or inspect operations for the specified billing account. Those permissions may differ. **Recipient** means the current authorized billing contact for that invoice and account. **Automation** is the unattended runtime acting within an approved account scope. Account, invoice, recipient, and reminder identities are logical contract terms. The target's actual identities and capabilities must be established before dependent integration.

## Requirements

### R-1 Enable unattended reminders within the authorized billing scope

**Basis and delta.** 2026-11-21 request, **The incoming request**, “Set up automatic reminders”. Preserve billing account ownership and invoice workflows as intent. Add account-scoped automation through an authorized operational entry point. No existing entry point, permission, or invocation was verified. Implementation and the checks below are pending.

| Acceptance line | Positive | Failure | Boundary | Consumer surface and confirmation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Activation | An authorized operator activates reminders for a named billing account. Confirmation reflects the committed policy. The automation applies it without an operator session or open client. | Unauthorized, invalid, or failed changes produce no committed change or success acknowledgment. If the outcome is uncertain, the operator can read back the authoritative setting after access recovers. | A scope with no committed setting is off. Repeating an already-applied value reports that value and creates no extra reminder obligation. | Authorized configuration acknowledgment and read-back, plus the unattended processing boundary. Compare the selected scope with a different account and compare enabled processing with processing from the same opening state while disabled. |
| Deactivation | Once off is committed, pending work cannot receive a new dispatch authorization. Re-enabling allows evaluation under current eligibility and existing reminder history. | A stale or unauthorized change cannot overwrite the current setting. | Work authorized for dispatch before off may still complete. Its status follows R-4. By itself, losing the operator's access removes that operator's rights and leaves committed policy in force. Revoking the automation's authority prevents new authorizations. | Configuration and delivery boundaries. **F-1 and transitions.** The operator submits a desired value for one account against the policy state they observed. **T-1** commits a current, authorized change from off to on or on to off and returns the committed value. Resubmitting the already-current value is a no-op. A competing change based on superseded state is rejected unless the requested value already matches the current one. Unknown acknowledgment is resolved by read-back. **T-2** holds new dispatch authorizations while off and releases that policy gate on re-enable. R-2 and R-4 still govern each invoice.

**State and data.** The billing configuration authority owns the committed on/off policy and its revision in the account's authoritative settings. Automation reads only within its permitted scope. Account operators with configuration permission can read and change it. A failed change preserves the previous value. Removing an account ends its policy, and pending work for that scope cannot initiate a new send. The settings mechanism must preserve policy across runtime restarts. Its actual facility, quotas, and access are open.

**Open gaps.** The billing owner must identify the target account, authorized configuration entry point and roles, and activation authority before binding or turning on a real account. The runtime owner must verify autonomous execution and revocable account-scoped authority before integration. No real account is presumed enabled.

### R-2 Determine which unpaid invoices are eligible and when

**Basis and delta.** 2026-11-21 request, **The incoming request**, “for unpaid invoices”. Preserve authoritative invoice status, payment, due date, amount, recipient association, and existing exclusions. Add eligibility evaluation for reminders. The semantic groups below are proposed contract groups. Their mapping to actual billing states and records is unknown. Implementation and the checks below are pending.

The proposed default is one reminder occurrence per invoice for the lifetime of that invoice. An issued, collectible invoice qualifies when it has a positive outstanding balance and a valid due date, and the billing account's local calendar date is later than that due date. The first eligible date is the next local calendar date. This includes **partially paid** invoices with a positive balance and eligible invoices **already overdue** at activation. A missing evaluation at the threshold leaves an otherwise eligible invoice discoverable on a later evaluation.

| Acceptance line | Positive | Failure | Boundary | Consumer surface and confirmation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Invoice state and date | An issued, collectible invoice with a positive balance, including a partially paid one, becomes eligible when the local date first exceeds its due date. A later unattended evaluation can find still-eligible invoices, including existing overdue invoices in an activated account. | Suppress drafts and other unissued invoices, paid or zero/credit-balance invoices, voided or canceled invoices, and invoices marked non-collectible or subject to an authoritative collections hold. An unknown state or unreadable authority prevents dispatch and records an operational reason. | Throughout the due date and on earlier dates the invoice is ineligible. A missing or invalid due date, time zone, or balance prevents dispatch and is distinguishable from a normally ineligible invoice. | Unattended processing outcome and captured outbound effects. Compare each named group using otherwise eligible records, compare immediately before and at the start of the next local date, and compare activation with a subsequent evaluation. |
| Freshness and resumption | Before dispatch authorization, use current policy, automation authority, invoice status, balance, due date and recipient. Corrected data can qualify on a later evaluation if the occurrence is still available under R-4. | When payment, cancellation, a hold, an extended due date, recipient removal, disablement or revocation takes effect before dispatch authorization, it prevents dispatch under the superseded facts. A source outage defers evaluation or dispatch and leaves the work recoverable. | A partial payment before authorization changes the balance used for the message if the invoice remains eligible. If a change follows authorization, the dispatch may complete with the authorized facts. Acceptance never creates another occurrence when the due date or balance is edited. | Authoritative invoice/configuration boundary and delivery capture. Control the order of authoritative changes and authorization. Assert captured recipient and balance or absence of a handoff, as applicable. |

**F-2 and transitions.** The unattended evaluation considers invoices in enabled scopes as time advances or on recovery. **T-3** makes an eligible, unsent invoice available for dispatch, keyed by its billing account and immutable invoice identity. An ineligible record produces no send. Incomplete or unavailable authoritative facts defer work and expose the reason. **T-4** grants dispatch authorization only after current eligibility and recipient validation, subject to R-1 and R-4. The deciding order is between authoritative changes and that authorization. If the target cannot establish this ordering at its supported boundary, the integration remains open.

**State and data.** Invoice facts originate in the authoritative billing source. Reminder processing reads them under its permitted scope and makes no invoice mutations. Before and after evaluation, the billing source remains the owner of status, balance, due date and account/invoice identity. A stale discovery read can identify a candidate but cannot grant dispatch authority. Rediscovery and any incremental state must preserve coverage of previously due work after downtime and leave unrelated billing changes intact.

**Open gaps.** The billing owner must map issued and collectible states, holds (including any disputes or payment-plan states), date and balance semantics, account time-zone source, and invoice identity reuse or migration rules before eligibility integration. Confirm the already-overdue cohort before the first real activation. The runtime owner must demonstrate the effective authorization ordering and visibility of payment, policy, and recipient changes before relying on that boundary. The maximum allowed age for catch-up and handling of very old invoices are open product decisions. Catch-up against real invoices depends on that decision.

**State and data.** The billing/contact authority owns contact scope and address. The automation uses address and message fields only to perform the authorized handoff. The delivery channel and intended recipient receive the minimum invoice and address information specified above. No credentials, payment instrument details, customer attachments, or copied invoice records belong in the message or diagnostics. No separate product-side copy of the message or address is required for history. Provider retention and removal follow the approved channel policy once confirmed. A message already handed off may reach the recipient after policy changes or account removal.

**Open gaps.** The billing owner must confirm the approved contact-selection and suppression rules, sender and billing instructions. The delivery owner must confirm the authorized channel, sender identity, payload constraints, redaction and retention, and permitted controlled mailbox before delivery integration and verification. A controlled receiver is mandatory for recipient-level verification. If access requires a person and no authorized host route exists, the billing operator owns that unverified observation at handoff or the effect it gates. No live recipient is used as a verification fixture.

### R-4 Prevent duplicate reminders and recover interrupted work

**Basis and delta.** 2026-11-21 request, **The incoming request**, “automatic reminders”. Preserve invoice and account identity rules and any applicable existing communication policies as intent. Add recoverable occurrence tracking and truthful handling of dispatch uncertainty. No runtime durability or delivery capability was observed. Implementation and the checks below are pending.

| Acceptance line | Positive | Failure | Boundary | Consumer surface and confirmation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Occurrence uniqueness | Each account and immutable invoice pair has one logical occurrence. Overlapping evaluations, repeated triggers, and restarts converge on the same occurrence. Confirmed channel acceptance prevents a further dispatch for that occurrence. | A stale worker or duplicate result cannot revert an accepted occurrence or authorize a second dispatch. | Distinct invoice identities can each get a reminder, including identical invoice-local labels in different accounts. A changed date, balance, or contact within one invoice does not create a new occurrence. | Observe occurrence history and outbound effects across overlapping evaluations and a restart. Compare same-invoice replay with distinct invoices and inspect outcomes after a late result. |
| Recovery and exhaustion | Interrupted work before dispatch authorization remains recoverable. Definitive temporary non-acceptance can be retried within an approved retry policy, with a fresh eligibility decision and the same occurrence identity. Permanent rejection or retry exhaustion ends automated attempts with an actionable status. | If the channel may have accepted the handoff, record an unresolved outcome and reconcile against authoritative channel evidence. Count acceptance if established. Only evidence of non-acceptance permits another attempt under the retry policy. Without such evidence, keep the unresolved outcome visible and suppress further attempts. | At the approved retry deadline or attempt cap, whichever comes first, no new retry is authorized. An already-authorized attempt can still resolve. An authoritative late acceptance of that attempt closes the unresolved outcome. A bounce after confirmed acceptance records a delivery failure and does not reopen dispatch. | Interrupt before authorization, after authorization with no recorded result, and after confirmed acceptance. Inject definitive refusal, exhaustion and late results. Check both the operator status and the number and identity of outbound handoffs. |

**F-4 and transitions.** **T-5** records the result of an authorized attempt. The record can become accepted, retryable after definitive temporary non-acceptance, closed after definitive permanent non-acceptance, or unresolved after an ambiguous result. An interruption after authorization with no definitive outcome takes the unresolved path. **T-6** reconciles an unresolved attempt using authoritative channel evidence. A definitive result follows T-5. Repeated or stale results cannot overturn a newer result or reauthorize a handoff. **T-7** lets retryable work return to T-4 only while the retry policy permits it. At expiry or exhaustion it closes without another handoff. Before authorization, current ineligibility prevents an attempt without consuming the occurrence. Recoverable readiness may be derived from authoritative state.

**State and data.** The automation owns the durable occurrence status, scoped identifiers, attempt identity, and bounded outcome metadata. A ready invoice can enter an authorized attempt only if it still passes R-1 to R-3 and either has no earlier attempt or T-7 permits a retry. An active, unresolved, accepted or closed occurrence cannot authorize another attempt. Concurrent processors must agree on which attempt owns dispatch, and a reclaimed worker cannot supersede a newer committed result. Recovery after restart must preserve the suppression decision for any possible or confirmed handoff. Dispatch activity ends with acceptance or closure; its suppression record follows the lifecycle below. An unresolved attempt stays suppressive until reconciled or until the identity can never produce future work. Retain the minimum suppression state as long as an invoice could be evaluated again. Purge personal or detailed diagnostic data independently under the approved policy. Account or invoice removal ends dispatch eligibility even if earlier work is replayed. Removal of history requires either preserved suppression or confirmation that the corresponding identity cannot re-enter processing.

**Open gaps.** The product and operations owners must establish the permitted retry delays, deadline, attempt cap, and handling of definitive rejection after a contact correction before enabling dependent retries. The delivery owner must establish which responses or read-backs prove non-acceptance, and how unresolved attempts can be reconciled. The runtime owner must verify durable ownership, restart recovery, concurrent completion protection, deletion and any migration handling. Ambiguous outcomes remain suppressed if reconciliation is unavailable.

## Submission

**summary** — The specification covers account-scoped unattended activation, eligibility for overdue invoices, customer delivery, and handling of retry, duplicate suppression, and operational status. Assumptions are explicit activation with off as the initial default, local-date eligibility beginning the day after the due date, inclusion of already-overdue invoices within the approved catch-up cohort, inclusion of positive partial balances, one occurrence per invoice, plain-text email through the authorized billing channel, and existing operational surfaces. Open decisions include live catch-up bounds, approved sender/contact policy, retry bounds, channel recovery evidence, retention, and target authority and capacity. Those gaps keep the dependent integration, real dispatch or verification unready. The eligibility and lifecycle contracts can be reviewed independently. No material conflict between sources was observed. No implementation or product verification took place.

**primary** — R-1 (unattended activation), R-2 (unpaid-invoice eligibility), R-3 (recipient gets an actionable reminder).
