# Grok Bot for Salesforce

## Scope and basis

“Create a Grok bot in a Salesforce development org.” — Incoming request, 2026-11-21, “The incoming request.” “Grok bot” maps to R-2. “In a Salesforce development org” maps to R-1. R-3 and R-4 cover recovery and data handling for that outcome. The standalone stage supplies no prior specification, discovery or research result, or selected baseline. Existing org state is unknown. Grok is the required inference provider. The request specifies no reference product for workflow or visual parity.

An assigned user opens **Grok Bot** inside authenticated Lightning Experience, enters text in **Prompt**, selects **Send**, and reads a Grok reply there. The default is independent, single-turn questions using synthetic, non-sensitive content.

**Open gap.** The implementation owner must select a finite request deadline and define its cutoff observation against actual org and model behavior. A matching completion observed before the deadline may be processed. At or after the deadline a still-pending attempt terminates as a temporary failure and a delayed result is ignored. This gates timed integration and boundary checks. See capacity and timing below.

The org owner controls credential references and grants. The xAI team owner controls provider credentials and retention terms. Retire the org credential and revoke the provider key when access ends. xAI documents default audit retention of 30 days followed by deletion, with team-specific terms and zero-data-retention options. The team owner must confirm the applicable policy and permitted account use before live calls. The org owner must check native diagnostics configuration and whether relevant paths can expose payloads before live acceptance. Synthetic verification is independent of those prerequisites.

## Non-functional requirements

- **Visual direction and component composition.** In the bot surface, start light with a view-local dark option, following Salesforce shell conventions. The named reference is light Google Gemini with Material 3 Expressive principles, adapted through the ShipLoop [UI visual-direction guide](../GUIDES/platforms/ui.md#light-expressive-starting-point). Use an airy layout, tonal rounded surfaces, readable type hierarchy and a single accent for the primary action. The prompt and reply are the focal area, with the composer in easy reach. A consistent spacing and type scale, semantic light/dark palettes and one icon set apply across composer, exchange, feedback and appearance control. Inspect the rendered screen in both appearances and in empty, waiting, reply and failure states. The plan selects concrete treatments within this direction.
- **Interaction and accessibility.** The empty state makes entry and disclosure clear. Pending, error and result changes are visible and announced to assistive technology without unexpectedly moving focus. Visible focus, accessible names, logical keyboard traversal, keyboard activation and a reachable composer apply in both themes. Motion is used only to explain state changes and is suppressed when reduced motion is requested. Verify keyboard and assistive-technology behavior. Check visible, unobscured focus and WCAG 2.2 AA contrast, including 4.5:1 for standard text, 3:1 for large text and 3:1 where required for controls and graphic indicators. [WCAG 2.2][wcag]
- **Responsive behavior.** At 320 and 1280 CSS pixels of available width, the bot content reflows without horizontal overflow, including long unbroken answer text. Prompt, feedback and controls remain readable and operable. Verify both widths in a browser within the host surface or a constrained host region. Native Salesforce mobile app support is outside scope. [WCAG 2.2, Reflow][wcag]
- **Capacity and timing.** For interactive sends, verify one provider call per admitted attempt, no auto-retries, no app record writes, and bounded payloads under model and Salesforce limits. Salesforce documents a 120-second cumulative callout timeout per Apex transaction, 6 MB synchronous callout request/response size in the general limits table, and current 10 MB synchronous Apex heap in Winter ’27, with a 6 MB compatibility setting. The HttpRequest reference lists a 10 MB synchronous request-body limit, so verify applicable request and response limits on the target and size conservatively against the lower documented ceiling until resolved. In Developer Edition, the external callout concurrency ceiling is 20. These are ceilings, and the app must leave headroom for serialization, parsing and simultaneous working copies. Unresolved bounds are the deployed response/output budget, end-to-end and outbound deadlines, and approved team usage/cost envelope for live checks. The implementation owner and team owner resolve them using actual edition, model entitlement, limits and a controlled timing/size observation before dependent live checks. A cap-induced incomplete response follows R-3.b. No production SLA or capacity target is required for this development feature. [Salesforce callout limits][sf-time] · [Apex governors][sf-limits] · [Winter ’27 heap limits][sf-winter] · [HttpRequest body limit][sf-http] · [xAI rate limits][x-rate]
- **Privacy and operability.** Under a real approved dev session, verify R-4 isolation and data handling, confirm the known actual retention policy before exercising the provider, and verify redaction on successful and failed outcomes. After deployment, authorized operators can identify the feature revision and permission state and recover per R-1.c. Durable history, migration, shared data recovery and production portability targets are excluded by the development-only, transient scope.

| Flow | Allowed transitions and observable outcomes | Case mapping |
| --- | --- | --- |
| F-1 Enter the bot | T-1 Salesforce grants let assigned user Dev-A reach the empty view. Signed-in Dev-B without grants is denied entry. An unassigned invocation made from a previously open view is denied after grant removal. An operator reconciles setup or rolls back using R-1.c. | R-1.a–c, R-4.b |
| F-2 Submit and complete | T-2 Idle empty/reply/error view admits valid Send, snapshots the prompt, and becomes pending. T-4 An idle Clear returns the view to empty. | R-2.a–c, R-3.a, R-4.a and R-4.c |
| F-3 Recover or discard | T-5 Failure or deadline on the active attempt moves pending to error with draft retained. Explicit Send from error follows T-2. **Desk replay method.** Independently walk the guards in the acceptance table and track view, draft, active attempt, answer, error and outbound count. The following are recomputed contract outcomes. In each fixture, unheld completions are observed before the deadline and the prompt limit admits the fixture text. These outcomes supply no evidence of deployed behavior.

1. **Admission, reply and Clear** (F-2). Assume Dev-A has the R-1 grants in fixture org Dev-1 and opens an empty view. Submit a string of space, tab and newline. R-2.a rejects it with count 0. Replace it with `What is 17 + 8?` and Send. T-2 admits it with count 1. Withhold the result and activate Send again. R-3.a leaves count 1 and the active prompt unchanged. Supply a completed result for this attempt with a reasoning item `fixture-private` and two assistant display segments `25` and ` <tag>`. Under T-3 the rendered answer is the literal `25 <tag>` in order, with no tag execution or reasoning. Send `What is 9 * 8?` after completion. T-2 gives count 2 and only the second prompt is sent. Return completed text `72`, then Clear while idle. T-4 leaves the view empty and count 2. Repeat the first success from a clean fixture with display text `twenty-five` and check the exact changed reply so a hardcoded answer fails.
2. **Retry and stale error** (F-3). Dev-A starts in an authorized empty view. Send `Reply with ORBIT.` and inject HTTP 429. T-2 then T-5 produces a rate-limit message, retained draft, absent answer and count 1. Clear the fixture fault and manually Send the retained draft. Count becomes 2. Hold its completion, leave and reopen under R-3.c and send `Reply with LANTERN.` from the empty replacement view. Count becomes 3. Deliver the old attempt's delayed HTTP 503 while the replacement waits, then deliver its own completed `LANTERN`. T-6 discards the old error. T-3 yields only the LANTERN exchange. No automatic retry occurred.
3. **Revocation** (F-1). In the desk model the org owner may remove access, and Dev-A has an already-open idle view. Once invocation permission removal is effective, submit `Reply with ORBIT.` from that view. Under R-1.b, the attempt cannot reach xAI and the outbound delta is 0. The view can report only safe denial. Dev-B, initially unassigned, starts from sign-in and cannot reach the prompt via the delivery link. This derives the oracle from effective roles, whose actual availability is an open prerequisite.
4. **Isolated authorized views** (F-2 and F-3). Start Dev-A and Dev-C as different users with the R-1 grants. Each opens an empty view. Dev-A sends `Reply with ORBIT.` and Dev-C sends `Reply with LANTERN.` under T-2. Return Dev-C's completed `LANTERN` first and Dev-A's completed `ORBIT` second. T-3 gives each user their own prompt and answer. Dev-A clears after completion under T-4. Dev-A sees neither answer and Dev-C still sees LANTERN. Each view caused one provider request, and Clear caused none.
5. **Timed and size edges.** NOT-REPLAYED: concrete values for the request deadline and payload bounds are unresolved. The implementation owner supplies those values, then checks just below, exactly at, and just above each applicable bound. Use repeated ASCII text with byte length equal to the accepted prompt limit and one extra byte, plus multibyte input to discriminate byte-count and character-count validation.

**Live case and policy.** In the selected org as Dev-A, submit `What is 17 + 8?` and then `What is 9 * 8?`, accepting an answer that conveys 25 and 72 respectively without assuming exact wording. Confirm each provider result corresponds to its send and that the UI displays that result. Use the controlled replay to prove exact output propagation and guard handling. Live org role, browser, rotation/diagnostics and inference checks are NOT-REPLAYED: no selected org or account, role access, credential configuration, or implementation exists for this stage to verify. The delivery owner is responsible for approved org/revision and user-visible checks at release-verify. Live fault injection and credential rotation require an authorized disposable fixture or an owner-supported check on the selected target. The org and xAI team owners supply person-only access/configuration at the dependent operation, with any person-only read-back due at handoff if access is unavailable to the verifier. Product acceptance may leave at most one primary requirement unverified only with a relevant passed lower-layer test. This does not certify any outstanding live obligation.
