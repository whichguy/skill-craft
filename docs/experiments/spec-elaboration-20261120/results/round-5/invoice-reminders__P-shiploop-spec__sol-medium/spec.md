# Invoice Reminders

## Source and scope

**S1 — User request, 2026-11-20.** “Set up automatic reminders for unpaid invoices.” This is the authority for automatic operation and notifying the intended recipient about an unpaid invoice. The eligibility, cadence and channel below are proposed product defaults where the request is silent.

No prior spec, selected baseline, repository, discovery, research, or reference system was supplied for this standalone stage. Existing behavior, current settings and parity are unknown. This spec defines a proposed additive capability. Payment processing, invoicing, collections escalation, and new account management are outside the requested change. No deployment or live send is part of this stage.

The account's invoice authority supplies payment state, cutoff and billing contact. Authorized account configuration determines the enabled scope. Unattended processing evaluates that scope and submits eligible reminders. The billing contact receives the email. An authorized billing operator can inspect the authoritative outcome through the supported operational surface, which has yet to be identified. No new identity, shared workspace, settings page, or manual send control is required.

## Requirements

### R-1 — Evaluate unpaid invoices automatically

**Basis and delta.** S1, § Source and scope, “automatic” and “unpaid invoices.” Adds unattended discovery and eligibility evaluation. The proposed change leaves invoice amounts, statuses and contact management with their existing owners. There is no observed prior behavior to reconcile. Implementation and checks C1–C3 are pending.

**Proposed rules.** Once automation is enabled for an authorized scope, evaluate both newly overdue and already overdue invoices in the approved rollout cohort. Default to one reminder per eligible invoice over its lifetime. Eligibility requires an issued, collectible invoice, a positive outstanding balance, a current cutoff strictly earlier than the evaluation instant, and an authorized billing contact. Partially paid invoices with a remaining balance qualify. Draft, paid, void, canceled, deleted and collection-held invoices are ineligible. A missing or invalid cutoff, unreliable eligibility facts or unavailable current authority prevent dispatch. Scope enablement precedes unattended processing. No resend is triggered solely by an edit or reopening after a reminder was accepted.

| Acceptance line | Positive | Failure | Boundary | Consumer surface and confirmation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| C1 | With the authorized scope enabled and no user session or per-invoice action, a due evaluation finds the eligible invoice and processes it through R-2. | If the invoice read fails or returns an incomplete scan, record the interruption and recover unfinished coverage through a later evaluation. Do not infer eligibility from a failed read. | With an empty cohort or disabled scope, no reminder is submitted. | Observe the unattended run and actual outbound request, then compare interrupted and recovered coverage with the authoritative invoice fixture. |
| C2 | An issued, collectible invoice with a positive residual balance becomes eligible strictly after its cutoff, including an invoice already overdue in an approved cohort. | A missing or invalid cutoff or unresolved balance suppresses submission and is distinguishable from normal ineligibility. | Before and exactly at cutoff, and for zero balance, draft, void, canceled, deleted or collection-held invoices, observe no submission. | Set authoritative invoice facts and controlled time. Compare the eligibility outcome and outbound submissions across otherwise matching fixtures. |
| C3 | Immediately before each submission, including a retry, use current invoice facts, contact and enabled scope. A qualifying invoice uses the current contact and balance. | A payment, hold, deletion, disablement, contact revocation or other disqualifying change effective by the final authority check suppresses the submission. Unavailable authority defers it. | The final check is the proposed cutoff for the race. A change after that check may race with submission. Any later attempt requires a fresh check, and an accepted submission cannot be recalled by this feature. | At the invoice and delivery boundaries, order changes on both sides of the final check and compare the authoritative revision, submitted payload and absence of additional submissions. |

**State and service boundary.** The invoice authority owns the invoice, balance, cutoff, collectible status and billing-contact relationship under the account and invoice identity. The source's existing authorized writers alone change these facts. The reminder processor reads the permitted cohort and consumes only the fields needed for evaluation and delivery. Invoice data stays with the authority except for transient evaluation and the minimal delivery payload in R-2. No worker or operator can use a caller-supplied account identifier to obtain access. Before and after evaluation the invoice is unmodified. The rule for concurrent changes is the final authoritative check in C3. Stale projections or event hints may identify candidates but cannot authorize dispatch. Source-side changes provide the authority for deletions and revocations. A failed or partial read must be distinguishable from an empty cohort, and missed or interrupted enumeration must be recoverable without duplicate notifications. Existing invoice event history is outside this feature's ownership. The reminder processor owns evaluation, suppression and source-read failure outcomes under R-3. A subscription or cache is unnecessary to specify this contract.

**Open gaps.** The billing owner must identify the approved sender scope and rollout cohort, including whether to enable existing overdue invoices. The billing owner must confirm cutoff semantics for date-only invoices and time zones, collectible status mapping (including credits, disputes and holds), and any exclusions or policy changes for reminders. The runtime owner must find the authoritative enumeration and current-read routes, worker identity and current access, and establish that C3 can be enforced at the selected boundary. Live scanning and dispatch depend on those answers. Eligibility logic against explicit cutoff instants and synthetic invoice facts can proceed independently.

### R-2 — Deliver the reminder to the authorized billing contact

**Basis and delta.** S1, § Source and scope, “reminders for unpaid invoices.” Adds an outbound notification for an eligible invoice. Existing invoice and payment journeys are preserved by design. Email and the one-reminder cadence are defaults, and no delivery provider, template or invoice destination has been observed. Implementation and checks C4–C5 are pending.

| Acceptance line | Positive | Failure | Boundary | Consumer surface and confirmation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| C4 | An eligible invoice produces an email for the current authorized billing contact. It identifies the sender and invoice, states the current outstanding amount and currency, and directs the recipient to the existing authorized invoice or payment route when available. | If there is no valid authorized contact, submit nothing, record the reason and allow future evaluation after correction. Never substitute an unverified recipient. | The message is sent only within the enabled account and current approved contact scope. An invoice link, if used, preserves the established access policy. | In a controlled mailbox, inspect sender, envelope recipient, invoice reference, balance, currency and any link destination. Use distinct accounts and invoice values to detect leakage or hardcoded content. |
| C5 | One confirmed acceptance consumes the reminder for that scoped invoice. Concurrent evaluations, retries after acceptance, restart and later invoice edits produce no additional accepted submission. | A definitively rejected attempt stays unaccepted. An ambiguous response is recorded as unresolved, with resubmission held while its effect is unknown. Recovery follows C6 and C7. | Distinct invoice identities can each receive a reminder, even with the same display reference or contact. A delivery failure after acceptance does not rearm the invoice. | Compare the durable outcome with distinct provider submissions and recipient messages under replay, overlap, failure and restart. Use provider-observed identity and effect, not a local “sent” flag alone. |

**State and service boundary.** The reminder identity is scoped to the owning account and stable invoice identity for the one reminder, independent of attempt identity, display reference and recipient address. Before handoff the processor establishes recoverable responsibility for that identity with exclusive submission authority. Afterward its outcome is one of the states in R-3. An obsolete processor cannot override a newer outcome or make a second submission. Proof of non-acceptance must rule out later acceptance of that attempt, including an outstanding request or a processor that could still submit it. A lookup with no match is insufficient on its own. The delivery service, if used, owns the external acceptance evidence. Acceptance means the service took responsibility for the submission. Only recipient-side evidence establishes receipt. An asynchronous notification is an observation of a provider outcome only after its origin and attempt correlation are established. Revalidate R-1 authority for a new attempt. Destination and content are disclosed only to the approved delivery route and intended recipient. No raw mailbox content is required in the product's reminder history.

**Recipient interaction.** Use a plain-text transactional email by default, with a clear subject, labeled invoice details, and one clear next step using the existing billing route or sender's approved billing contact instructions. The recipient reads it using their email client. The reminder creates no new interactive interface or client-side state. Email submission, acceptance, delivery and failure have distinct meanings for operator feedback.

**Open gaps.** The billing owner must confirm channel, sender, contact selection for multiple contacts, required content, contact permissions or exclusions, and the existing billing route or approved reply instructions. The runtime owner must verify the actual sending identity, provider route, submission and receipt semantics, and supported correlation and recovery capabilities. No live send can rely on those until established. Copy and scope checks can be prepared with synthetic fixtures.

### R-3 — Recover processing and show truthful outcomes

**Basis and delta.** S1, § Source and scope, “automatic reminders.” Adds recoverable processing and observable outcome distinctions as inferred safeguards for the requested external effect. Invoice and payment state remain outside reminder control. No operational surface or event coverage was observed. Implementation and checks C6–C9 are pending.

| Acceptance line | Positive | Failure | Boundary | Consumer surface and confirmation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| C6 | Pending work survives interruption and is revisited without user presence. After confirmed non-acceptance, a retry can proceed only if transient failure policy, remaining retry allowance and current R-1 eligibility all permit it. | Confirmed permanent failure or exhausted retry allowance ends automatic attempts and exposes an actionable outcome. Disqualification suppresses further submissions while it applies. | A restart before any possible submission may resume work once established. A restart after possible submission follows C7. | Interrupt unattended processing at both handoff boundaries. Compare the recovered outcome, attempt records and provider acceptance history. |
| C7 | An unresolved attempt reconciles to accepted on authoritative acceptance evidence, or becomes retryable on authoritative proof of non-acceptance if C6 permits. | With missing or inconclusive evidence, keep the attempt unresolved, surface it for an authorized operator and hold resubmission. | Duplicate or stale observations cannot move accepted work backwards or overwrite the current outcome. | Inject an interrupted provider response and reconcile with independently controlled provider history. Compare durable outcome and accepted submission count before and after a worker restart. |
| C8 | Authorized billing operators can distinguish ineligible or suppressed, pending, retryable, unresolved, accepted and ended without acceptance. Report recipient receipt or delivery failure only when supported by the selected surface; neither changes the acceptance invariant. | A denied or revoked identity cannot read reminder status or discover another account's records. A rejected or timed-out attempt cannot be presented as accepted. | Recheck permission on each status read even when invoice facts have not changed. Recipient visibility is limited to the addressed notification and existing authorized billing route. | Read status through the selected operator surface as authorized, revoked and unrelated actors. Compare its outcome with delivery evidence and check denial responses for data disclosure. |
| C9 | Owned outcome records allow authorized operators to trace evaluation interruptions, suppression, attempts, acceptance, retry, unresolved outcomes, terminal failures, recovery and access denials using safe correlation. | Diagnostic sink failure cannot turn a failed send into success or authorize another submission. The durable operation outcome remains authoritative for recovery and status. | Normal and error records omit recipient addresses, message bodies, invoice amounts, payment-link contents and credentials. Access and retention apply to diagnostic records as well as status. | Read back owned outcomes and inject a diagnostic sink outage and unauthorized lookup. Inspect allowed metadata and confirm original outcome and deduplication survive. |

**Lifecycle and service boundary.** The reminder processor owns durable progress from reservation through known outcome, keyed by scoped invoice identity. Pending and retryable can advance only with current authority. Unresolved advances only on authoritative reconciliation, never on elapsed time alone. Accepted or ended is terminal for automatic submission, and accepted can carry subsequent delivery observations. A pre-submission suppression does not consume the reminder and may be reevaluated if eligibility returns. Revocation stops protected read-back and disallows new unauthorized dispatches, even if unfinished work exists. Accepted evidence and terminal outcomes must outlive any replay eligibility. On removal from the reminder scope, prevent subsequent restart or replay from sending old work, including after expiry or deletion of reminder records. Re-enrollment must preserve this invariant or keep the affected invoices out of automatic processing until it can. The operator's status view has no independent authority. Authentication lifecycle and provider-owned delivery diagnostics remain with their respective owners. Subscriptions, long-lived connections and client caches are inapplicable to this contract.

Durable reminder state may retain scoped identifiers, attempt references, status, reason codes and timestamps needed for recovery and read-back. Recipient address, message content, outstanding amount and payment link are read for the active attempt and passed only to the authorized delivery route. Application diagnostics may contain opaque correlation and sanitized outcomes subject to restricted access. Runtime credentials belong to the runtime's approved secret/identity mechanism, with renewal and removal owned by the runtime operator. The billing retention policy must cover reminder outcomes, diagnostic records and provider-held delivery data. Purge when their approved lifetime ends, preserving the replay exclusion above, and confirm removal at each controlled destination.

**Open gaps.** The runtime owner must establish durable recovery, exclusive submission authority, operational read-back and diagnostic retrieval in the deployed runtime. The billing and runtime owners must agree retry intervals, finite budget, maximum reminder age, responsibility for unresolved sends, and retention and deletion rules (including provider retention and re-enrollment). Verify quotas and diagnostic coverage before enabling live work. Pending those choices the safe operational default is no blind resend, no live activation, and no unverified use of a status surface. The synthetic recovery model is independent of the provider selection.

## Behavior model and acceptance evidence

### Flows

| Flow | Ordered workflow and alternatives | Outcome and trace |
| --- | --- | --- |
| F-1 | An unattended evaluation begins after authorized scope enablement. It reads the cohort and evaluates current invoice facts (T-1). It skips an ineligible invoice and revisits incomplete coverage in a later run. | Automatic identification of unpaid overdue invoices, R-1 C1–C3. |
| F-2 | An eligible candidate obtains submission authority (T-2). The processor rechecks authority and submits asynchronously relative to the evaluation trigger (T-3), or suppresses the attempt (T-1). The provider responds or leaves an ambiguous outcome (T-4). | Recipient reminder and single acceptance, R-2 C4–C5, with R-1 C3 checked at execution. |
| F-3 | Pending work is resumed, a confirmed failure is retried or ended (T-5), or an unresolved outcome is reconciled (T-6). Authorized staff read the resulting status and diagnostics (T-7). The lifecycle ends per approved retention (T-8). | Recovery, access and truthful outcomes, R-3 C6–C9. |

An invoice may become eligible later while its submission state remains unchanged. “Accepted” and “ended” are terminal for automatic submission. Delivery observation is separate from acceptance.

## Submission

**summary**

- Defined the automatic evaluation, recipient notification and recovery contracts with consumer-surface acceptance lines. Stage evidence is a desk walkthrough of specified behavior. Implementation and product verification remain pending.
- Assumed an authorized account scope, an approved cohort that can include existing overdue invoices, one plain-text email per eligible invoice, and strict overdue eligibility with a positive remaining balance. Assumed the final current-authority check is the decision point for payment or revocation races. Defaulted to withholding live sends until scope and runtime authority are established, and withholding blind retries on unknown submission outcomes.
- Open decisions include the actual cohort, billing rules, communication policy, timing, retry and retention bounds, and selected runtime capabilities. No conflict between supplied requirements is known. If billing policy requires a stronger payment-versus-submission guarantee than C3 or the selected runtime can provide, record it as an open conflict and keep that dispatch path unready.
- The billing owner supplies the person-only policy and scope decisions before affected live activation. The runtime owner establishes supported routes, permission, state, quotas, and operational read-back before dependent planning or preparation. Synthetic contract work can proceed. The verification owner checks the authorized worker/provider/operator boundaries at system-test and actual consumer receipt by product-acceptance in a controlled inbox, or at release-verify for a production-only obligation. If inspection and test strategy establish a person-only verification route, retain the named owner, due gate or handoff phase, and unverified status. At product-acceptance, at most one primary requirement may remain unverified, and only with a passed lower-layer test for that requirement. None has passed here.
- Revalidate against later authoritative findings. Record evidence and affected consumers in the knowledge checkpoint, investigate current-scope gaps through the applicable stage or Improve route, and use add-work for acceptance corrections. If a finding conflicts with accepted intent, obtain direction before changing the contract.

**primary**

- R-2 — Send the eligible invoice's reminder to the authorized billing contact.
