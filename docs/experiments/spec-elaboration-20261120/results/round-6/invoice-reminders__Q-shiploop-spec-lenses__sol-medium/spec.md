# Invoicing product specification

## Scope and basis

**Source.** Incoming request, 2026-11-21, § The incoming request: “Set up automatic reminders for unpaid invoices.” The standalone-stage instructions supply the evidence limits. No earlier specification, repository, selected baseline, reference product, discovery or research result was supplied. Existing product behavior and access remain unknown. Compatibility clauses below describe what the feature must preserve once the baseline is identified.

**Actors.** A billing operator with current authority over the billing account manages automatic reminders and can inspect outcomes within that account. An unattended processor evaluates invoices and requests delivery under the account's authorized sender authority. The invoice's current authorized billing recipient receives the reminder. Invoice, payment, recipient and permission changes come from their existing authorities. The recipient needs no new identity or sharing grant.

**Working product defaults.** Use the existing approved billing email channel, one automatic reminder per eligible invoice, and evaluate after its due date. Treat an invoice with a collectible balance after a partial payment as unpaid. Offer controls through the existing billing administration surface. These defaults are proposed interpretations of the request. Honor established billing, communication and appearance rules when discovered, and reconcile any conflict before the affected behavior is frozen. Manual resends, recurring dunning sequences, new messaging channels, payment collection, and customer account management are outside this change.

## Requirements

The working default includes older overdue invoices, subject to verified history and existing policy. Unresolved history coverage holds activation for affected invoices. Pure eligibility and date semantics can be reviewed from synthetic fixtures.

### R-2 — Control automatic reminders

**Basis and delta.** 2026-11-21 request, § The incoming request, “Set up automatic reminders.” Add durable, scoped enablement and suppression controls. Preserve existing billing permissions and independent invoice and manual communication flows. Control names describe observable actions. The actual administration surface and account model were not supplied. Pending implementation and permission and state verification.

| Acceptance line | Positive expectation | Failure expectation | Boundary expectation | Surface and confirmation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| R-2.a | An authorized billing operator can read the effective account setting and save enable or disable and a reminder delay in positive whole calendar days. The initial state is disabled and the proposed default delay is one calendar day. Confirm a change only after it is durably effective. | Reject unauthorized, cross-account, malformed, fractional, nonpositive, out-of-supported-range and stale conflicting changes. A failed save preserves the last confirmed setting and exposes a safe reason to the authorized actor. An uncertain save is shown as unconfirmed until read-back. | A repeated identical save has no extra effect. A missed response, reload or restart allows read-back of the effective setting. An effective delay change recalculates due times for unfulfilled reminders under R-1.a. | On the supported billing control surface, compare replies and read-back from two authorized views and a denied principal. Verify persistence after restart and no dispatch while initially disabled. |
| R-2.b | The authorized operator can pause and resume reminders for one invoice. Account disable suppresses reminders for every invoice in that account. A later enable preserves per-invoice pauses. | Unauthorized or stale changes leave the effective controls and dispatch effects unchanged. A reminder cannot be committed when current controls or sender authority cannot be verified. | The latest committed control before dispatch commitment governs. Disabling or pausing suppresses selected but uncommitted work and its retries. An already committed external dispatch may finish. Resume or re-enable re-evaluates current eligibility without resetting fulfilled or uncertain reminders. | Observe the control read-back and delivery submissions across pause, disable, enable and resume interleavings for two invoices. **State and lifecycle.** Billing administration owns the account enablement and delay and the invoice pause flag, scoped to their authoritative account and invoice identities. Authorized operators can read and change controls only in scope. The processor reads effective controls with its own grant. There is no customer visibility into these controls. Invoice pause begins clear and ceases to apply when the invoice is removed. Account settings cease to apply when the account is removed or its sender authority withdrawn, with purge governed by R-5. The supported control surface shows pending, confirmed, denied, conflicting and unknown outcomes. It reads current values on re-entry after a stale or unknown result.

**Open gaps.** Billing owner must identify the actual control entry point, account and principal identifiers, grant/revocation contract and supported maximum delay before control integration. Release owner must verify the authorized unattended identity and ability to turn off dispatch before activation. No initial enablement is implied for any real account by this spec.

### R-3 — Run reminders automatically and recover safely

**Basis and delta.** 2026-11-21 request, § The incoming request, “automatic reminders.” Add unattended, recoverable processing and one fulfillment per account/invoice reminder. Preserve independent invoice activity and manual sends. No processing runtime, historical delivery correlation or delivery idempotency capability was supplied. Pending implementation and recovery checks.

| Acceptance line | Positive expectation | Failure expectation | Boundary expectation | Surface and confirmation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| R-3.a | While enabled, processing repeatedly discovers due invoices and advances each eligible reminder without an active user session. Once capacity or a dependency returns, overdue unfulfilled work is reconsidered from current state. | Failed evaluation, interrupted work or capacity limits leave unfinished work recoverable and observable. Dependency failure cannot cause dispatch using unverifiable facts. | Empty runs submit nothing. Overlapping or repeated runs, and late, duplicate or reordered source signals, never earn an additional reminder. An old run cannot replace a newer outcome. | On the target runtime, sign out, advance past a due date, interrupt and resume processing and repeat triggers. Compare operator evidence and actual accepted delivery counts per invoice, including records beyond a single processing unit. |
| R-3.b | Once a reminder has been durably accepted by the delivery authority, retain its fulfilled identity across retries, restarts, date edits, state changes and control toggles. Reconcile interruptions between external acceptance and local confirmation against the original attempt. | If delivery acceptance is uncertain, expose that state and hold the affected reminder. Retry only when authoritative evidence establishes non-acceptance or verified same-operation replay ensures no additional message. Retryable confirmed failures recheck all guards. Permanent failures and exhausted retries require correction or definitive reconciliation before any further attempt. | Simultaneous claims for the same account and invoice allow one dispatch commitment for that attempt. Late or repeated results apply only to their matching attempt and cannot regress confirmed fulfillment. | Force concurrency and interruptions before dispatch and after acceptance before local confirmation. Observe the provider's accepted submissions and confirmed product history for the original identity, then run duplicates and show the count remains one. |

**State and lifecycle.** Reminder processing owns the logical reminder identity (account and invoice for the single automatic reminder) and tracks attempt identity separately. Durable outcomes are **pending** (recorded, eligible to be considered), **dispatch committed** (one allowed attempt, acceptance outstanding), **retry waiting** (confirmed transient non-acceptance), **acceptance unknown** (may already have been accepted), **accepted** (delivery authority accepted responsibility), and **stopped** (permanent failure, exhaustion or no longer actionable). Ineligibility and pauses are evaluation outcomes, independent of fulfillment. An uncommitted reminder can wait or stop according to whether its invoice can become eligible again. Unknown outcomes reconcile from the original attempt; after restart, absence of an acknowledgment leaves the outcome unknown. Only authoritative matching evidence advances or resolves it. Accepted fulfillment is terminal for automatic sending even if transport later reports a bounce. Retain fulfillment or an equivalent suppression fact for as long as the same invoice can qualify, including across compatible rollback and re-enable. If that fact cannot lawfully or safely be retained, hold automatic dispatch for that invoice until non-duplication can be established. Scoped operators can see the outcome across sessions under R-5.

**Open gaps.** Runtime and delivery owners must establish independent execution, recovery/read-back capability, overlap and interruption behavior, retry count, backoff, timeout, retry deadline, run cadence and maximum catch-up delay. The release owner must reconcile existing reminder producers and historical sends before enabling affected accounts. An unavailable history or ambiguous external result supplies no permission to resend. The ongoing duplicate-suppression contract and its verification can be planned independently of the eventual cadence and limits.

| R-4.a | At dispatch commitment, send for the intended account and invoice to the invoice's current authorized billing destination through the approved billing sender. The message identifies the biller and invoice reference, states the amount outstanding and currency and the due date, and asks the recipient to review or pay through the existing billing route. | Missing, invalid, ambiguous or disallowed destination, untrusted sender authority or unavailable required message facts prevent dispatch and yield an operator reason. | A contact change or communication restriction effective before commitment governs the destination. **State and lifecycle.** Billing owns the recipient and restrictions. Delivery receives only the routing and message data needed for the attempt. External messages already accepted may persist.

**Open gaps.** Billing and delivery owners must establish recipient precedence for multiple contacts, permitted sender and invoice access route, contact restrictions and message policy, channel acceptance/failure semantics, and allowed retention and deletion before real delivery. Where authoritative ordering of a payment, contact or control change against dispatch commitment cannot be demonstrated, affected sends stay disabled pending resolution. No partner API, callback or provider guarantee was observed.

## Flow and transitions

The legal order is **evaluate → authorize commitment → request external delivery → read back outcome**. Evaluation alone grants no permission to send. Dispatch commitment is the point at which an attempt is authorized to cross the delivery boundary without further cancellation. An abandoned provisional selection is still subject to all eligibility and authorization checks. The selected runtime must demonstrate how that point is ordered against effective billing and control changes before activation.

| Flow | Trigger, ordered interactions and outcome |
| --- | --- |
| F-1 · R-2, R-5 | Operator reads effective controls, submits an account-scoped change, then sees its durable result or reads back after an uncertain response (T-1). The unattended processor uses effective settings after the operator leaves. |
| F-2 · R-1 through R-3 | Independent trigger evaluates the account and invoices against current source, time and controls (T-2). A selected item advances only if still authorized and due and its reminder identity is available (T-3). Empty, interrupted and skipped work is visible via R-5. |
| F-3 · R-3, R-4 | The authorized processor uses the committed attempt to request delivery. Matching acceptance, classified rejection or uncertain completion is recorded (T-4). Recovery reads back or safely replays the same attempt, or on verified non-acceptance uses a newly authorized attempt if permitted (T-5). Subsequent transport outcomes update status only (T-6). |
| F-4 · R-2, R-3, R-5 | The operator inspects scoped outcomes, disables automation or pauses one invoice (T-1). The release operator verifies containment, preserves business data and unresolved or fulfilled reminder identities, and may restore compatible processing (T-7). |

| Transition | Legal start and guard | Result and recovery | Acceptance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T-1 · Effective control change | Existing scoped account or invoice, current billing grant and valid change against the effective control revision. | Commit the new setting or retain the previous one on rejection. An identical repeat against the effective value is a no-op for dispatch. Re-read current settings after a lost response. | R-2.a, R-2.b |
| T-2 · Evaluate | Unattended trigger in authorized scope, current clock and source facts. | Eligible unfulfilled work becomes or remains pending. Suppress ineligible items with reasons. Scope evaluation failures and reconsider when authority is restored. | R-1.a–c, R-3.a, R-5.a |
| T-3 · Commit attempt | Pending or retry waiting when its retry is due, complete current facts, due and enabled, currently authorized, unpaused, permitted destination, unfulfilled identity, no active or unresolved attempt. | Single attempt becomes dispatch committed. A losing concurrent trigger or a disqualifying change before commitment yields no submission. Work interrupted before commitment is reconsidered. | R-1.b, R-2.b, R-3.b, R-4.a |
| T-4 · Classify attempt | Dispatch committed, matched to the original attempt. | Verified acceptance becomes accepted. Verified transient non-acceptance becomes retry waiting unless exhausted. Permanent non-acceptance or exhaustion becomes stopped. Abandoned attempt or indefinite acknowledgment at the applicable timeout becomes acceptance unknown. | R-3.b, R-4.b |
| T-5 · Recover attempt | Acceptance unknown or retry waiting. A stopped attempt can re-enter pending after an authorized correction only when definitive non-acceptance and the effective retry policy permit it. | Matching evidence resolves unknown to accepted, retry waiting or stopped as appropriate. With verified replay protection, the same operation may be replayed subject to current dispatch guards. Otherwise unknown stays on hold. A new attempt returns through T-3. | R-3.a, R-3.b, R-5.a |
| T-6 · Record transport result | Authenticated, matched transport result for the original attempt. | Apply a substantiated newer result to transport status. Repeated, stale, unmatched or untrusted results cannot change fulfillment or authorize another send. | R-4.b, R-5.a |
| T-7 · Stop, revert or restore | Authorized release operator and verified affected scope. | Stop new commitments, allow reconciliation of prior attempts, preserve billing data and existing actions. Restore only with effective controls and suppression state accounted for. | R-5.b |

Incremental remote writes to invoices, a client-side invoice replica, and recipient interaction with processing status are inapplicable because this feature evaluates existing billing facts and sends a reminder. No separate subscription, cache, notification service or provider callback is assumed.
