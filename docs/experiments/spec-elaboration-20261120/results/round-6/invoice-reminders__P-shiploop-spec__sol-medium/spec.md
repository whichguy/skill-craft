# Invoice Billing

## Scope and sources

**Request (2026-11-21, “The incoming request”).** “Set up automatic reminders for unpaid invoices.”

The direct outcome is that an eligible unpaid invoice prompts the billed customer automatically, without a person sending each message. Correct targeting, suppression and recovery constrain that outcome. The billing authority continues to own invoice issuance, balances, payments, customer contacts and access. Collections escalation, late fees, payment collection, customer replies, and a new settings screen or dashboard are outside this request.

**Evidence.** This standalone stage was supplied with the request and the guides indexed in [GUIDES/README.md](../GUIDES/README.md). No earlier specification, project repository, discovery or research result, selected baseline, parity reference, or runtime target was supplied. Existing invoice and reminder behavior is unknown. The sections below define proposed behavior, with evidence limited to the request and the specification review. The guides explain how to model and verify the behavior and supply no billing policy.

**Actors and boundaries.** An authorized billing operator controls activation for the intended billing scope and can inspect scoped operational outcomes. The invoice authority owns current billing and recipient facts. The reminder runtime initiates scheduled work under scoped service authority and hands off eligible reminders to the approved outbound channel. The customer observes the result at the approved billing destination. These are logical roles. Their actual product surfaces and whether they share a runtime are open.

## Requirements

### R-1 — Run reminders automatically under an authorized policy

**Basis and delta.** 2026-11-21 request, “The incoming request,” specifically “Set up automatic reminders.” Introduces unattended evaluation under a scoped policy. Preserve unrelated billing settings and existing permission rules when identified. There is no baseline to establish how activation or scheduling works today. Implementation and runtime checks for F-1 and the acceptance lines below are pending.

| Acceptance | Positive expectation | Failure expectation | Boundary expectation | Consumer surface and confirmation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| R-1.a Activate or stop | An authorized billing operator can establish an active reminder policy for a specific billing scope through the supported configuration surface and can disable it there. Read-back reflects the effective state. | An unauthorized, out-of-scope or invalid change leaves the effective policy unchanged and reports denial or validation failure without revealing another scope’s state. An unconfirmed write is never represented as effective activation. | A scope with no confirmed active policy sends nothing. A disable committed before final dispatch authorization prevents that handoff, including previously selected work. | Effective policy read-back and outbound observations. Confirm allowed, denied, invalid and disable cases with the selected authority (TC-1, TC-5). |
| R-1.b Evaluate without a person | While active, scheduled evaluation finds eligible work without an open client session. After an interrupted or missed evaluation, a subsequent successful evaluation considers still-eligible invoices under the current policy. | If the policy or invoice authority cannot be established, the affected work makes no outbound handoff and the scoped operator can distinguish a blocked evaluation from an empty result. | Overlapping or repeated evaluations cannot multiply reminders. Completed work stays subject to R-4. A scope with no eligible invoices has zero handoffs and a successful evaluation. | Deployed schedule and scoped run outcomes, correlated with outbound observations. **State, data and recovery.** The billing configuration authority owns the effective policy keyed by billing scope. Activation begins from inactive when no effective policy exists. Changes affect only the selected scope. Authorized billing operators may read and change their scope and the runtime may read only the scopes it is authorized to handle. Use the latest committed authorized policy at final dispatch authorization. Stale conflicting edits cannot undo a newer committed change without a fresh authorized decision. Disable ends future authorization, including pending work, but an already handed-off message cannot be recalled by disabling. Reactivation re-evaluates future work from current facts and preserves reminder history for deduplication. Remove or expire obsolete policy records under the billing scope’s approved lifecycle. Verify actual role, read-back, concurrency, scope isolation and background recovery.

**Configuration and scheduling boundary contract.** The billing configuration authority owns effective policy and the scheduler supplies evaluation triggers. A policy edit changes only the authorized scope and fields. Read effective policy back after an unconfirmed result. Evaluate all still-eligible work after missed or duplicate triggers and use the current policy and runtime access at dispatch. The runtime owns blocked and completed evaluation dispositions. A trigger acknowledges initiation only. A streaming policy feed and external completion callback are inapplicable because neither is required.

**Domain rules and open gaps.** Proposed default is disabled until confirmed activation. For an activated scope the default is one reminder per invoice after its due cutoff, at the first successful eligible evaluation, including eligible invoices already overdue at activation after the operator confirms that cohort. Product owner must confirm production timing and timezone semantics, cohort and any additional cadence or quiet-hour rules before enabling the affected scope. Billing integration owner must identify the configuration route, actor roles, unattended execution, recovery path and applicable scheduling limits during discovery/planning. Existing automation and historical sends must be checked before activation to avoid duplicate reminders. Conditional logic and isolated examples can proceed while production scheduling and activation remain unready.

Preserve the invoice authority’s status, balance, due date, ownership and contact rules. Existing field meanings and consistency guarantees were not supplied. Implementation and checks for F-2 are pending.

Partial payment leaves it eligible if all conditions still hold. | Missing, inconsistent or unavailable facts that prevent eligibility or authority from being established defer sending and yield a scoped reason. A confirmed paid, draft, void, deleted, held from collection or out-of-scope invoice yields zero handoffs. An out-of-scope reference cannot disclose record existence to an unauthorized caller. | Exactly at the cutoff is ineligible. The first evaluation after it can qualify. Zero balance is ineligible. | R-2.b Recheck at dispatch | Authorize each handoff using fresh effective policy, current service access, invoice eligibility, and the current billing destination. **State, data and recovery.** The invoice authority owns the scoped invoice identity, current status, outstanding balance, due cutoff, collection hold, and authorized billing recipient. Existing authorized billing actors and payment processes remain the writers. The reminder runtime reads the minimum needed for the evaluated invoice. Before and after, invoice and contact records are changed only by existing billing operations. A selection or stored work candidate is a hint, subordinate to the latest authorized state at final authorization. Payment and contact changes that commit before that point govern the send. Source access failure waits for a subsequent authorized evaluation. In-memory invoice and recipient details expire at the end of the work attempt. Billing records follow their existing lifecycle. Verify balance/status changes, deletion, revocation without an invoice edit and destination replacement at the source boundary.

**Invoice boundary contract.** Billing is the authority for invoice eligibility inputs, contact approval and scope. Scans may use incremental selection if subsequent evaluations recover missed changes and never lose still-eligible work. Current authorization and billing facts govern dispatch regardless of selection age. The reminder path reads invoice state and has no invoice write or asynchronous invoice completion to reconcile. It owns sanitized eligibility, suppression and source-failure dispositions. A separate cache or subscription contract is inapplicable because neither is required.

**Domain rules and open gaps.** Proposed defaults treat partial payment with a remaining collectible balance as unpaid, exclude invoices on collection hold, and begin only after the due cutoff. The product owner must settle the treatment of disputes, payment-in-flight, credits and any other exceptions the source exposes before eligibility for those states is ready. The billing integration owner must confirm how authoritative status, deadlines and timezones, positive balance, recipient approval, permission revocation and concurrent changes are represented. A read-only contract inspection and a sandbox check using the actual runtime identity are due in discovery/planning before binding final eligibility to the source. A source unable to establish current eligibility or permission leaves that scope’s live sending unready.

The customer can identify the invoice by its customer-facing reference and understand that payment is due. | If the message cannot be composed safely, the destination is invalid, or the outbound service rejects the handoff, expose a scoped failure and follow R-4. **State, data and recovery.** The invoice authority retains the billing address. The runtime may use it and the invoice reference transiently to compose and submit the scoped message. Only the approved outbound service and billing recipient receive the message, with the existing authorized channel operators able to inspect it under that channel’s policy. A recipient change follows R-2.b. Store no additional address or message-body copy in reminder history or logs. Channel-side address, message and receipt data follow the approved retention/deletion policy. The recipient controls copies already delivered to their inbox. An invalid or rejected handoff follows R-4. Verify the real sender, destination, body and status against channel evidence rather than treating an internal success entry as receipt.

**Delivery boundary contract.** The reminder runtime owns send intent and authorization. The approved outbound channel owns accept/reject and any later delivery facts. Submit under the authorized service sender with fresh policy and destination. Correlate responses to the exact attempt and invoice scope without leaking addresses in diagnostics. Reordered or duplicate reports may update only their own attempt with validated evidence and cannot reverse confirmed acceptance or create a send. Recovery and the treatment of ambiguous results follow R-4. An inbound customer reply flow is inapplicable to automatic reminders.

**Domain rules and open gaps.** Email is the working channel default, subject to an approved sender, recipient mapping, preferences and required content. The product owner and channel owner must establish these and any consent, locale or communication restrictions before sending live messages. Channel discovery/planning must demonstrate isolated receipt, rejection semantics, and whether delivery/bounce evidence is available. Recipient receipt is due at product acceptance. Provider support for channel milestones and the exact sender, syntax and copy for production remain open. Pure message and eligibility behavior can be reviewed against fixtures.

Introduces recovery and duplicate prevention so automation cannot multiply messages or abandon known incomplete work silently. Preserve existing operational permissions and unrelated events when found. No persistence, outbound result lookup or observability capability has been established. Implementation and checks for F-3 are pending.

| Acceptance | Positive expectation | Failure expectation | Boundary expectation | Consumer surface and confirmation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| R-4.a Enforce one accepted reminder under the default policy | Keep one logical reminder identity per billing scope and stable invoice identity. Record attempt ownership durably before external handoff. Concurrent evaluations, duplicate triggers and restart converge on that identity. | If durable ownership cannot be established, no send starts. An abandoned attempt whose handoff cannot be ruled out goes to unknown for reconciliation. Stale actors or reports cannot create a second handoff or overwrite a newer attempt’s result. | At most one accepted handoff for the logical reminder, including reactivation or later invoice edits. Conclusive proof of nonacceptance plus no possible late handoff permits recovery. Without either, the operator can see an unresolved outcome and no new attempt can start. | A crash after handoff but before recording its result is an ambiguous outcome. The operator can recover the result from authoritative state once its read path is available. | Duplicate reports have no additional business effect. Diagnostic output contains no address, raw message, raw channel error, or credentials. | Existing authorized operating surface and diagnostic retrieval. **State, data and recovery.** The reminder runtime owns durable state scoped by billing scope and invoice identity. Individual attempts and channel receipts are distinct from that logical identity. Only the authorized runtime updates attempt outcomes. A scoped operator may read them and authorize recovery through the supported operating path, without overriding current eligibility or forcing an unsafe retry. Store the minimum identifiers, decision/outcome, timestamps and safe correlation needed for restart, deduplication and investigation. Retain suppression of repeats while earlier accepted invoices can still qualify. Before purge, the scope lifecycle must make affected identities permanently ineligible or otherwise preserve the no-repeat guarantee. Stop new actions on scope closure and expire records and diagnostics under an approved retention/deletion policy. Reconciliation can settle a historical attempt without authorizing new work. Verify restart, overlapping work, no unsafe repeat after purge, revocation, and cleanup.

**Operational boundary contract.** The runtime’s durable state is authoritative for processing and final scoped status. Use currently authorized reads for operator access. Updates are scoped to the original logical reminder and matching attempt, and duplicate or stale results preserve newer state. Processing can continue and recover with the operator absent. Owned diagnostic coverage includes blocked evaluation, suppression, accepted handoff, known failure, unknown handoff and reconciliation. Verify authorized retrieval and sink failure. No new login flow, event transport, or push updates are required.

**Domain rules and open gaps.** Product owner and operations owner must settle retry deadline/backoff and the handling of invoices reopened after payment if a further reminder is desired. The default allows no further accepted reminder for a reopened invoice with the same identity. Before implementation of live dispatch and recovery, the billing and channel owners must establish scope-stable invoice identity, durability/concurrency, safe recovery evidence, and the actual authorized operating surface and retention rules. If channel lookup cannot prove nonacceptance, an ambiguous attempt remains held without resending. If the runtime cannot guarantee exclusive authorization, durable ownership and safe handoff recovery, production dispatch remains unready. Review and conditional checks of the model can proceed.

## Behavior model and case mapping

The flow and transition labels below are references within the requirements. Attempt outcomes live under one logical reminder identity. “No attempt” can mean new work or an invoice previously suppressed. “Owned” means a durable exclusive attempt exists. “Unknown” means a handoff may have happened. “Retryable” means the earlier attempt cannot be accepted. “Held” means a known failure requires authorized remediation or retries are exhausted. “Accepted” is terminal for sending under the default. Eligibility and policy are independent of these outcomes. A blocked or suppressed evaluation alone cannot clear an accepted or unknown result.

| Flow | Ordered interaction and outcome |
| --- | --- |
| F-1 / R-1 | An authorized operator changes scope policy and verifies effective read-back (T-1). The schedule independently initiates evaluation (T-2), even after an interrupted evaluation. Inactive, blocked and successful empty runs have different dispositions. |
| F-2 / R-2, R-3 | A scoped evaluation produces eligible candidates (T-2). The runtime establishes durable attempt ownership (T-3). It then revalidates and either suppresses the candidate or sends a scoped notice to the authorized destination (T-4). |
| F-3 / R-3, R-4 | The channel reports an outcome, or the runtime detects an interrupted or ambiguous attempt (T-5). Recovery reconciles evidence and, where allowed, returns to fresh authorization for another attempt (T-6). An authorized operator can retrieve the result (T-7). |

| Transition | Guard, change and recovery | Observable check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| T-1 / F-1 / R-1.a | The configuration authority accepts a valid scoped change from an authorized actor based on the current policy. Commit before confirming. Denied, invalid and stale changes have no effect. When commit is uncertain, read back from the authority before confirmation. | TC-1, TC-5 |
| T-2 / F-1, F-2 / R-1.b, R-2.a | The schedule evaluates the active scope from authorized source facts. Emit candidates or a successful empty result. Ineligible and indeterminate input produce no candidate for that invoice. On interrupted or blocked evaluation, a later successful evaluation reconsiders still-eligible work. | TC-2, TC-3, TC-6 |
| T-3 / F-2, F-3 / R-4.a | For an eligible candidate with no attempt or a permitted retry, the runtime commits one owner for the new attempt. A competing, stale or unauthorized actor cannot acquire ownership for the same work. Failed or unconfirmed ownership permits no handoff. An abandoned attempt moves through T-5. | TC-4, TC-6, TC-7 |
| T-4 / F-2 / R-2.b, R-3.a | The current attempt owner rechecks all guards and uses the approved destination and invoice reference from that authorization. If any guard fails or safe composition fails, there is no submission. End the owned attempt as retryable when a failed guard conclusively prevented handoff. A known permanent composition failure becomes held. If the absence of a handoff cannot be established, follow T-5. An independently authorized later attempt still requires T-3 and T-4.
