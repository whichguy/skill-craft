# Grok Bot for Salesforce

**Basis.** 2026-11-20, Incoming request § The incoming request: “Create a Grok bot in a Salesforce development org.” This is a first specification. There is no accepted product spec, selected baseline, repository, discovery result or research result. Target-org behavior and existing assets are unknown. The primary documentation reviewed here establishes supported contracts and limits, with no observation of the target or its entitlements.

**Scope interpretation.** An authorized user opens a text chat inside one Salesforce development org and receives an answer generated by xAI Grok. Follow-ups use the current open conversation. The product uses the existing Salesforce identity. The operator is the Salesforce administrator authorized to configure the bot and assign access. The xAI account owner controls provider entitlement, spending and credentials. “Grok bot” is interpreted as a Grok-powered assistant; no parity with another Grok interface or named packaged product was requested. Production, public access, external chat channels, CRM grounding or actions, provider tools, attachments, voice, scheduled work and shared or saved conversations are outside scope.

## Requirements

### R-1 — Find and open the bot in Salesforce

**Basis and delta.** The 2026-11-20 request (§ The incoming request), “in a Salesforce development org,” establishes the in-org entry. New behavior is a discoverable bot for authorized development-org users. Preserve unrelated navigation, permissions, components and records. See SF-1, SF-2 and the Salesforce guide’s “Consumer entry access” section. The target, deployed identity, role and route are unobserved. Deployment and browser checks are pending.

- **R-1.a — Entry.** Positive: an authorized user finds **Grok Bot** through Salesforce navigation and opens the authenticated chat with an empty conversation and available composer when enabled. Failure: a hidden, missing or misdirected destination fails acceptance even if the bot’s assets are deployed. Boundary: the first visit and a fresh visit both open an empty conversation. Surface and confirmation: from the selected Lightning app/navigation, follow the delivered route and verify org, role, destination and usable composer in the target browser (C-01).
- **R-1.b — Access.** Positive: a signed-in user with the bot grant can open and submit, subject to runtime gates in R-4. Failure: anonymous, signed-out or ungranted users receive Salesforce sign-in or an access refusal and cannot invoke Grok through a direct entry or a runtime request. Boundary: visibility alone grants no invocation authority. Surface and confirmation: use the allowed and denied personas on both browser and actual runtime entry, with outbound-call evidence for denial (C-02).
- **R-1.c — Development delivery.** Positive: the intended revision is present and the intended user can reach it in the identified development org. Failure: unresolved target, namespace collision, failed or partial deployment, mismatched revision, or missing visibility leaves delivery unverified. Boundary: the operation is confined to bot-owned resources and explicitly scoped grants in that development target. Surface and confirmation: compare pre-change inventory with terminal operation status, revision-specific read-back, effective grants and the browser result; verify unrelated resources in the touched slice are preserved (C-01, C-11).

**State and data.** Target is one development org, with immutable org ID, edition, accessible Lightning app, bot entry identity, deployed revision and effective user grants recorded before acceptance. Org setup owns these resources. Before rollout, identify any existing names, owners and effective grants. After rollout, the new entry resolves to the intended revision and unrelated user access stays intact. Leave the bot disabled if setup is partial; reconcile actual state before retry, restore scoped changes under R-4. Org home or metadata presence alone supplies neither entry nor revision evidence.

**Open gaps.** Org owner must identify the unique org and intended roles, and establish authorized read, metadata, configuration and grant routes at prepare. Developer checks edition, API version, namespace, app/tab availability and collisions before selecting metadata identities. Operator establishes the exact navigation entry and assigned grants before release-check. Ownership of any colliding resource must be resolved before changing it. These gaps gate target-bound work.

### R-2 — Submit text and receive a Grok answer

**Basis and delta.** The 2026-11-20 request (§ The incoming request), “Create a Grok bot,” calls for real Grok inference. The proposed text interaction and its error behavior are new. Existing Salesforce data, permissions and automation are preserved; the bot performs no business-record queries or mutations. See XA-1 through XA-4, SF-2 and SF-4. Provider availability and quota for the intended team are unverified. Runtime implementation and checks are pending.

- **R-2.a — Valid submission.** Positive: the enabled, granted user submits a nonblank text prompt within the configured bounds. The bot sends the text and any permitted context from R-3 to the configured Grok model and displays the completed assistant text under that prompt. Failure: absent, null, non-text or whitespace-only input is rejected before dispatch with actionable validation and no phantom response. Boundary: accept input exactly at the effective UTF-8 byte limit, including supported Unicode; reject over-limit input before dispatch and preserve the draft. Surface and confirmation: browser plus server/controlled provider request evidence for exact input, configured model and returned text. Verify two distinct synthetic prompts against live Grok with correlated provider results (C-03, C-04).
- **R-2.b — Failure and refusal.** Positive: a definitive, user-visible outcome ends pending and lets the user recover. Failure: distinguish invalid input/context, unavailable access/configuration (including provider 401/403 or unavailable configured model), rate/quota or exhausted provider credit, connectivity/timeout, provider outage, explicit refusal, and invalid output (unexpected status, malformed, empty or incomplete response). Render a safe message and appropriate next action for each group; show a refusal as a refusal. Preserve the failed draft and prior completed pairs. Boundary: a transport timeout leaves upstream completion and cost unknown; do not auto-replay or show partial output as success. Surface and confirmation: browser, controlled provider and runtime diagnostics; compare HTTP/status fixtures and subsequent dispatch counts, and verify that a deliberate retry is a new attempt (C-05).
- **R-2.c — Text and data boundary.** Positive: the provider receives only configured inference parameters and the text entered or generated in this chat as allowed by R-3. Failure: HTML/script content is displayed as inert text; provider instructions, URLs and output never cause Salesforce reads, writes or tool use. Boundary: exclude attachments, Salesforce records and org/user identifiers from inference input; keep credentials, reasoning traces and raw provider errors outside browser payloads. Surface and confirmation: inspect the outbound body and browser response and use hostile-looking literal output with sentinel record/secret fixtures (C-06).

**State and data.** A send targets the configured xAI team/model through the authorized org-side invocation. Request text is processed transiently in Salesforce; pending request correlation belongs to its conversation and attempt. Before an admitted send, there is no provider work for that attempt. After it, one dispatch at most for that admitted invocation, with a single matching terminal UI outcome. Duplicate UI activation is suppressed in the active view; the bot neither retries transport nor assumes exactly-once delivery by the provider. Provider owns any submitted data and usage recorded on its side. A retry after uncertain delivery can incur a second charge. Free-form input is for synthetic, non-sensitive development data only; the composer tells users that text goes to xAI and to exclude personal, CRM and secret data. There is no promise to detect all such text.

**Open gaps.** xAI owner supplies a development team/project, active inference entitlement, model ID, key provisioning, billing/limits and approved provider data handling before live integration. Developer establishes numeric input, context, output and wait bounds before bounded integration work, using R-2.a, R-3 and the non-functional assessment. No model alias, budget or quota is assumed.

### R-3 — Keep one isolated conversation while the chat is open

**Basis and delta.** The 2026-11-20 request (§ The incoming request), “Grok bot,” supports a conversational default. New behavior is short-lived follow-up context and per-page state; preservation covers existing Salesforce records and users’ independent sessions. See SF-2, XA-1 and XA-4. No consumer implementation, runtime storage behavior or overlap has been observed. State and browser checks are pending.

- **R-3.a — Context and lifetime.** Positive: include the ordered successful user/assistant pairs from this conversation plus the new user prompt in a follow-up. Failure: failed, refused, superseded and pending turns never become context. Boundary: a fresh page, reload, leaving the bot, logout or **New conversation** discards local content; a different page or user starts empty. If total context exceeds the bound, explain the limit and let the user edit or start fresh; never silently drop older pairs. Surface and confirmation: browser and captured request bodies across two turns, reset, reload and independent users/pages (C-07, C-09).
- **R-3.b — Pending and repeated actions.** Positive: show a waiting state for the current prompt and allow only one active attempt per open conversation. On matching success commit the prompt/answer pair once. Failure: repeated submit while pending causes no additional dispatch; unmatched or duplicated completion causes no mutation. Boundary: a deliberate later submission of identical text after the first finishes is a new turn. Different conversations can proceed independently within shared provider limits. Surface and confirmation: exercise duplicate input and delayed, duplicate and reversed completions through controlled browser/runtime observations (C-07, C-08).
- **R-3.c — Reset or interruption.** Positive: reset clears the draft, transcript and status immediately, invalidates that conversation’s pending turn and permits a new prompt. Failure: disconnection during a send ends in an error at the applicable deadline if the view survives; reconnect enables a manual retry and does not resume or fetch the old result. Boundary: a previously dispatched request can still finish or incur cost after reset, timeout or navigation; it cannot change the new view or be presented as canceled upstream. Surface and confirmation: browser with a controlled delayed provider and a dispatch observer (C-08).

**State and data.** View owner is the signed-in person in the current open instance. Its transient local state comprises draft, ordered completed pairs, pending attempt, error status and view theme. No other person’s conversation is delivered to the client. The theme is view-only and resets to the prescribed starting appearance on a fresh load. The Salesforce side holds only what is needed during a call; no Salesforce conversation records, shared client store, durable job, event replay, or cache of prompts/replies. The provider’s separately governed retention is addressed below. Reset and exit remove local references, not provider copies. Concurrency scope is one active attempt per view, with distinct conversation and attempt identities; section “Behavior model” defines commit and invalidation precedence.

**Open gaps.** Developer supplies the combined context and state envelope and checks cross-user isolation, failed-turn exclusion and page/session termination in the selected Salesforce shell before integration acceptance.

### R-4 — Configure, restrict, diagnose and recover the bot

**Basis and delta.** The 2026-11-20 request (§ The incoming request), “in a Salesforce development org,” implies operability in that org. Proposed controls cover provider configuration, activation, scoped user access, diagnosis and restoring a known-good bot. Preserve unrelated org settings, permissions, automation and records. See SF-2 through SF-5 and XA-2 through XA-4. Setup authority, prior revision, credentials, observability and recovery route are unobserved. Operator implementation and checks are pending.

- **R-4.a — Configure and activate.** Positive: the org operator sets the permitted endpoint/model binding, credentials through approved secure provisioning, and bot access; checks the settings and confirms a real authorized synthetic send before declaring it ready. Failure: disabled, unset or invalid configuration fails closed and exposes a safe unavailable status. Boundary: the initial deployment starts disabled, and bot users cannot change configuration, access or keys. Surface and confirmation: read back non-secret setup, compare scoped grants, and observe a real authorized prompt after enabling in the identified org (C-10).
- **R-4.b — Stop and revoke.** Positive: operator can stop the bot for all users or revoke one user’s grant, and the xAI owner can revoke a key at the provider. Failure: recheck authority at admission and before releasing a reply; if bot access, enable state or the admitted configuration revision is no longer valid at the reply check, suppress that output. Boundary: decisions use the authoritative state effective at each check. A call already dispatched may incur cost, and delivered answers cannot be recalled. Provider key revocation applies when xAI enforces it; provider rejection follows R-2.b. Surface and confirmation: controlled slow call and live permission checks using two users, on/off, revocation, stale open page and a direct attempt (C-02, C-10).
- **R-4.c — Diagnose.** Positive: an authorized operator can tie a failed or completed request to its safe outcome. Required categories are access refusal, configuration refusal, input/context rejection, dispatch, successful completion, model refusal, throttling/billing, provider auth, timeout/connectivity, other provider error, malformed/incomplete output and result suppression. Record only opaque attempt reference, time, outcome category, status class when available and elapsed time when applicable. Failure: diagnostic access denied to ordinary consumers; diagnostic sink failure neither leaks sensitive data nor converts a failure into a success nor repeats provider work. Boundary: no prompts, answers, secrets, usernames, raw headers or provider bodies in bot diagnostics. Surface and confirmation: trusted runtime observation and operator access plus redaction, category and simulated sink-failure checks (C-05, C-06, C-10).
- **R-4.d — Recover and restore.** Positive: on a bad change, stop, reconcile actual config and deployment, restore the last known working bot revision/configuration when one exists, and verify authorized and denied access plus a synthetic send before declaring recovered. For first delivery with no known-good revision, keep the bot disabled and undo only isolated bot-owned changes when authorized. Failure: failed restoration leaves it disabled and identifies the incomplete step for the operator. Boundary: prior key material is never restored from source or logs; provision a valid replacement if required. No conversation migration or restoration is needed. Surface and confirmation: controlled config failure and restoration in the identified development org with before/after inventory and terminal status (C-11).

**State and data.** Salesforce Setup owns enablement, active non-secret configuration revision and per-user permission assignments; only authorized operators may change them. Operator changes take effect when applied by the authoritative service; if concurrent setups conflict, read back the effective revision/grants before proceeding. A configuration change supersedes admitted replies tied to its older revision, including an off/on change before completion. Secrets belong to the xAI owner and the protected Salesforce runtime credential binding; only approved provisioning and runtime use are allowed. Rotate by provisioning the replacement and verifying it, then revoking the old key; for compromise, revoke and disable first. Remove the Salesforce binding and revoke the xAI key on decommission. Keep diagnostic records in an operator-only platform facility, under its confirmed retention and removal controls. Recovery reuses valid current credentials and the existing Salesforce data.

**Open gaps.** Org operator and xAI owner supply effective grants for metadata, configuration, assignment and credential provisioning separately, and establish the approved disable/rotate/restore routes before release-plan. Developer must establish the runtime’s fresh authorization/configuration check behavior, including propagation and delayed return, before accepting live revocation, and confirm the diagnostic sink, reader, retention and removal before enablement. The xAI owner confirms actual provider retention and authorized scope before any real send.

## Behavior model

**State inventory.** Org configuration is durable and operator-owned, initially disabled; effective states are disabled, enabled and ready to attempt inference, and unavailable due to absent/invalid setup (which fails closed). A user is granted or denied by current Salesforce authority. A view is initially empty; completed pairs stay readable until cleared. A draft is empty, editable, pending or retained on failure. Each attempt is pending, committed, failed or superseded. Only a pending, matching, still-authorized attempt can commit. Terminal attempts cannot commit again. View-local states are transient. Details and lifetimes are in R-1 through R-4.

| Flow | Permitted transitions and observable result | Verification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| F-1 — Discover and enter (R-1) | **T-01.** From the identified org, signed-in granted user follows the supported entry. Granted and enabled yields empty, ready chat; granted but disabled yields unavailable; signed-out or denied cannot submit. | C-01, C-02 |
| F-2 — Ask or follow up (R-2, R-3) | **T-02.** From a ready or recoverable view, validate current authority, configuration, draft and combined context. Reject locally/server-side as appropriate with no outbound send if a gate fails; otherwise bind the attempt and show pending. **T-03.** Runtime dispatches once, waits within deadline and receives a result. Matching, valid, authorized completion appends one pair and clears the submitted draft; matching failure releases pending and retains the draft. A send while pending is ignored. | C-02 through C-08 |
| F-3 — Clear or detach (R-3) | **T-04.** New conversation clears the view and replaces its identity; leaving or logout ends the view. A timeout invalidates the attempt while keeping its draft if the view survives. Late, duplicate or unmatched completions cannot change the resulting view. A later explicit retry or new send uses its own identity. | C-07 through C-09 |
| F-4 — Operate (R-1, R-4) | **T-05.** Operator inventories the development target, deploys disabled, configures and grants only the intended scope, enables and verifies. **T-06.** Disable/revoke stops newly checked admissions, and a change visible at the reply check suppresses a now-disallowed response. **T-07.** On partial setup/bad change, stop, read back, and restore/verify a known working revision or keep the initial bot disabled; provider key revocation or replacement follows R-4. | C-01, C-02, C-10, C-11 |

**Ordering.** In a view, send admission, reset, deadline and result commits take effect in their processed order. A reset or deadline processed before a result invalidates it; a previously committed result is cleared by a subsequent reset. At the Salesforce boundary, the admission check determines whether a request may dispatch, and the final authority check determines whether Salesforce may release an answer. Neither undoes a provider dispatch. Independent users and views share only provider capacity and org configuration.
