# Invoice Reminders

## Basis and scope

On 2026-11-21, under “The incoming request,” the user wrote “Set up automatic reminders for unpaid invoices.” The explicit outcomes are unattended reminder operation and reminders for unpaid invoices. The proposed product defaults below define when an invoice qualifies, whom to notify and how often. An invoice owner’s authorized operator enables the policy for that owner’s scope, the reminder runtime evaluates invoices independently of any user session, and the invoice’s billing contact receives the reminder.

This standalone stage has no earlier spec, repository, selected system baseline, discovery or research result. No existing behavior, external reference system, service capability or parity obligation was observed. The proposed delta is additive at the contract level. No earlier clauses or cases were available to reconcile. The feature reads invoice and payment state without changing billing, collecting payment, creating invoices or replacing manual communication. Scope includes the reminder message and authorized setup and operational outcomes. It does not include a new dashboard, identity system, customer portal or reminder campaign editor.

## Requirements

### R-1 Automatic eligibility and activation

**Basis and delta:** 2026-11-21 user request, “The incoming request,” specifically “Set up automatic” and “unpaid invoices.” Adds owner-scoped activation and time-based evaluation. No prior configuration behavior was available to preserve. Invoice and payment records are read-only to this feature. Implementation and checks are pending.

| Acceptance | Positive | Failure | Boundary | Consumer surface and confirmation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Activation | An authorized operator enables reminders for a defined invoice-owner scope after its source, sender and unattended runtime bindings are valid. The confirmed setting applies without a user session. | An unauthorized, wrong-scope or invalid setup change leaves the effective setting unchanged. A permitted operator sees unavailable or incomplete setup if required bindings cannot be verified. | Repeating the same authorized setting leaves one effective policy. Disabling it prevents new dispatch authorizations from the effective change onward. | Authorized setup read-back and recipient inbox. Confirm effective setting, account isolation and unattended processing in A1. |
| Due evaluation | On each permitted evaluation, consider issued, collectible invoices with a positive outstanding balance at or after the effective due cutoff. Include already-overdue invoices on the first eligible evaluation after activation or recovery. A partial payment that leaves a positive balance still qualifies. | If the authoritative scope, due information, lifecycle state or balance cannot be determined, suppress that invoice for this evaluation and expose the reason to the authorized operator. Reconsider when verified inputs return. | **Default:** first eligible evaluation at or after the due instant. A date-only due date is inclusive in the invoice source’s authoritative billing time zone and the cutoff is the start of the following local day. Before the cutoff, or with zero or negative balance, draft, void, deleted or otherwise non-collectible state, there is no reminder. A missing time zone for a date-only due date is indeterminate. | Operator outcome and recipient inbox. Confirm boundary and mixed outcomes with a controlled clock and changing invoice inputs in A2. |

**State and domain rules:** The invoice source owns the current invoice identity, issuer scope, collectible status, due information and outstanding balance. Use the authoritative balance in invoice currency after recorded payments and credits. Preserve the source’s lifecycle and currency semantics. Authorized billing actors change those records through the source. The unattended reminder identity reads only the scoped data required for evaluation, and the recipient sees only the reminder described in R-2. There is no retained eligibility snapshot that can authorize later dispatch. The policy belongs to the invoice owner, is held by the runtime’s authoritative configuration facility, and is visible and mutable only under current scope permissions. Concurrent setting changes follow committed order; a stale change cannot silently overwrite a newer one. Setup failures keep the previous effective setting. Remove the policy when the scope is decommissioned, after preventing further dispatch for that scope. Losing a particular operator’s permission ends that operator’s control without transferring or expanding the runtime’s authority.

**Open prerequisites:** The product owner or authorized operator must identify the invoice-owner scope and authorize activation under its sender identity before live setup. The implementer must establish the source’s supported lifecycle, balance and due-time mapping and the actual unattended runtime capability before binding evaluation. A valid source mapping may make some fixture states inapplicable in that deployment. The evaluation rule and isolated checks can be designed while these bindings are unresolved.

### R-2 Recipient, message and dispatch eligibility

**Basis and delta:** 2026-11-21 user request, “The incoming request,” specifically “reminders for unpaid invoices.” Adds an outbound reminder for an eligible invoice and guards its use of billing information. No existing reminder channel or recipient behavior was evidenced. Implementation and checks are pending.

| Acceptance | Positive | Failure | Boundary | Consumer surface and confirmation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Recipient | **Default:** address the reminder by email to the single current recipient designated for billing on that invoice. | Missing, invalid, ambiguous or unauthorized billing destination suppresses dispatch and exposes an actionable reason within the invoice owner’s scope. Reconsider on a later evaluation after correction. | Send only to the currently authorized billing destination. A prior address, incidental account contact or other invoice’s contact receives zero copies. | Outbound recipient, authorized outcome and controlled inboxes. Confirm current-address delivery, prior-address silence and invalid-address suppression in A3. |
| Message | **Default:** send a plain-text reminder that identifies the issuer and the invoice’s customer-facing reference, states that payment is outstanding and says to disregard the reminder if already paid. | If a safe issuer name or invoice reference cannot be resolved, suppress dispatch and report the reason to the operator. | Only the selected invoice’s reference appears. The default message omits balances, payment links, attachments and information about other invoices. | Actual received message. Inspect subject, body, sender and envelope in A3. |
| Final eligibility | A current dispatch authorization for the owner and invoice permits handoff only after rechecking enabled policy, due cutoff, collectible state, positive balance, recipient and sender authority. This check is ordered against changes to those facts. | If the current check fails or its ordering cannot be established, there is no handoff and the authorized operator can distinguish suppression from an attempted send. | Changes committed before dispatch authorization win. An already-authorized handoff can race with a payment or other later source change. The feature cannot retract a message the channel has accepted. Any later attempt needs its own current authorization. | Authoritative source, authorized outcome and outbound handoff. Observe both orders of payment versus dispatch authorization and recipient/scope revocation in A4. |

**State and domain rules:** The invoice source owns the recipient designation and invoice-facing reference. The owner authorizes the sending identity. The reminder runtime uses personal contact data only for current eligibility and handoff. Durable reminder state and operational events exclude the address, message contents and payment details. Discard transient address and message data at the end of the current evaluation or handoff. The channel receives the address and message only for dispatch, and the recipient controls the received copy in their own inbox. No recipient session or interaction is required. Contact changes observed before authorization replace pending contact data. An authorization is exclusive to the current attempt and permits one handoff. It cannot be reused after interruption. Recovery and late results are governed by R-3.

**Open prerequisites:** The implementer must confirm that the source and authorization boundaries can establish the required ordering for final authorization. The invoice owner or authorized operator must confirm the actual billing destination mapping and sender authority before a live send. The selected channel’s permitted data handling and delivery observation are pending. These prerequisites affect live dispatch and real-boundary verification. Message and suppression rules can be specified and checked using scoped fixtures.

### R-3 One reminder and recoverable delivery

**Basis and delta:** 2026-11-21 user request, “The incoming request.” A reminder that runs automatically must be safe to evaluate again or recover after interruption. Adds per-invoice outcome tracking and retry/reconciliation behavior. No legacy attempt, acceptance or audit behavior was available to preserve. Implementation and checks are pending.

| Acceptance | Positive | Failure | Boundary | Consumer surface and confirmation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Duplicate prevention | **Default:** allow at most one channel-accepted automatic reminder over the lifetime of one issued invoice, identified by owner scope and stable invoice identity. Record handed-off outcomes durably. | After overlapping evaluations or worker interruption, only one attempt may be authorized at a time and an interrupted attempt cannot be resumed with a stale authorization. A stale completion cannot overwrite a newer or terminal outcome. | Before the first acceptance a fresh attempt requires definitive evidence that the preceding attempt did not and can no longer be accepted. At and beyond one acceptance, repeated scans, contact or date changes, disable/re-enable and reopening the same invoice produce no second acceptance. A genuinely new invoice with its own identity is evaluated independently. | Scoped operational outcome, handoff observations and test inbox. Confirm competing evaluations, repeat processing and separate invoice identities in A5. |
| Recovery | A confirmed temporary non-acceptance can be retried on a later permitted evaluation after fresh eligibility and authority checks. A confirmed permanent rejection surfaces for correction and is reconsidered only after the implicated input is corrected. | On a timeout or interruption with uncertain acceptance, hold further sends for that invoice until authoritative evidence resolves the original attempt. Confirmed acceptance closes it. Confirmed non-acceptance permits reconsideration only when the old attempt can no longer reach the channel. | Respect runtime and channel admission limits. Stop submitting when the boundary denies capacity and leave eligible work discoverable for a later permitted evaluation. Unresolved attempts do not authorize a speculative retry. No retry with invalid eligibility or after acceptance. | Authorized operational outcome, attempt observations and inbox. Reconcile exact attempt identity under a controlled lost response and restart, then exercise the selected channel in A6. |
| Outcome reporting | Authorized operators can distinguish pending/held work, suppression with reason, confirmed rejection and channel acceptance. Correlate attempts with outcomes through opaque scoped identifiers. | If the channel result is unavailable, show uncertainty. A diagnostic sink failure preserves the business outcome and does not authorize another attempt. Stale or wrong-scope results have no effect. | Channel acceptance means submission accepted. Only an observed message establishes receipt. Unauthorized readers see no invoice, attempt, recipient or outcome data from another scope. | Authorized outcome read-back, diagnostic destination and controlled inbox. Compare canonical state and observations in A6 and A7. |

**State and domain rules:** Before an attempt, the owner/invoice pair is unattempted or eligible for reconsideration. The reminder runtime durably records an exclusive attempt before possible external effect. The attempt may become confirmed non-acceptance (temporarily retryable or waiting for correction), uncertain, or accepted. An accepted invoice is terminal for automatic dispatch. Current attempt identity and authorization gate outcome writes; after restart an abandoned attempt is treated as uncertain unless non-acceptance and inability to accept later are established. The runtime owns the current state and recovery. Authorized operators can read scoped outcomes. Recipients have no access to the operational record. Keep the minimum scoped identifier and outcome needed to distinguish pending effects and prevent repeats while the invoice can be reconsidered. On permanent removal, prevent further dispatch and remove reminder state when any in-flight handoff is resolved or fenced. If a pending handoff cannot be resolved or fenced, retain only the minimum hold marker and keep the cleanup obligation visible. Late observations cannot recreate removed state or apply to another invoice. The source must supply a stable identity that cannot be confused with reissued or restored records. Source retention is unchanged.

**Open prerequisites:** The implementer must verify durable conditional state transitions and attempt reconciliation against the selected runtime and channel. If a channel lacks authoritative evidence for an ambiguous attempt, leaving that attempt held is the defined safe outcome. The implementer and service owner must establish actual admission limits, recovery and scoped operational retrieval before live use. The data owner must confirm applicable operational-event and provider retention/deletion policies before live use. The invoice-level hold, terminal state and isolation rules are independently specifiable.

## Behavior and boundary contracts

| Flow | Trigger, transitions and observable result |
| --- | --- |
| F-1 Setup and evaluation (R-1) | T-1 An authorized operator with a valid scope and bindings changes the effective setting and reads it back. Invalid or stale changes leave it intact. T-2 An unattended evaluation reads current source facts. Ineligibility stops that invoice for this pass, indeterminate facts report suppression, and eligibility allows consideration for F-2. |
| F-2 Dispatch (R-2, R-3) | T-3 For a candidate with no accepted reminder or active/uncertain attempt, obtain exclusive attempt ownership and current dispatch authorization before handoff. A final eligibility failure reports suppression and releases any attempt reserved in this step once it cannot produce a handoff. A later permitted evaluation can reconsider the invoice from current facts. T-4 Hand off with that attempt identity. Record acceptance or definitive rejection against the current attempt. A lost, ambiguous or interrupted outcome leaves the attempt held. |
| F-3 Recovery (R-1, R-2, R-3) | T-5 Recover abandoned work against the current attempt from durable state. Acceptance closes it, definitive non-acceptance plus inability to accept later permits later reconsideration through T-2 and T-3, and insufficient evidence retains the hold. T-6 Permanent invoice/scope removal ends dispatch eligibility and allows cleanup after outstanding effects are resolved or fenced. Stale results cannot resurrect it. |

These are logical boundaries. Facilities may be provided by the same product.

| Boundary | Authority and freshness | Incremental state, completion, recovery and owned coverage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Operator → reminder policy and outcomes | Current invoice-scope authorization gates setup, changes and read-back. Revocation takes effect for subsequent operations without waiting for cached state to expire. | Change only the selected scope’s policy using current state. Read-back confirms effective setup, while send outcomes are independent. Reminder ownership covers accepted/rejected configuration changes and scoped outcomes. Confirm authorization and retrieval in A1 and A7. |
| Invoice authority → unattended reminder runtime | Current source facts determine eligibility and dispatch authorization. Delayed invoice or payment hints, if present, are prompts to re-read. Missing or revoked access stops affected dispatch. | Evaluate current eligible work without changing source records or losing unrelated invoices on recovery. Re-evaluation recovers missed work. The reminder runtime owns eligibility suppression and dependency errors. The invoice authority continues to own changes to invoice and payment facts. Confirm source and cutoff behavior in A2 and A4. |
| Reminder runtime → delivery channel → billing contact | The runtime acts under the owner’s authorized sending identity. The selected channel owns its acceptance and delivery signals. Recipient and sender authority are checked at dispatch authorization, and retries recheck current authority. | Each handoff and outcome refers to one scoped attempt. Unknown outcomes are reconciled against the original attempt or held. Duplicate, stale and wrong-scope results cannot change effective state. The reminder runtime owns attempt, rejection, hold, recovery and accepted-submission observations. Confirm real handoff and receipt separately in A3, A5 and A6. |

Inapplicable: consumer sessions, inbound replies, push subscriptions, a replica/cache and cross-system billing writes are outside the reminder contract.

## Submission

**summary**

The specification defines unattended evaluation of collectible unpaid invoices, current-authority dispatch to the designated billing contact and recovery without duplicate reminders. “Set up automatic” maps to R-1 Activation and Due evaluation. “Reminders for unpaid invoices” maps to R-2 Recipient, Message and Final eligibility, with R-3 protecting repeated and interrupted runs. The supplied stage sources establish no baseline parity, runtime limits or conflicting requirement.

- **R-1 assumption:** First eligible evaluation at or after the due cutoff, including already-overdue invoices. Date-only due dates include the full billing day in the source’s billing time zone. This distinguishes past-due reminders from pre-due prompts and avoids guessing a missing time zone. Alternatives are pre-due prompts, a grace delay or excluding the pre-existing backlog. Revalidate against the invoice owner’s due-date and reminder policy and when runtime scheduling and admission limits are known.
- **R-2 assumption:** Use email to one authoritative invoice billing contact. Plain text with issuer, invoice reference, outstanding status and disregard-if-paid guidance is sufficient to identify the obligation while limiting exposure. Alternatives are another established channel, a designated set of billing recipients or additional payment details. Revalidate when the owner’s supported channel, contact and message conventions are available.
- **R-3 assumption:** One accepted automatic reminder over an issued invoice’s lifetime, with an ambiguous handoff held pending definitive evidence. Repeated evaluations need a stopping rule to avoid unbounded customer contact, and a lost result can conceal an accepted send. Alternatives are a finite recurring schedule or retry based on a verified channel deduplication contract. Revalidate if an accepted follow-up policy or authoritative channel evidence changes the decision. The count of one is a product default within the documented dispatch contract, not a provider quota.

Open prerequisites are scoped to activation, live source and channel integration, runtime capacity, operational data handling and real-boundary verification, as recorded under R-1 through R-3. The product owner or authorized operator supplies the target and sender authority before activation. The implementer and service owners establish runtime feasibility before dependent integration. The delivery team owns system-boundary checks, and real receipt is due at release verification if it cannot be observed before release. Outstanding required outcomes follow the unverified policy. A primary outcome can be left unverified at product acceptance only with a passed lower-layer test for that requirement and a recorded reason, owner, settlement check and due phase. This standalone stage produced the specification and paper walkthroughs. Implementation and product verification are pending. Later discoveries that contradict accepted behavior must use the existing correction route and reconcile affected cases before dependent work proceeds.
