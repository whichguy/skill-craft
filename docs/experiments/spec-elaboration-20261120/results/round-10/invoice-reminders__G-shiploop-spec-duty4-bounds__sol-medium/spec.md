# Invoicing

## Scope and basis

Source S1 · Incoming request, 2026-11-21, **The incoming request** · “Set up automatic reminders for unpaid invoices.”

The product evaluates invoices unattended and sends reminders to the recipient authorized by the applicable billing policy. The protected outcomes are timely automatic initiation under that policy, suppression when an invoice is ineligible, and a reminder reaching the authorized destination without duplicate sends for the same scheduled occurrence. The scope is reminder behavior. Invoice issuance, payment collection, changes to balances or contact data, and a new settings or status screen are outside this request.

Dispatch authorization is the product's decision to release a specific handoff under current authority. Candidate reads and reservations do not authorize a send. Channel acceptance and recipient delivery are later observations. R-2 and R-3 govern changes before release. R-4 governs ambiguous or failed handoffs after release.

Under **Where you are**, this standalone stage has no prior spec, repository, discovery or research result, selected baseline, or parity reference. S1 is the observed product instruction. The conditions below derive from S1 and the correctness needs of unattended sending. The current invoice workflow, system capabilities, and existing automation are unknown. No existing behavior was verified or reconciled. “Pending” indicates an implementation or verification obligation.

## Requirements

### R-1 · Run automatically on the applicable schedule

**Basis and delta.** S1, **The incoming request**, “automatic reminders.” Add unattended evaluation of reminder occurrences. Preserve the invoice owner's rules and records. Existing automation and timing rules are unknown. Implementation and checks C1 and C2 are pending.

- **A1 · Automatic trigger.** Positive — When an invoice's scheduled occurrence becomes due under the effective policy, the product evaluates it without a person, open client, or per-invoice send action. Failure — With missing or invalid timing policy or an untrustworthy clock, no send is authorized and the authorized operational consumer can distinguish the failure from an evaluation with no due invoices. Boundary — An occurrence may become eligible at its scheduled instant, never before. The maximum lateness and the disposition of missed occurrences are open. **Surface and confirmation** — Observe the unattended runtime's evaluation outcome and outgoing delivery boundary on either side of a supplied schedule instant with the client closed (C1, C2).
- **A2 · Occurrence scope.** Positive — Each due occurrence is associated with one invoice within its owning account. Where the approved policy permits multiple occurrences, each is evaluated independently under R-2 through R-4. Failure — An occurrence whose invoice or owning-account identity cannot be resolved grants no send authority. Boundary — Repeated or overlapping evaluation of the same occurrence preserves its identity, including across restart. An occurrence outside the effective policy grants no send authority. **Surface and confirmation** — Compare the authoritative schedule, scoped occurrence outcomes, and sends under repeated evaluation and with an invalid target (C2, C6).

**State and recovery.** The billing policy owner controls effective reminder policy. The unattended product reads the policy and time for the scheduled evaluation, with changes and reads restricted to their authorized scope. Scheduling reads invoice identity and policy and writes only reminder processing outcomes where needed. Before evaluation there is no authority to send from a timing signal alone. Afterwards any eligible occurrence still needs the current-state and recipient checks. On restart, reapply the effective policy and R-4 to scheduled work still allowed by that policy. A stale or removed policy cannot independently authorize a send. Retire superseded policy from active use and use its revision or equivalent provenance only while needed for authorized recovery and audit. Policy storage, evidence retention, and execution limits depend on the target.

**Open gaps.** The billing owner must supply any existing rule or approve the initial trigger anchor and timing, including whether unpaid invoices before their due date qualify, recurrence or a single reminder, any repeat/end rule, time zone/calendar if applicable, missed-run behavior and whether already-open invoices enter the schedule. The request gives no basis for a cadence, reminder count, age window, start date, or historical catch-up default. These choices gate schedule activation and its real timing checks. The runtime owner must identify the unattended execution facility, effective principal, clock, schedule resolution, and execution and workload limits. Eligibility, recipient isolation, and recovery semantics are independently specifiable.

### R-2 · Send only while the invoice qualifies

**Basis and delta.** S1, **The incoming request**, “unpaid invoices.” Add an eligibility guard for reminders. Preserve authoritative invoice, balance, and payment state and their existing ownership. Their schema, freshness guarantees, and status mapping are unknown. Implementation and checks C3 and C4 are pending.

- **A1 · Eligibility.** Positive — A due occurrence may proceed when the authoritative billing record identifies the target invoice as unpaid and eligible for collection under the billing policy. Failure — A missing record, inaccessible authority, ambiguous eligibility, or inconsistent data defers or suppresses the occurrence without authorizing a send, with the corresponding reason visible to an authorized operational consumer. Boundary — A paid or otherwise conclusively ineligible invoice grants no send authority. **Surface and confirmation** — Compare billing truth for each target with the outgoing handoffs and recorded dispositions (C3).
- **A2 · Changes in flight.** Positive — Dispatch authorization uses current authoritative eligibility and authority. Failure — If the product cannot establish their freshness and ordering relative to dispatch authorization, it withholds authorization and records the reason. Boundary — A payment, other disqualifying change, or revocation authoritative before dispatch authorization prevents that authorization, including if a prior read said eligible. A change effective after authorization prevents further authorizations under the disqualifying state. A reminder already released may still arrive. A proven rejection requires a fresh authorization before any later handoff. **Surface and confirmation** — Exercise both orderings at the dispatch boundary, including a stale candidate, and observe scoped outgoing effects and disposition (C4, C7).

**State and recovery.** The authoritative billing owner controls the account-scoped invoice, balance, payment and collectibility facts. Existing authorized billing actors may change those facts. The reminder principal reads the necessary fields and does not mutate them. The product may hold transient facts for evaluation. An older snapshot cannot override an authoritative change at dispatch authorization. After an inconclusive read or authorization check, any new evaluation must obtain current facts. The runtime discards transient facts when evaluation ends. Billing-record retention and removal remain with the billing authority. Reminder status readers receive only their authorized scope.

**Open gaps.** The billing owner must identify the actual source, stable invoice/account identity, status and balance rules, including partial payments, disputed, voided, written-off or reopened invoices where supported, and how the source recognizes committed payment. The runtime and billing owners must establish a verifiable dispatch-authorization ordering against authoritative updates and revocation. Unknown ordering gates real sending and the race check. Synthetic partitions can exercise the intended outcomes independently.

### R-3 · Deliver a usable reminder to the authorized recipient

**Basis and delta.** S1, **The incoming request**, “reminders.” Add recipient-facing notice of the unpaid invoice. Preserve billing/contact ownership and existing authorization rules. Recipient policy, channel, and their current behavior are unknown. Implementation and checks C5 and C8 are pending.

- **A1 · Recipient and content.** Positive — At dispatch authorization resolve the approved destination for that invoice within its owning account. The notice identifies the invoice in terms the recipient can recognize and says that payment is outstanding. Failure — If the destination is missing, invalid, unauthorized, ambiguous under policy, or cannot be confirmed, suppress or defer sending and expose a safe reason to the authorized operational consumer. Boundary — An identically named invoice or contact from another account grants no destination authority. A destination change or revocation authoritative before authorization supersedes any earlier candidate. **Surface and confirmation** — Inspect the actual destination and recipient-visible notice on an isolated approved channel, with contrasting same-ID and changed-destination cases (C5).
- **A2 · Truthful completion.** Positive — Record channel acceptance from an authoritative matching result, and report delivery as confirmed only when the channel supplies evidence of it. The recipient observes the actual reminder on the chosen channel for the successful case. Failure — A confirmed rejection, known later delivery failure, and indeterminate result remain distinguishable from confirmed delivery. Boundary — Provider acceptance alone means handed off. Where the channel exposes no delivery confirmation, status remains handed off with delivery unconfirmed. **Surface and confirmation** — Observe recipient output, the delivery boundary, and its matching operational status for successful, rejected, delayed failure and unknown results (C5, C7, C8).

**State and recovery.** The billing/contact authority retains the destination and access rules. The runtime reads it in scope and uses minimum necessary invoice data and destination for the notice. Those fields are visible to the authorized recipient on the delivery channel and are sent only to the approved delivery boundary. Transient render data is removed after the attempt. Any required provider-side copy and retention depend on the approved channel. Logs contain scoped correlation/outcome and safe reason codes, with no destination, message body, invoice amount, or secrets. Diagnostic access follows current account or operational authorization. Changes in the source win before dispatch authorization. A previous delivery cannot be withdrawn by editing contact data.

**Open gaps.** The billing owner must identify the intended audience, routing rules (including the recipient of record and any consent or suppression rules), approved channel and content constraints. No audience or channel default is supported by S1 alone. The channel owner must confirm the effective sender, destination isolation, available evidence, redaction and retention/deletion route, and authorize an isolated receiving target. These gate the live recipient check and integration. No graphical product surface is specified. The notice itself needs legible, unambiguous text in the supported receiving surface. A newly required graphical surface would reopen the scope and visual requirements.

### R-4 · Prevent duplicate sends and recover safely

**Basis and delta.** S1, **The incoming request**, “automatic reminders.” Add interruption and duplicate handling for unattended sends. Preserve authoritative billing and contact data. Existing reminder history, processing facilities, and provider recovery guarantees are unknown. Implementation and checks C6 through C8 are pending.

- **A1 · Single accepted handoff per occurrence.** Positive — Record enough recoverable, scoped outcome information to prevent another accepted handoff for the same invoice occurrence after a recorded acceptance or recovery. Failure — If prior acceptance cannot be ruled out and safe replay is unproven, default to holding the occurrence in an indeterminate state with an authorized reconciliation outcome, with no fresh handoff. Boundary — The effective allowance is one accepted handoff per account/invoice/occurrence. At that allowance, concurrent, repeated, stale, or restarted work creates no additional accepted handoff. Distinct eligible occurrences and invoices are unaffected. **Surface and confirmation** — Count scoped accepted handoffs at the delivery boundary and compare them to durable outcomes across contention, restart, and loss of acknowledgment (C6, C7).
- **A2 · Recovery.** Positive — A definitive rejection or verified non-acceptance may lead to another attempt on the same occurrence only if an approved retry policy permits and R-1 through R-3 still authorize the send. Authoritative evidence of acceptance from reconciliation closes off fresh handoffs for that occurrence. Failure — A crash, timeout, or uncertain result leaves recoverable state and an honest disposition. If the outcome remains unknown or retry is unavailable or exhausted, the authorized operational consumer can distinguish the hold or terminal failure. Boundary — Late or duplicate results must match the scoped occurrence and attempt and cannot regress a newer authoritative outcome or initiate another handoff. Exhaustion under the effective retry policy permits no further automatic attempts. **Surface and confirmation** — Compare dispatch counts, correlation, and status after interruption, reconciliation, late results, exhaustion, and invalidation before retry (C7, C8).

**State and recovery.** The reminder runtime owns per-occurrence processing and outcome state in authorized runtime-side storage with recovery across interruption. The scoped identity is account, invoice and policy occurrence. Attempt and result correlation identify the specific handoff. Before dispatch, a recoverable disposition must protect against concurrent release and restart. After release, the state reflects confirmed acceptance, definite non-acceptance, or indeterminate outcome. The processor owns reconciliation. Only the current authorized processing owner or authoritative result can advance matching state. The authorized billing/operational consumer can inspect scoped outcome and recovery reason. Accept only enough retained data to reconcile and prevent duplicates, separate from message bodies and contact data. Purge recovery state when it can no longer be needed for replay exclusion or authorized evidence under the applicable record policy. Cleanup must not reopen an occurrence to replay. Secrets, if required by the runtime, belong in its protected credential facility and are accessible only to the authorized runtime principal. They are never shown or logged and are revoked or removed when the binding ends.

**Open gaps.** The runtime and delivery owners must establish facilities for restart recovery, scope enforcement, ordered state updates, and authoritative outcome reconciliation, including any provider replay window and whether safe replay is supported. The billing/runtime owners must confirm when retries are authorized, retryable outcomes, retry timing, exhaustion and expiry, who resolves an inconclusive result, and retention/purge rules. There is no evidence for a retry count, delay, processing deadline, or retention duration. The safe hold applies wherever acceptance is indeterminate and safe replay is unproven. Provider-specific retries and cleanup stay unready pending those contracts.
