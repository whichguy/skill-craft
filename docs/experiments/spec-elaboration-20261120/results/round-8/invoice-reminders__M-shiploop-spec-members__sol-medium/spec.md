# Invoice Billing

## Scope and basis

The user requested “Set up automatic reminders for unpaid invoices” on 2026-11-21 (incoming request, **The incoming request**). The billing operator enables the policy for a billing account. Background processing evaluates that account's invoices and sends an eligible reminder to the designated billing contact. The operator can confirm enablement and inspect what happened. This specification does not change invoice balances, payment processing, contact maintenance, or collection policy.

The incoming request is the only product source. The standalone stage provides no existing specification, selected system baseline, discovery or research results, or reference product. The policies below are proposed defaults for the unspecified parts of the request. Existing product behavior, interfaces, and external capabilities are unknown. Logical actors and surfaces name the required authority and observations. Their concrete bindings are open where noted.

## Requirements

### R-1 Enable or stop automatic reminders

**Basis and delta.** The 2026-11-21 request, **The incoming request**, “Set up automatic reminders,” adds account-scoped enablement and unattended operation. There are no prior clauses to reconcile. No account, role mapping, control route, or scheduler was inspected. Implementation and C-1, C-2, and C-6 are pending.

- **Enable (F-1, T-1).** Positive — an actor currently authorized to manage reminders for the target billing account can enable the default policy and read back its effective state. Background evaluation proceeds after the actor leaves the control surface. Failure — a rejected change leaves the effective policy intact. If the outcome is unknown, the operator sees an unconfirmed result and uses authoritative read-back before assuming it took effect. Boundary — absent configuration means disabled. An already-effective request at the current revision is a no-op and preserves the activation time. **Surface and confirmation** — compare control response and authoritative state, then observe a scheduled outcome with the operator session closed.
- **Disable (F-1, T-1).** Positive — a confirmed disable prevents any new release for that account until an authorized re-enable. Failure — a failed disable reports failure and does not claim that processing stopped. Boundary — a handoff already released may finish and must still be reconciled under R-4. Re-enable creates a new activation time and never clears an invoice's attempt history. **Surface and confirmation** — compare effective configuration and delivery-boundary observations for disable ordered before and after release.
- **Control authority (F-1, T-2).** Positive — current, account-scoped management permission governs policy reads and changes. Failure — missing permission, revoked permission, or a foreign account denies access without exposing account data or changing policy. An unverifiable permission also denies access. Boundary — conflicting writes against the same revision resolve to the first committed change. A stale writer gets a conflict and an authorized read-back path. **Surface and confirmation** — use allowed, revoked, and foreign identities, and two conflicting requests against one revision, verifying final state with an authorized observer.

**State and lifecycle.** The billing account's policy authority owns durable enabled/disabled state, activation instant and current revision. Only current authorized operators and the scoped background actor read it. Only current authorized operators change it. Restart preserves confirmed configuration. Before enrollment or after disable there is no permission to release new reminders. Account closure ends enrollment and suppresses further releases. Disable retains the minimal history needed for duplicate prevention. Policy history and audit cleanup follow the retention gap in R-4.

**Open gaps.** Billing owner must identify the target accounts, current management permission and a supported authorized control/read-back route before activation or integration checks. Runtime owner must identify an unattended trigger, recovery owner and actual operating limits before scheduler integration. No new sign-in, recipient account, preference page, or public control is in scope. A verified enable/disable route is still required.

### R-2 Determine which unpaid invoices get a reminder and when

**Basis and delta.** The 2026-11-21 request, **The incoming request**, “automatic reminders for unpaid invoices,” adds a due-date eligibility rule and suppression at release. No prior invoice behavior is established. Invoice statuses, dates, balance calculations and authority ordering have no inspected source. Implementation and C-3, C-4, C-6 are pending.

- **Eligible invoice (F-2, T-3).** Positive — a finalized, issued, collectible invoice with a positive remaining balance qualifies during its reminder window. A partial payment qualifies if a positive balance remains. Failure — an unavailable, contradictory or unmappable authoritative invoice record blocks release and leaves an operator-visible reason. Boundary — zero or negative balance, fully paid, unissued/draft, void, canceled, deleted, written-off/uncollectible, or collection held (including disputed invoices on hold) produce zero releases while those conditions apply. Missing or unparseable due dates are ineligible with a reason. **Surface and confirmation** — observe the eligibility disposition and count releases for representative records from each group, including a stale eligible snapshot superseded by authoritative state.
- **Reminder window (F-2, T-3).** Positive — for a due date of D, the default opportunity starts at 09:00 UTC on D+1 and ends at 00:00 UTC on D+2. A background evaluation can release during that window if the start is at or after the current activation instant. Failure — evaluations outside the window produce no release. Boundary — 09:00 is included, midnight is excluded, exact activation at opening qualifies, and there is no catch-up if the activation is after opening or an outage spans the whole window. A due-date edit before release recomputes the window. This is one reminder per invoice, with no repeat series. **Surface and confirmation** — drive the background entry at the start and end boundaries, across a restart and a due date change, and verify the actual release count.
- **Recheck at release (F-2, T-3/T-4).** Positive — release uses current policy, authoritative invoice revision, and current authorized bill-to contact for the same account and invoice. Failure — withhold release when those facts cannot be established. Boundary — a payment to zero, a hold, a due-date change out of window, a disable, or a contact revocation effective before release takes precedence over queued work. An already released handoff follows R-4. **Surface and confirmation** — force each change between selection and release, observe no submission with stale data, and compare the converse ordering against delivery and operation records.

**State and lifecycle.** The billing authority owns account ID, stable invoice ID, status, due date, balance and collection holds. The contact authority owns the current billing addressee and delivery permission. The background actor has scoped read permission. Only existing billing and contact authorities mutate their records. Selection is transient. Eligibility and destination are reevaluated at the release boundary and a stale candidate conveys no authority. An invoice keeps its identity across due date or recipient edits. A canceled or deleted record suppresses new work, subject to reconciliation of previously released work. Existing invoice and contact retention and deletion stay with their owners.

**Open gaps.** Billing owner must map source statuses, holds, date semantics, balance and correction behavior, and supply a stable account/invoice identity. Confirm how due timestamps, business time zones and any existing grace policy map to the provisional UTC rule. Scope excludes installments with separate due schedules, escalation, late fees and payment promises. Runtime and billing owners must demonstrate ordering between source changes and the release boundary, including the case where the source changes after a candidate was read. A stale read or a best-effort payment event does not satisfy that rule. Before evidence exists, withhold release where freshness or ordering is uncertain. This gates binding and live release. The policy cases can be specified independently.

### R-3 Send the invoice reminder to the designated billing contact

**Basis and delta.** The 2026-11-21 request, **The incoming request**, “reminders,” adds a customer-facing message. Email and one designated bill-to address are default product choices. No prior message, sender, address policy or mail facility was inspected. Implementation and C-5 and C-6 are pending.

- **Recipient and content (F-2, T-4).** Positive — submit one email to the current permitted invoice billing address. It identifies the sender, invoice reference, due date, and remaining balance and currency, using the values authorized at release. Include an approved way for the recipient to resolve the invoice, using the existing payment instructions or billing contact route once verified. Failure — missing, invalid, ambiguous, cross-account, suppressed or revoked destinations, or unavailable required message data, prevent submission and produce an operator-visible reason. Boundary — exactly one designated destination qualifies. An address change before release replaces the address used for the message. The reminder does not add CC, BCC, alternate recipients, attachments or sensitive payment credentials. **Surface and confirmation** — inspect an authorized synthetic mailbox's envelope and displayed message, and compare the delivery-boundary submission count for suppressed cases and changes before release.

**State and lifecycle.** The approved billing sender and background identity use scoped authority for the account. Resolve contact data and render the message for the active attempt. Retain recipient and content only in the participating billing and delivery surfaces where needed for the approved purpose and retention policy. The reminder history and diagnostics omit raw addresses and bodies. The intended recipient sees only their invoice details. Current permission is rechecked for each release, even on retry. Delivery acceptance may make recall impossible. Recipient copies follow the recipient's mail lifecycle.

**Open gaps.** Billing and delivery owners must identify the actual authorized sender, address selection and suppression rules, resolution instructions, and isolated mail route. Confirm the service's message-size and addressing constraints, data retention, and completion evidence on that route. Customer locale and custom copy are outside the default policy. Live email remains unready until these prerequisites are resolved.

### R-4 Prevent duplicate reminders and report truthful outcomes

**Basis and delta.** The 2026-11-21 request, **The incoming request**, “automatic reminders,” implies unattended processing and trustworthy completion. This adds delivery outcome tracking and bounded recovery for the proposed single reminder. There is no previous attempt history, delivery boundary or observability contract to reconcile. Implementation and C-6 through C-9 are pending.

- **Prevent duplicate effects (F-2/F-3, T-4/T-5).** Positive — for a given account and invoice, overlapping evaluations, rediscovery after restart and duplicated results converge on one logical reminder and at most one accepted handoff. Failure — inability to establish exclusive release authority or durably retain the pending attempt prevents a new submission. Boundary — an accepted handoff permanently consumes the reminder opportunity for that invoice, including after a due-date edit, reopen, or disable/re-enable. **Surface and confirmation** — observe authorized status and actual delivery-boundary effects under overlap, replay and restart.
- **Recover from definitive failure (F-3, T-6).** Positive — a definitively unaccepted transient first attempt gets one automatic retry on the next evaluation that satisfies the fresh release check within the first attempt's original window. Failure — a permanent refusal, a second definitive refusal, or expiry of that window ends automatic recovery. A definitive refusal is never shown as accepted. Boundary — two submissions maximum per invoice. A due-date edit cannot extend the retry deadline, and a lost acknowledgment alone never permits retry. **Surface and confirmation** — count submissions, inspect final disposition, and repeat after expiry, payment and permanent refusal.
- **Recover from unknown outcome (F-3, T-5/T-7).** Positive — missing or ambiguous acknowledgment moves the attempt to outcome unknown and blocks further submissions until authoritative reconciliation. Positive confirmation of acceptance resolves it to accepted. Definitive nonacceptance permits the T-6 decision. Failure — if truth cannot be established, keep the hold and surface it for authorized operational investigation. Boundary — restart, expiry, stale data, and a later contradictory or duplicated result do not authorize a resend or undo confirmed acceptance. An outage before attempt registration leaves evaluation eligible for later recheck. A crash after registration that cannot prove release did not start is ambiguous. **Surface and confirmation** — interrupt before and after the durable boundary, inject late results, check authoritative operation status and count submissions.
- **Report outcomes (F-3, T-8).** Positive — an authorized operator can distinguish configuration off, ineligible or suppressed (with reason), pending handoff, accepted by delivery, retry pending, failed/expired, and outcome unknown. Show the attempt time and a safe invoice/attempt reference where applicable. Show current policy and eligibility separately from any recorded handoff outcome so a later disable or payment cannot hide an unresolved or accepted handoff. Failure — status unavailability is explicit. A diagnostic sink failure never turns an unknown handoff into acceptance or triggers another send. Boundary — delivery acceptance is the final sending milestone required here. Inbox placement, reading, and actual payment have separate meanings. Foreign or revoked viewers cannot read operation detail or infer record existence. **Surface and confirmation** — compare authorized status and redacted events against authoritative attempt evidence for each disposition and assert access denial and truthful state when the diagnostic destination fails.

**State and lifecycle.** The reminder authority owns minimal durable attempt state for the account/invoice pair. The background actor records the intent before any external submission and is the only authorized release actor. Keep distinct logical reminder and attempt references, the applicable revisions, timestamps and outcomes. Pending means responsibility was recorded and acceptance is unsettled. Pending can become accepted, retry pending, failed or unknown. Unknown can resolve only from definitive evidence. Retry pending can coexist with a temporary eligibility block and becomes failed/expired if the first attempt's window closes. Suppression before the first release does not consume the opportunity. Accepted and terminal failure consume it. Late results for another attempt or a superseded owner cannot overwrite newer confirmed state. Account closure ends new work and narrows reconciliation access to authorized operations. Retain minimal duplicate-prevention state while the same invoice identity can reenter eligibility, or establish permanent exclusion before deleting that state. Clean up transient payloads after use and purge remaining attempt and diagnostic data under the approved retention policy. Operator read-back obeys current account scope on each read.

**Open gaps.** Delivery and runtime owners must verify real refusal and acceptance semantics, correlation/lookup, abandoned attempt recovery, and concurrent release behavior before live sending. If the delivery route cannot resolve an ambiguous attempt, operations retains the hold until authoritative evidence exists. The human investigation remains open. Billing/data owner must set retention and deletion rules, including erasure while an invoice could reenter scope and retention at the delivery provider. Operations owner must identify the status and diagnostic routes, enforce access and retention, and decide the owner and response window for unresolved sends. Required checks are pending at system-test on the chosen isolated boundaries. Any only-person check is owned by the authorized billing or delivery operator and is due at handoff, or at the activation phase if it gates safe activation.

## Submission

**summary** — Specified account-scoped automatic reminders, including eligibility, payment suppression, customer delivery and duplicate-safe recovery. Defaults assumed an authorized billing operator, disabled before enrollment, a single email to the designated billing address, one UTC reminder window starting the morning after the due date, no enrollment catch-up, and one retry only after a definitive transient refusal. Billing integration, permission and freshness mapping, delivery authorization and reconciliation, retention and runtime bounds remain open for the affected work. No material conflict is evidenced. The specification and paper walkthrough are complete for this stage.

**primary** — R-1 (enable automatic processing), R-2 (select and time eligible unpaid invoices), R-3 (send the reminder). At product acceptance, at most one primary requirement may remain unverified, and only with a passed lower-layer test. No such pass is recorded here.
