# Invoice Billing

## Basis and scope

The 2026-11-21 incoming request, “Set up automatic reminders for unpaid invoices,” is the sole product source. The standalone stage supplied no earlier spec, repository, discovery, research, baseline, or parity reference. Existing product behavior and capabilities are unknown. The requirements below add reminder behavior while preserving the billing authority's ownership of invoices, payments, credits, and their business rules. The request authorizes the feature definition. In this stage all recipients, identities, and actions are hypothetical fixtures.

An authorized billing administrator activates the policy for an existing billing scope. Thereafter the runtime evaluates invoices and sends email to the invoice's billing contact without an administrator, customer, or browser initiating each reminder. The administrator uses the established administration route. The recipient surface is a plain-text email, and operational verification uses authorized status and delivery records. This scope adds no screen, report, terminal interface, login, manual send, payment workflow, or new customer sharing.

## Requirements

### R-1 — Activate the automatic reminder policy

**Basis and delta.** 2026-11-21 request, “Set up automatic reminders,” in the incoming request. Adds scope-level activation and automatic execution. Preserves existing administrator roles and unrelated billing settings. [Evidence limits and replay](#specification-replay) apply. Implementation and the checks below are pending.

| Line | Positive expectation | Failure expectation | Boundary expectation | Consumer surface and confirmation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| R-1(a) | An administrator with current authority for the billing scope can enable or disable the default policy. The administration route confirms the effective state only after the change takes effect. | An unauthorized, wrong-scope, or failed change leaves the previous effective state and returns a denial or failure. A lost acknowledgment is resolved by authorized read-back of the effective state. | The policy starts disabled before explicit activation. Repeating the effective setting is a no-op. Concurrent changes are ordered by authoritative commit, and an earlier response cannot restore an older setting. | Authorized administration route and scope read-back. TC-1, TC-2. |
| R-1(b) | Once enabled, the runtime evaluates eligible invoices, including existing ones in a live window, without an open client. Restart or missed execution resumes from current policy, invoice facts, and reminder dispositions. | With missing or unconfirmed activation, runtime authority, or current invoice data, dispatch is withheld. Resume evaluation when confirmed access returns. | Disablement or revocation ordered before dispatch authorization prevents new submissions, including retries. TC-3, TC-13, TC-15, TC-17. |

**Domain rules and data.** Activation belongs to the billing scope, and effective-state changes are written only by its authorized administrators through the existing authority. Runtime work uses its own scoped authority and can only read the policy. The setting persists across restart until changed or the scope is removed. Removal or loss of runtime access stops new dispatch. Activation does not modify invoices or existing notification settings. Acknowledgment and effective state are independently checked. Custom schedules, per-invoice administration, and new roles are outside scope.

**Open gap.** The billing owner must identify the intended scope, authorized administrator and runtime roles, existing setup and read-back route, and unattended execution capability before target setup or integration planning. Confirm access at preparation and activation with the actual identities. Independent policy and eligibility design can proceed.

**Domain rules and data.** The reminder runtime owns durable scope/invoice/ordinal dispositions. Attempt identity adds the attempt number. Do not persist a second reminder copy of the address or body. **Open gap.** Billing and delivery owners must identify the approved sender/reply identity and recipient source, confirm an approved delivery route and isolated receiving mailbox, and establish an effective uniqueness and recovery boundary for authorized attempts. ### R-4 — Recover delivery failures and expose truthful status

**Basis and delta.** 2026-11-21 request, “automatic reminders,” in the incoming request. Adds bounded autonomous recovery and delivery disposition. Preserves provider ownership of submission results and billing ownership of invoice status. [Evidence limits and replay](#specification-replay) apply. Implementation and the checks below are pending.

| Line | Positive expectation | Failure expectation | Boundary expectation | Consumer surface and confirmation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| R-4(a) | On confirmed temporary non-acceptance, retry no earlier than 1 hour after the first such result and 24 hours after the second, if the same slot is still live and R-2 still permits sending. | Permanent rejection ends the slot. A temporary rejection on attempt 3 ends the slot as exhausted. If a retry cannot be authorized, no attempt is consumed. | At most 3 distinct authorized attempts per slot, including the first. Reauthorizing a conclusively unsent reservation does not consume another attempt. At the retry instant the attempt may proceed. Slot closure takes precedence over both a retry becoming due and any later resumption. A subsequent live ordinal can proceed independently. | Authorized reminder status, clock and outbound attempt captures. TC-11, TC-14, TC-17. |
| R-4(b) | Record a correlated provider acceptance as “Provider accepted.” Correlate later rejection or bounce evidence where offered and expose it distinctly, without reopening the slot. | Timeout, lost acknowledgment or a crash with uncertain submission yields “Outcome unknown.” Reconcile only from authoritative evidence for that attempt. Known non-acceptance takes the appropriate temporary/permanent branch. Unresolved uncertainty permits no resend of that attempt or its slot, including after restart. | Late outcomes can refine the matching attempt, even after its window closes. An older outcome cannot change a newer attempt's disposition or authorize a retry after closure. Provider acceptance alone makes no claim about inbox arrival or reading. | Authorized status, actual provider receipts and authorized mailbox. TC-9, TC-12, TC-15, TC-16. |
| R-4(c) | Operators can distinguish withheld, retry pending, outcome unknown, expired, exhausted, permanently rejected, provider accepted and reported bounce, including a safe correlation reference and reason. Owned records identify reservation, recovery and outcome events without sensitive contents. | A diagnostic sink failure preserves durable business status, its original failure reason and ordinary recovery rules. TC-12, TC-15, TC-18. |

**Domain rules and data.** The runtime owns reminder outcomes and recovery. The delivery authority owns acceptance, explicit rejection and any reported bounce. After a durable reservation, only the matching attempt can change its delivery evidence. A stale processor cannot overwrite a newer disposition. A conclusively unsent reservation may resume within the slot’s still-live window under fresh R-2 authorization as the same attempt, with the current authorized invoice facts. If non-submission cannot be established, use the unknown-outcome branch. Keep needed correlation and disposition under R-3's lifecycle. Approved delivery infrastructure and the addressed customer mailbox handle the message under their own access and retention rules. Approved operator diagnostics retain only the minimum safe event fields and use scoped access and controlled expiry. No mandatory inbox-read tracking or automatic message recall.

**Open gap.** The delivery owner must confirm runtime send authority, the provider's acceptance and rejection semantics, correlation/read-back options, ambiguity recovery and any bounce coverage. The runtime/privacy owners must confirm protected durable state capacity, purge triggers, existing operational event destination, role access, diagnostic and provider retention and removal handling, and reconciliation limits. Verify those on the actual authorized route before production dispatch. If authoritative reconciliation is unavailable, preserve the unknown outcome and suppress slot resubmission. No live service, delivery, access or lifecycle check is verified here.

## Behavior model

### Flows and state

| Flow | Trigger, ordered actions and observable end |
| --- | --- |
| F-1 · R-1 | Administrator uses the established authority to change activation, authority checks scope and commits, administration route confirms effective state or reports denial/failure (T-1). Runtime independently reads the effective state at dispatch. |
| F-2 · R-1, R-2 | Unattended evaluation reads current policy and scoped invoice facts, determines eligibility and live ordinal, then withholds or proposes a candidate (T-2). A candidate is provisional until the dispatch authorization in F-3. |
| F-3 · R-2, R-3 | Runtime orders current facts against authorization, durably reserves the attempt before submitting, then hands the authorized email to the delivery route (T-3). The delivery receipt is asynchronous relative to the billing administrator. |
| F-4 · R-4, R-3 | Runtime records correlated acceptance or failure (T-4), reconciles uncertain outcomes (T-5), re-enters F-2 for a permitted retry, or closes/supersedes a slot (T-6). The operator can read the resulting status (T-7). |

The invoice authority holds durable source data. For this feature an invoice is currently eligible, temporarily excluded, or permanently retired, as derived by R-2. Window membership is derived from the clock and effective due date. Activation is a durable disabled/enabled dimension owned by the billing authority. The runtime holds durable scope/invoice/ordinal dispositions and attempt facts. Before any reservation, an ordinal is unstarted. A reservation is in progress until evidence resolves it or it becomes unknown. A retry-pending disposition may advance to the next attempt. Provider accepted, permanently rejected, exhausted, superseded and expired are final for dispatch at that ordinal. A bounce annotates accepted delivery; an unknown attempt can still be reconciled, with a retry allowed only on proven temporary non-acceptance within the original slot and under the attempt cap. Persist only relevant status for invoices observed by the feature. Selection inputs are transient.

## Non-functional requirements

- **Reliability and temporal correctness.** During an enabled scope's live windows, operations continue without a client and recover after an interruption using authoritative facts. Concurrent evaluation, stale results and ambiguous submission preserve the per-slot protections. Verify overlapping work, an interruption before and after provider acceptance, and current authority on recovery via TC-3, TC-10, TC-12 and TC-15. Calendar windows and retry bounds are functional defaults in R-2 and R-4. Scheduler latency, provider timeout, end-to-end dispatch latency and recovery service targets are unresolved pending the runtime, workload and provider evidence. The delivery/runtime owners must determine those before relying on them for execution capacity.
- **Capacity and compatibility.** Invoice volume, overdue backlog, dispatch bursts, scheduling precision, storage/retention capacity, provider limits and cost ceiling are unresolved. The runtime and delivery owners must identify the supported runtime and authoritative invoice and delivery surfaces, verify documented quotas and measure representative volume before enabling a real scope. A saturated runtime must still honor the expiry and duplicate-prevention rules. Verify against an authorized workload and record what was dispatched, withheld or expired. No platform-specific quota or SLA is asserted.
- **Security, privacy and operability.** Read and dispatch only with current scoped authority. Purge feature-owned records under R-3 and validate third-party, diagnostic and retention prerequisites under R-4. Verify rejected cross-scope operations, effective revocation, redaction, purge and recovery with restricted fixtures, plus authorized event retrieval. No additional personal data collection or login is required. Access, retention, deletion and legal obligations of the selected environment remain to be confirmed by its owners before production use.
- **Consumer presentation.** R-3(a) specifies readable plain-text content, which can be checked in a real receiving mailbox. Rendering, theme, keyboard access and responsive behavior belong to the recipient's email client and the unchanged administration surface. A new graphical or terminal interface is outside scope, so no new visual direction or interface design requirement applies.
- **Release conditions.** Enablement requires an approved target, scoped authority, an overlap check for existing reminder automation, and verified receipt/status semantics. Disablement is the operational stop for new authorizations. Already accepted mail cannot be recalled by this feature. Check current policy, in-flight results and resumed eligibility before reactivating. These are observable gates, with target and operating limits still open.
