# Invoice Billing

## Scope and evidence

**Source:** “Set up automatic reminders for unpaid invoices.” (2026-11-21, incoming request). The direct outcomes are automatic processing of eligible unpaid invoices and reminders reaching the intended billing recipient. Eligibility, delivery safeguards, and recovery make those outcomes safe to operate. This spec covers the background workflow and its outbound notice. Invoice editing, payment collection, customer identity management, and a new settings or status UI are outside this scope.

**Observed:** The supplied working directory contains the ShipLoop guides. The standalone brief supplies no repository, prior spec, discovery or research result, selected baseline, comparator, or runtime evidence. Existing invoice states, interfaces, policy and provider behavior are unknown. Parity mapping is inapplicable because the request names no reference system. The proposed before/after rules below are contract invariants, not observations of an installed product.

**Inferred:** A reminder requires an authoritative balance, an authorized destination, and a processing path that runs without someone opening the product. The overdue rule and email in this document are proposed product defaults. Logical actors and boundaries describe responsibilities and observations. Deployment topology, technology and vendor decisions remain open. No architecture decision was made.

**Verification status:** All product implementation and checks are pending. The case identifiers name planned observations, with a manual contract walk recorded separately. For internal behavior the verification owner is the billing feature owner, due at system-test. For actual recipient delivery the verification owner is the delivery/integration owner, due at system-test on an authorized end-to-end target or at release-verify if a live target is required by the delivery contract. If a check can only be performed by an authorized person and the delivery host has no route to it, record that person as owner and retain it unverified at handoff or at the phase whose external effect it gates. At product-acceptance an unverified primary outcome can be retained only with a passed lower-layer test. That does not satisfy the missing consumer observation. No lower-layer pass exists here.

## Requirements

### R-1 Automatic reminder processing

**Basis and delta:** The 2026-11-21 request, “Set up automatic reminders for unpaid invoices,” supplies “automatic.” Add background processing while leaving invoice and payment writes with their existing owner, if present. Existing setup and scheduling behavior are unknown. Sources are the incoming request and Behavior model § Sequence flows. Implementation and the following checks are pending.

| Acceptance line | Positive | Failure | Boundary | Surface and confirmation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A — Run unattended | With an authorized active policy, when a reminder opportunity becomes due, the processor evaluates it without a person opening the product or manually sending a notice (F-1, T-1). | A processor interruption leaves unfinished work recoverable under R-5. | Disabled or unapplied policy produces zero dispatches. A run before an opportunity is due produces zero dispatches. | Observe the configured runtime's background trigger, processing outcome and outbound attempts with all user sessions closed. Advance controlled time across the due instant and repeat with policy disabled. C-1. |
| B — Apply a valid policy | Operations apply a policy only through existing authorized billing controls. A valid change governs subsequent authorization to dispatch. | Reject unauthorized or invalid changes without replacing the active policy or producing a dispatch. | A disable committed before dispatch authorization suppresses pending work. Previously accepted messages may already be on the delivery path. | Observe authoritative policy read-back and outbound attempts with valid, invalid and unauthorized updates and a pending opportunity. C-2. |

**State and lifecycle:** Policy is billing-scope-owned durable configuration, readable and changeable only by authorized billing actors and readable by the processor for that scope. Trigger input is a wake-up signal, usable only by the authorized processor. It carries no authority to send and need not be retained beyond processing. Policy edits use the latest committed version, with stale writes rejected so a stale writer cannot undo a disable. Disable ends dispatch authorization; policy removal and account closure follow the billing scope's lifecycle and prevent dispatch. Recover after interruption by re-evaluating current policy and work state (R-5).

**Rules left open:** Actual authority and activation path, scope of enablement, cadence and repeat count, local time and timezone rules, backlog on first enablement, and treatment of missed occurrences after downtime. Proposed default is explicit activation per billing scope, with no historical catch-up on initial enablement until approved. The product owner and billing integration owner must settle these before real scheduling and activation. Background trigger mechanism, quotas, and configuration read-back surface require runtime evidence. Policy gating and timer-independent behavior can be designed using symbolic due times.

**State and lifecycle:** Target identity is billing scope plus invoice identifier. Billing owns invoice, balance, status, due date, currency and revision. The processor may read only authorized records. Current billing state wins over earlier candidate reads. Candidate snapshots are transient and discarded when the decision finishes. Invoice removal or loss of authority invalidates pending eligibility. Source retention remains governed by billing. Recovery rereads the source and re-evaluates R-1 and R-2.

**Rules left open:** The product owner must confirm overdue versus pre-due coverage, treatment of disputed/held invoices, grace periods, credit adjustments, legacy records, and due-date edits that change what counts as an opportunity. The billing integration owner must map actual states, safe discovery of due records, source freshness, and how dispatch authorization orders against committed billing and access changes. That ordering must be demonstrated at the actual boundary. A stale list or notification alone cannot authorize dispatch. Affected live eligibility and race verification remain unready.

### R-4 Prevent duplicate reminder submissions

**Basis and delta:** The 2026-11-21 request, “Set up automatic reminders for unpaid invoices,” implies repeated background execution without repeated copies for one opportunity. Add dispatch coordination while preserving legitimate separate occurrences under the approved cadence. Prior deduplication and dispatch behavior are unknown. Sources are the incoming request and Behavior model §§ States and transitions, Incoming events, connections, and state agreement. Implementation and checks are pending.

| Acceptance line | Positive | Failure | Boundary | Surface and confirmation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A — Coordinate one occurrence | For one billing scope, invoice and logical scheduled occurrence, concurrent or repeated processing shares one reminder outcome (F-2, F-3, T-2 through T-5). | A stale processor or duplicate callback cannot create another authorized submission after recorded acceptance. Uncertain submissions follow R-5. | Different authorized occurrences may each send even when the invoice is unchanged. An occurrence in one scope cannot suppress another scope's work. | Overlap processing of the same occurrence, replay it after acceptance, and compare actual outbound counts per scoped occurrence with a second authorized occurrence. **State and lifecycle:** Scope, invoice ID and logical occurrence identify one reminder. The reminder processor owns its durable outcome for as long as an old occurrence may be retried or replayed. A dispatch attempt/correlation reference identifies a submission attempt. Source revision identifies the eligibility snapshot and does not itself create a new occurrence. Concurrent work can advance one outcome only, and late evidence must be associated with the authorized attempt. Recovery must preserve this identity across interruption. Deletion of outcome data requires ending replay of the affected occurrence first.

**Rules left open:** Billing product and integration owners must define stable occurrence identity across rescheduling, policy and invoice edits, and verify the deployed coordination and replay bounds. The delivery integration owner must verify whether the selected boundary supports safe correlation, outcome lookup or deduplication during an interrupted handoff. C-7's same-occurrence contract is independent of a particular coordination technology.

### R-5 Recover and expose truthful reminder outcomes

**Basis and delta:** The 2026-11-21 request, “Set up automatic reminders for unpaid invoices,” implies unattended continuation through recoverable interruption and truthful evidence of what happened. Add scoped reminder outcomes and recovery, preserving the source invoice and any applicable operator controls. No existing diagnostics, processor or provider recovery capability has been inspected. Sources are the incoming request and Service discovery §§ Asynchronous cooperation, Observability coverage. Implementation and checks are pending.

| Acceptance line | Positive | Failure | Boundary | Surface and confirmation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A — Recover interrupted work | Resume eligible unfinished work after interruption. Retry a definitively unaccepted transient attempt within the approved retry policy after rechecking R-1 through R-3 (F-3, T-4, T-5). | Definitive permanent rejection, retry exhaustion or policy expiry stops retries with a reason. An uncertain handoff is held for reconciliation, with no blind resubmission. | Recovery before any submission can make the first attempt. Recovery after possible submission requires authoritative resolution or safe replay demonstrated at the chosen boundary. | Inject interruption before submission, a definitive transient rejection, and loss of the acceptance receipt after submission. Compare attempts, current eligibility, and durable outcomes across restart. C-8. |
| B — Report outcomes accurately and safely | Authorized billing operations can retrieve a scoped outcome and reason for suppression, acceptance, reported delivery/failure, retry or unresolved handoff. Diagnostics permit correlation without message content. | If the diagnostic sink is unavailable, preserve durable outcomes and business errors. Duplicate or stale reports cannot overwrite stronger authoritative evidence or reopen sending. Reject reads from other scopes. | Channel acceptance alone is shown as accepted. Unsupported delivery milestones remain unknown. A post-acceptance bounce is recorded against that attempt with no automatic resend in the proposed policy. | Inspect state read-back, diagnostic events and actual attempts through duplicate/out-of-order reports, sink interruption, a bounce and an unauthorized read. C-9. |

**State and lifecycle:** The processor owns durable occurrence outcomes and recovery state outside any user session. The delivery authority owns submission/acceptance facts and any reported downstream outcome. Business state survives worker restart and diagnostic sink loss. Only authorized scoped billing operations and the processor can see the needed outcome data. Retain minimal scoped identifiers, timestamps, safe outcome/reason and necessary correlation references. Raw addresses, invoice contents, message bodies, payment links and credentials stay out of diagnostics. End recovery on confirmed terminal disposition, expiry, or permanent loss of eligibility for that occurrence. Purge operational copies under approved retention and account-removal rules while preserving R-4's replay invariant.

**Rules left open:** Billing operations owns retry limits/backoff, expiry, reconciliation ownership, incident handling and retention decisions. Delivery integration must confirm available authoritative delivery states, authentication and correlation, loss recovery, and permitted read-back. Resolve retry/expiry and retention before live recovery. An unresolved handoff without authoritative resolution stays visible for operations and cannot authorize a resend.

## Service boundary contracts

These are logical boundaries. Integration owners must identify the actual interfaces, supported operation identities and deployment permissions before treating checks as runnable.

| Boundary | Authority, freshness and incremental state | Completion, recovery and owned coverage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Processor ↔ billing and policy authority | Read under current trusted billing scope. Obtain current status, balance, due date, policy and destination at dispatch authorization. Revocation applies even without an invoice edit. Candidate discovery may be incremental only if missed, repeated and out-of-order changes recover without losing eligible records or permitting stale sends. Verify restart and revocation with unchanged invoice data. | A candidate lookup completes as a read and grants no send authority. On an unavailable or ambiguous response defer or suppress as specified. Billing owns source changes. Processor owns suppression/deferral and authorization outcomes. Async billing mutation completion is n/a: reminders perform no billing writes. |
| Processor ↔ outbound channel | Submit using an authorized sender and scoped destination established by T-2. Reject unauthenticated or cross-scope reports. Apply partial outcome updates only to the matching attempt, with stale or duplicate evidence unable to regress state. | Accepted submission is distinct from receipt. Reconcile lost acknowledgment with authoritative channel evidence or verified safe replay. Channel owns provider disposition facts. Processor owns attempts, local acceptance recording, uncertainty, retries and terminal outcomes. Verify actual receipt independently. |
| Billing operations ↔ policy and outcomes | Enforce current scope and permissions on changes and read-back, including after revocation. Concurrent policy edits are conditional on the version being changed. Incremental operation feeds are n/a: scoped read-back of authoritative state suffices. | Confirm an applied policy only after durable commit. Read-back distinguishes applied policy from async send outcomes. Billing operations authority owns policy change records. Processor owns reminder outcomes. Verify visible reasons and revoked access. |

Reuse existing owned diagnostic coverage where verified. The billing operations owner must confirm destinations, authorized retrieval, required event coverage and purge, including a retrieval check with redacted records, before operational sign-off. A missing observation leaves coverage unknown.

- **C-1/C-4 · Pay between selection and authorization.** Fixture invoice `INV-101` is open, due 2026-11-20, balance USD 100.00, with opportunity `occ-1` due 2026-11-21 at 09:00 UTC. At 08:59:59 UTC T-1 cannot select a due occurrence. At 09:00:00 UTC T-1 may select it. Pause before T-2. An authorized billing fixture writer commits payment in the billing authority, setting the balance to USD 0.00 and status to paid. This is a source action, permitted by the fixture's billing authority, outside the reminder workflow. T-2 reads the paid record and suppresses submission. Recomputed result at the dispatch observer is zero attempts, and scoped outcome read-back reports payment/ineligibility. Reset to the original fixture and repeat with a partial payment of USD 40.00, leaving USD 60.00 and an open invoice. The unchanged authorized destination allows T-2 to authorize and T-3 to submit. Recomputed result is one submission to `billing@example.test` for `INV-101`, showing USD 60.00, the original due date and the approved instruction. A stale USD 100.00 notice fails the check.
