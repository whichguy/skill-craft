# Invoice Reminders

## Scope and source

**Request** — “Set up automatic reminders for unpaid invoices.” User instruction, 2026-11-21, conversation § The incoming request.

The billing organization sends reminders to its designated billing contact. The invoice authority supplies current invoice and contact facts. An authorized background process evaluates and dispatches reminders while users are signed out. Authorized billing operations can inspect outcomes. These are roles. Existing systems may perform several roles.

The request establishes automatic reminders for unpaid invoices. Overdue eligibility, email and one reminder per invoice are reversible product defaults. As stated in the conversation § Where you are, the standalone stage has no earlier spec, repository, discovery or research output, selected baseline, reference system or selected runtime. There is no preserved clause or observed product behavior to reconcile. Implementation and product verification are pending.

## Requirements

### R-1 — Identify unpaid invoices automatically

**Basis and delta.** [Request](#scope-and-source), 2026-11-21, “automatic reminders for unpaid invoices.” Adds unattended evaluation of current invoice facts. The source limits above apply. Implementation and A1–A3 checks are pending.

| Acceptance line | Positive expectation | Failure expectation | Boundary expectation | Consumer surface and confirmation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A1 — Unattended evaluation | Once activated, evaluate unattended, including invoices already overdue, **default**, and those that cross the threshold later. | After a missed evaluation or interruption, the next successful evaluation includes invoices still eligible. | Before activation or when there are no eligible invoices, send zero reminders. Missed evaluations create no extra reminders. | In an isolated target enable under authorized scope, sign out, control time and interrupt processing. Compare outbound dispatch counts and identities. C1. |
| A2 — Eligibility | An issued, collectible invoice with a positive outstanding balance is eligible starting at the beginning of the day after its due date, **default**. Use the authoritative invoice billing time zone, then the organization's billing time zone, then UTC if both are absent, **default**. A collectible partially paid invoice with positive balance qualifies. | Withhold when the due date is missing, date or zone data are invalid, or collectibility or balance is unknown or conflicting. Give authorized operations a reason. Later valid facts can restore eligibility. | The invoice is ineligible throughout the due date. Draft, void, uncollectible and fully paid invoices and invoices with zero or credit balance are ineligible. | Compare controlled invoice facts and the sender's observed calls before and at the threshold and across status or balance changes. C1–C2. |
| A3 — Current authorization | Before authorizing each dispatch, use the current scoped grant, enablement, invoice, balance, due date and recipient. Decision-changing updates effective before authorization supersede candidate selection. | Withhold when current facts or authority cannot be established. Unauthorized principals cannot invoke scoped runs, read protected outcomes or authorize sends. | A payment effective before dispatch authorization suppresses the reminder if it clears the balance. A change effective after authorization cannot recall an in-flight message and applies to later authorizations. | Control ordering at the invoice-to-dispatch boundary. Compare current revisions, operations read-back and actual sender calls on each side of authorization. C2–C3 and C5. |

**Domain and state.** The invoice authority owns invoice, balance, due date, collectibility and recipient facts and the rules for issuance, payments, noncollectible status and reopening. The process reads under the organization's service authority by organization and stable invoice identity. Invoice, payment, contact and unrelated configuration facts are unchanged by success, withholding and interruption. The billing organization owns the enabled scope in its billing authority. Only the existing authorized billing role can activate or deactivate it. The worker reads current enablement at each authorization. Deactivation ends evaluation for that scope and supersedes stale eligibility. Scope removal ends reminder processing and follows the history lifecycle in R-3. Candidate data may be held privately for the evaluation and is discarded afterward. It cannot authorize dispatch. Current facts and permissions must govern authorization. A page position or time cursor cannot permanently skip eligible invoices. Invoice creation, payment recording and collections escalation are outside this change.

**Open prerequisite.** The billing owner identifies the authorized invoice target and runtime identity. The planning owner maps actual status, balance and date semantics and verifies a supported scoped read and ordering against dispatch authorization before dependent integration. Until that boundary is established, withhold real sends. Eligibility and race modeling can proceed with fixtures.

### R-2 — Send the reminder to the billing contact

**Basis and delta.** [Request](#scope-and-source), 2026-11-21, “reminders for unpaid invoices.” Adds a recipient-facing notification. The source limits above apply. Implementation and B1–B2 checks are pending.

| Acceptance line | Positive expectation | Failure expectation | Boundary expectation | Consumer surface and confirmation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| B1 — Recipient and message | Send a plain-text email, **default**, from the authorized billing sender to the current designated billing address. Identify the billing organization, invoice reference and due date and ask the recipient to pay the outstanding invoice. | Withhold when the sender or destination is missing, invalid, ambiguous, unauthorized or suppressed by the applicable sending policy. Expose the reason to authorized operations. A corrected route can qualify if the invoice is still eligible and no prior send was accepted. | Use the address effective at dispatch authorization. A change effective earlier invalidates the old address. Each message has one recipient. | Compare sender, envelope, content and controlled inboxes with current invoice authority data. C3. |
| B2 — Tenant and data isolation | Scope the message and outbound data to the authorized invoice, organization and recipient. Send only the address and required message fields, plus scoped correlation for the outcome. | Discard untrusted destination overrides and evaluate only the authoritative route. Deny cross-organization access and outward effects. | Include no payment credentials, invoice attachments, address list, payment history or other customers' data. | Exercise two organizations with colliding public invoice references. Inspect sender calls, the actual delivered message, old and unrelated inboxes and scoped operations reads. C3 and C5. |

**Domain and state.** The invoice authority owns the recipient mapping and edit permissions. Current sender permission and applicable suppression govern release. The sending boundary sees the minimum envelope and content for this purpose. Addresses and bodies are transient in reminder processing through handoff. Keep no reminder-owned copy in logs or delivery history. External retention and removal follow the approved sending and mailbox policies. Contact enrollment, new accounts, customer sharing, preference management and payment processing are outside scope. The recipient interaction is a static message with a clear sender, subject and readable plain-text body. There is no new page, screen, report or terminal UI.

**Open prerequisite.** The billing owner identifies an authorized sender and sending route and supplies access to an isolated recipient inbox. Planning confirms sender and recipient authority, suppression and applicable recipient data policy on that route before real dispatch and inbox verification. Content review can proceed independently.

### R-3 — Prevent duplicate dispatch and recover from interruption

**Basis and delta.** [Request](#scope-and-source), 2026-11-21, “automatic reminders.” Adds durable scoped dispatch outcomes so unattended processing can recover without replaying accepted reminders. The source limits above apply. Implementation and D1–D3 checks are pending.

| Acceptance line | Positive expectation | Failure expectation | Boundary expectation | Consumer surface and confirmation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D1 — One reminder milestone | Use one automatic reminder milestone per organization and stable invoice identity, **default**. Before handoff, establish durable ownership of the intent so overlapping evaluations cannot independently send it. Confirmed sender acceptance completes the milestone. | A crash or replay recovers the same intent. An accepted intent causes zero further sends. | After the first acceptance, suppress further sends even after a due date or recipient edit. Different stable invoice identities have independent intents, including public reference collisions. | Overlap evaluations, restart and replay across invoice edits. Observe actual sending calls and durable outcomes by scoped identity. C3–C4. |
| D2 — Definite and unknown outcomes | After authoritative recoverable nonacceptance, retry on a later eligible processing opportunity when the sending route permits, under the same intent and after A3. A permanent rejection stays visible until relevant input or authority changes. | A timeout, lost acknowledgment or crash after possible handoff leaves an unknown outcome. Suspend further sends and reconcile against authoritative evidence for the same intent. If conclusive evidence is unavailable, surface the unknown outcome for billing operations. | Acceptance found later completes the milestone. Proven nonacceptance permits retry only while eligible. Stale or repeated results cannot overwrite acceptance or reset a newer attempt. | Interrupt around handoff and inject controlled outcomes. Observe independent sender calls and acceptance counts alongside durable outcome and recovery. C4. |
| D3 — Truthful outcomes | Authorized billing operations can distinguish withheld, pending, sender accepted, definitive failure and unknown using scoped identity, time and reason where applicable. Owned events cover withholding, handoff acceptance, failure, unknown outcome, retry and reconciliation. | Diagnostic sink failure cannot change the business outcome or erase the durable state used for recovery. Revoked access denies subsequent reads even when status is unchanged. | Sender acceptance means the sending authority accepted responsibility. Report inbox receipt only with receipt evidence. Acceptance can precede receipt. | Compare status, safe event fields and a controlled sender outcome and inbox. Recheck access after revocation and fault the diagnostic sink. C4–C5. |

**Domain and state.** The reminder authority owns durable dispatch state keyed by organization and stable invoice identity. The runtime can write within the current authorized scope. Authorized operations can read within their current scope. Keep the intent, current outcome, needed attempt correlation and minimal times and reasons. Keep secrets, raw sender payloads, addresses and message bodies out of durable reminder records and diagnostics. The intent starts unaccepted, may become pending or unknown, and completes at sender acceptance. Definitive nonacceptance can allow renewed processing. A stale worker cannot overwrite a newer attempt or settled outcome. Recovery resumes pending work only with evidence handoff was never attempted, or by resolving an uncertain outcome under D2. Ineligibility stops later authorization. Reconcile outstanding attempts for ineligible invoices without resending. Retain the minimal suppression fact while the same invoice identity remains eligible for consideration or replay. Remove reminder-owned state when the identity is permanently removed from consideration and reconciliation closes. Expire diagnostic events under the approved operational retention policy. Recovery leaves the invoice intact. Recurring dunning, manual resend and collection actions are outside scope.

**Open prerequisite.** Planning confirms a runtime capability that persists scoped outcomes across restarts and coordinates overlapping attempts. Verify the selected sender's acknowledgment meanings, failure classification and correlated recovery before live use. If sender reconciliation is unavailable, keep ambiguous work visible and suspended until conclusive evidence is available. The billing owner identifies the authorized operations reader, diagnostic destination and retention and purge policies before production use. This gates dependent recovery, access and retention checks.

## Behavior and verification model

Flow and transition labels locate checks within the requirements.

| Flow | Permitted transitions and outcomes |
| --- | --- |
| F-1, R-1 | An authorized background evaluation under enabled scope selects candidates (T-1, A1). Current scoped facts at dispatch authorization qualify, withhold or suppress each candidate (T-2, A2–A3). |
| F-2, R-2 and R-3 | An eligible candidate with an authorized sender and contact obtains one durable dispatch intent and owner (T-3, B1–B2 and D1). The owner hands off the message and retains the outcome (T-4, D2–D3). Ineligible candidates, unusable routes and competing owners have no outbound effect. |
| F-3, R-1 and R-3 | Restart, a later evaluation or correlated authoritative evidence resumes or resolves outstanding work (T-5, A3 and D1–D2). Scoped status read-back and lifecycle cleanup follow D3 and R-3 (T-6). |

## Submission

### summary

The specification defines unattended selection of unpaid invoices, delivery to the authorized billing contact and recovery without duplicate dispatch. The manual model walks check the ordering and recompute expected outcomes. Product verification remains unverified.

| Requirement | Default and basis | Alternatives and revalidation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R-1 | Start eligibility the day after the due date so the full due date is available for payment. Use the invoice or organization billing zone and fall back to UTC for a deterministic date boundary. Include invoices still overdue at activation or after missed evaluations, since they meet the same eligibility rule. | Alternatives include advance notices, a grace period, requiring an explicit zone or future-only activation. Revalidate when the target invoice date semantics and approved billing, cutoff or rollout policies are known. |
| R-2 | Use plain-text email to the designated billing address. It can reach the contact without an open application. Use the minimum content that identifies the invoice and states the action. | Alternatives include an established approved in-app or other delivery route, or richer copy. Revalidate against the supported channel, authorized sender and billing contact policy at target selection. |
| R-3 | Use one reminder milestone per stable invoice identity. It fulfills the request while limiting unsolicited contact. Retry only after definitive recoverable nonacceptance and route permission so an ambiguous handoff cannot trigger a duplicate. | Alternatives include recurring milestones under an approved dunning policy or ending processing at first failure. Revalidate against approved cadence and the chosen sender's acknowledgment, retry and reconciliation contract. |

**Open and affected readiness.** No material requirement conflict is established. Target bindings, authority, quotas and retention evidence remain open under the affected requirements. The billing owner supplies authorized scope, sender, operations access and applicable policies before dependent setup or release. Planning and integration own verification of current authority, ordering and runtime capabilities before integration, and C1–C5 at system-test. Product acceptance owns confirmation of the unattended and recipient outcomes. Checks that only the authorized billing owner can perform remain open and unverified with that owner at the phase they gate, or at handoff when they gate no earlier effect. Independent behavior and fixture work can proceed. If later discoveries change a contract or oracle, record the evidence and reassess affected clauses and cases through ShipLoop's spec correction route. Use add-work for due acceptance defects. Unavailable access does not establish existing behavior.
