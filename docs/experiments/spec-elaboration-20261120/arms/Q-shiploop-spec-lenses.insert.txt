   Before you write them, think through what the request leaves unsaid, in one pass, along each of these lines.
   What a line turns up becomes a requirement, or an acceptance line under the R-<n> it bounds, inside the approved
   scope: add no feature the request's intent does not cover.
   - Everyone who meets this and every way they meet it: the ordinary use; the same use at its edges (the first
     time, the repeated time, the empty case, the largest case); its unhappy paths (wrong input, a refused action,
     a limit reached, something it depends on failing); and the people who run it (turning it on and knowing it
     worked, configuring it, turning it off for everyone or for one case, seeing what went wrong, carrying on with
     the data that exists, undoing a mistake, going back to the version before).
   - What each sentence of the request, and then each requirement you have written, takes for granted: that the
     thing it names exists, is found somehow, may be seen by this person, is one and not none or many. Follow each
     answer down until it is a fact about the world and no longer about this product.
   - Where the parts meet: each use, piece of state, outside system and existing feature against the others: two
     at once, one during the other, one undone while the other runs, one arriving late, one arriving twice.
   - The runtime's own rules that the behaviour cannot escape: what it forbids, caps, times out, will not do while
     the person waits, and requires before this runs at all or ships beyond a development environment. State the
     behaviour each rule forces, with its number; item 10 asks for its source.
   - What exists today and must still hold, and what must never be true however this is built: someone seeing what
     they may not, a thing done twice that should happen once, data lost on the way, a rule enforced on one path
     and not another.
   A requirement that names a dimension (the states that stop something, the fields a record holds, the controls
   an operator has, the conditions that refuse an action) lists its members, or says which it leaves out and why.
   One that names a limit gives the limit's value, or records it as unresolved (item 6).
