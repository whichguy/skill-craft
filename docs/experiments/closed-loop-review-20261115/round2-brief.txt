Design choices I made that you may challenge (they are decisions, not accidents):
- The contract's workspace is now the CAMPAIGN folder (a Git repo `init` creates, holding only campaign state), with the runtime's state, the contract and the receipts in the sibling `<campaign>.work`. A change to the SUBJECT between the verify run and the report is outside the fingerprint; the checks catch it as stale on their next run, and the card says to verify and report back to back.
- No readiness contract; readiness is a gate that records its result in the campaign. Every start is gated on the ratified boundary hash, a recorded readiness for that hash that shows the start rule, and every selected case released by the owner.
- `execution.pin` puts the adapter program and graders under the ratified hash; the script cannot prove an adapter reported truthfully and says so.
- A start that fails keeps its step key (so a retry cannot launch a second run) and three identical failures stop the campaign; failed adapter calls charge their wall time to `seconds`; the budget is checked between steps, so one step can overshoot it.
- A step starts from the LIVE build (an outside change is logged), and a stop is recorded even when the adapter can no longer be queried.
